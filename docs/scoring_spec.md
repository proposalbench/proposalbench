# Scoring specification (draft, v0.1)

Status: **draft for the pilot**. It will be frozen at v1.0, before any sealed-split run, and any
later change is logged in `docs/decision-log.md`. `src/proposalbench/scoring/score.py`
implements this document and changes only together with it.

## 1. What is scored

One sample = one proposal generated by one system for one task. The ProposalBench Score of a
sample is

    score = 0                                   if the sample is inadmissible or failed
    score = max(0, Quality − Deductions)        otherwise

Systems are compared on the mean score over tasks and samples, with uncertainty from a two-way
cluster bootstrap over calls and dossiers (section 6).

## 2. Admissibility (deterministic)

A sample is inadmissible when any of these hold:

| Check | Rule |
|---|---|
| Failed output | Fewer than 300 words of body text, or the system reported a model failure. |
| Language | The detected language differs from the call's language with confidence ≥ 0.6. |
| Over length, `inadmissible` contracts | Counted words exceed `max_words × (1 + tolerance)`, or a section with its own limit exceeds it. |

For `disregard_excess` contracts (Horizon Europe, ERC, MSCA), text beyond the limit is cut before
judging and the cut is reported. The reference list is never cut. Under-length is reported only.

The tolerance is encoded in each call (3% by schema default). The new v2 skeletons use zero
tolerance, with their page-to-word conversions and any invented limits stated explicitly.

## 3. Quality

Quality = 0.4 × Call responsiveness + 0.4 × Substance & plan + 0.2 × Applicant grounding.

Each component is 100 × the mean item score over its scored items. If a component has no scored
items, the weights of the others are renormalised.

**Items.** Each item is answered yes (1), partial (0.5) or no (0) by every judge of the panel, or
"na" (premise does not apply) or "cannot_determine". Item sources:

- *call items*, generated from the task's answer key: one per expected outcome and scope element
  ("addressed through concrete work"), one per exclusion ("stays clear"), one per mandatory
  element ("treated substantively"), and one per trap ("respects the condition");
- *scheme items* from `rubrics/schemes/<scheme>.yaml`;
- *generic items* from `rubrics/generic.yaml`, filtered by the dossier's method profile.

Every item cites pitfall ids from `docs/pitfall_taxonomy.md`.

**Evidence rule.** An item lists the verdicts that must be supported by a quote. If none of a
judge's quotes is found in the proposal (after normalising Markdown, quotation marks, dashes and
whitespace), that judge's verdict on that item is invalid.

**Panel aggregation.** An item's score is the median of the valid yes/partial/no verdicts. It is
scored only if a majority of the panel gave a valid verdict. If a majority says "na", the item is
not applicable. Otherwise the item is "abstained" and excluded. Abstention and invalid-verdict
rates are reported per judge.

**Scope.** Items read either the sections named in the item, or the sections mapped to the
item's roles by the scheme (e.g. role `methods` → section 1.2 in Horizon Europe). If a section
cannot be found, the judge reads the whole proposal. Grounding items and RESUB also receive the
dossier.

**Plan coherence (item PC1, Substance).** Each panel judge extracts a fact sheet (duration,
requested budget and every total-budget mention, work packages with months and person-months,
deliverables, milestones, partners and countries); entries are kept only when a strict majority of
the panel agrees. Deterministic checks then test duration against the call's maximum, activities
after the end (against the call's maximum when no duration is extracted), work packages ending
before they start, undefined work packages (also checked directly in the text), budget against the
call's cap and minimum (only when the budget belongs in the narrative), inconsistent budget
totals, person-month totals against work-package figures, and the consortium against the
eligibility rule. Each check is one PC1 criterion; checks the sheet cannot support are left out.

**Task-specific grounding (`GR-T`, Grounding, P19/P22).** The generic grounding items are met
by nearly every proposal, so each task adds atomic criteria from its dossier's hidden key: one
per intentionally missing fact (`dossier.gaps`: no specific value, commitment or date is stated
as established; a labelled placeholder or a justified estimate is correct), one per intentional
conflict between documents (`dossier.inconsistencies`: flagged, or worded so that neither value
is settled), then one per salient fact carrying a status (planned, pending, requested, under
review: the status is kept). Facts already tested by a trap of the task are left to the trap
item; at most eight criteria. Every criterion is `na` when the proposal does not touch the
point, and the item is `na` when all are. These criteria judge what the proposal states; an
unsupported claim is also an integrity finding, as with traps.

**Resubmissions (`RESUB`, Substance, P24).** Task `type` defaults to `full`; the other values
are `stage1`, `concept_note` and `resubmission`. A resubmission requires a nonempty hidden
`dossier.reviewer_criticisms` key (unique R1, R2, … ids); the prior application, review report and
subsequent developments remain in the visible dossier Markdown. Only resubmission tasks get
RESUB: one atomic yes/no criterion per criticism, requiring either a concrete change to aims,
design or plan, or a reasoned, evidence-based rebuttal. The change need not be labelled as a
response (Horizon Europe evaluates resubmissions afresh and has no response section).
Restatement, a promise to address it, a claimed revision the new narrative does not carry out,
or work claimed as done that the dossier does not show does not count. Judges read the whole
revised proposal and dossier; each yes needs a verifiable proposal quote. The applicable
criterion scores form one item score, so more criticisms do not create more item-level weight.
Task/call/dossier compatibility is checked on loading and by repository validation; `stage1`
and `concept_note` tasks require the matching call `proposal_type`. Ordinary tasks get no
RESUB even if their dossier retains criticism annotations.

**Horizon stage 1 (`horizon_stage1`).** The two-stage RIA outline uses only Excellence (1.1
objectives/ambition, 1.2 methodology) and Impact (2.1 pathways), at 10 pages × 500 = 5,000 words.
The four scheme items assess ambition, methodological logic, the impact pathway and
methodological gender/open-science requirements without demanding stage-2 implementation
sections. Official criterion thresholds are 4/5 each; the overall invitation threshold is set
per budget envelope, normally 8 or 8.5. The scheme maps all roles to its reduced section set.

**Erasmus KA220-HED (`erasmus_ka220`).** The 2026 online application fields become narrative
sections for relevance, partners, implementation, impact, summary and budget. Five scheme items
check transnational added value, cooperation, the discrete lump sum and its allocation,
institutional uptake, and accessible learning/assessment. Official weights are 25/30/20/25,
with 70 overall and category minima 13/15/10/13. The 6,000-word ceiling (12 × 500) is invented;
the blank form's printed page count is not an official narrative limit.

**CERV (`cerv`).** This implementation models CERV-2026-CITIZENS-CIV sub-priority 2.1, with
civil-society-led participatory democracy, not every CERV strand. Five items assess meaningful
citizen involvement, transnational value, event-based lump-sum inputs, safeguards and sustained
use. Relevance/Quality/Impact carry 40/40/20 points, with 25 for Relevance and 70 overall needed.
The selected call's 70-page Part B maximum overrides the generic form: 70 × 500 = 35,000 words.
Its EUR 300,000 upper cap is fictional; the real call has a EUR 75,000 minimum and no maximum.

**ERC PoC (`erc_poc`).** Four items cover derivation from the eligible ERC grant, distinctive
innovation, validation towards use and PI strategic decisions. The current WP2027 uses 0–5
marks: each of three excellence elements needs a score of at least 3 from a majority of experts.
The 10-page/5,000-word total is verified for 2027; 3/6/1-page section allowances and exemption
of the separate risk table are explicitly retained from the accessible 2026 applicant guide
as a benchmark layout convention, not asserted to be a verified 2027 form. The award is a
EUR 150,000 lump sum, normally 18 months, tied to an ongoing ERC main grant or one ending on
or after 1 January 2026.

**NSF CAREER (`nsf_career`).** A fictional solicitation modelled on NSF 22-586 adds research
and education sections inside the 15-page Project Description, alongside mandatory Broader
Impacts and conditional Results from Prior NSF Support. It retains the three atomic NSF
items and adds two for integration/career development and educational assessment. No numeric
NSF merit weights are invented. Summary and Description have independent 600/9,000-word
ceilings, with prior support capped at 3,000 inside Description; the departmental letter is
outside the narrative. PI eligibility and five-year duration follow CAREER.

**NIH R21 A1 (`nih_r21`, resubmission task).** The existing NIH rubric is reused with RESUB;
CALL-ST also requires `introduction`. One Introduction page, one Aims page and six Strategy
pages become separate 600/600/3,600-word ceilings, total 4,800. The Introduction summarises
changes and answers the summary statement; revisions must not be marked in the Strategy.
Only one A1 within 37 months of A0 is permitted. The Simplified Review Framework remains
applicable, without invented numerical factor weights.

**AEI Orientada (`aei_pid`, Spanish).** The new skeleton retains the pilot's seven section ids
and shared AEI rubric, with priority alignment assessed through call items. Verified Orientada
weights are 40/30/20/10, with thresholds 30/20/10 on the first three criteria; indirect costs
are 25% of direct costs. The invented direct-cost ceiling is EUR 99,000 so the Spanish
narrative respects the official English requirement from EUR 100,000. The 20-page memoria
becomes 10,000 words. The pilot data are not changed.

**Fictional foundation (`foundation`, concept-note task).** Six brief sections cover problem,
aims, approach, team, budget outline and expected impact. Four items assess focused fit, an
informative invitation decision, budget/scale and a plausible benefit. All eligibility,
weights (30/20/25/15/10), the 70-point invitation threshold and 3 × 500 = 1,500-word maximum
are invented, with no fidelity claim to a named funder. Two to three pages is guidance;
there is no two-page minimum. Roles map only to these six sections.

The new scheme rubrics supplement the existing generic checklist; official award weights
are call content and do not change ProposalBench's 40/40/20 component weights. Sources,
verification dates, format adaptations and item rationale are recorded in each skeleton and
`.codex-handoff/schemes_v2.md`.

## 4. Integrity deductions

Deductions are fixed points per de-duplicated defect, not rates, so adding harmless content
cannot dilute them.

| Severity | Points | Defects |
|---|---|---|
| Critical | 10 each, uncapped | A claim about the applicant's past or existing situation (publications, grants, positions, awards, results, numbers, partners, resources) that is unsupported or contradicted by the dossier: any judge may propose it (with a verified quote), every judge rules on it, and it is critical only when every valid ruling says critical (a majority finding it unsupported but not unanimously critical makes it minor). |
| Major | 4 each, uncapped | A reference whose authors are materially wrong for a real title (MISATTRIBUTED); a DOI that resolves to a different work; a DOI that does not exist (404 at doi.org and absent from Crossref and OpenAlex); a verified reference whose abstract a majority of the panel finds contradicts the citing sentence or is on a clearly unrelated subject (counted once per reference). |
| Minor | 0.5 each, at most 5 in total | Metadata errors in a real reference (year off by more than one, wrong venue, wrong DOI on a correct reference); a miscited dossier publication; minor embellishment of an applicant fact. |
| None | 0 | NOT_FOUND references; UNPARSEABLE entries; abstentions. |

**NOT_FOUND is never penalised in v0.1.** A reference that no index or web search can find may be
a book, a report, a non-English work or a fabrication. Penalising NOT_FOUND for the indexable
class (journal articles from 2000 on) will be switched on only if its false-positive rate on the
real references of the anchor set is at most 2%. Anchor calibration on 2026-10-31 (11 published
proposals, 598 references, OpenAlex unavailable): 32 of 430 indexable references were not
found (7.4%, 95% CI 5.3-10.3%), so NOT_FOUND stays neutral in v1. The same run found no
MISATTRIBUTED or DOI_UNRESOLVED verdict on the 598 real references (95% CI 0-0.6%).

A wrong DOI on a reference whose title and authors match a real work is classed separately
(`wrong_doi_real_work`) so that its severity can be reviewed independently of fabricated
attributions. Both are major in v0.1.

The leaderboard reports, next to the score, the share of samples with at least one critical
defect.

**Claim support.** Every citing sentence is paired with each verified reference it cites; pairs
whose reference has an abstract (OpenAlex, Semantic Scholar or the resolver record) are judged by
the panel as supports, partial, cannot_tell, unrelated or contradicts. Up to 80 pairs are
checked; above that a seeded uniform sample of 80 is drawn and the sampling fraction is reported.
An abstract that does not mention a claim is cannot_tell, which is neutral.

## 5. Diagnostics (reported, not scored)

Style profile (buzzword rate, excess style-word rate, em-dash rate, "not only ... but also",
gerund openers, bullet share, repeated 4-grams), duplicate paragraphs and headings,
meta-commentary, leftover placeholders, counted words versus the limit, reference status counts,
cost and wall time.

## 6. Statistics

- Uncertainty: two-way cluster bootstrap over calls and dossiers (tasks share both).
- Primary comparisons (pre-registered at freeze): Proposia versus the best `direct` baseline and
  the best `agent` baseline, both chosen on the public split. Holm correction across them.
- Inconclusive-result rule: if a 95% interval includes 0, the result is reported as "no
  detectable difference".
- No claims per scheme or per component.
- Rank stability under ±0.1 perturbations of the component weights is reported (Kendall τ).

## 7. Open items before freeze

- Uncited load-bearing claims (coverage) as a reported count, and whether it should deduct.
- Final judge panel, selected by the meta-evaluation.
- Words-per-page conversions per scheme, rendered from each scheme's official format.
