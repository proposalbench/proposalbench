# Datasheet: ProposalBench v1.0

Following the questions of Gebru et al., *Datasheets for Datasets* (2021).

## Motivation

**Purpose.** To compare systems that write research funding proposals from a call and an
applicant's prior research, on requirements that can be checked: responsiveness to the call,
substance and plan coherence, grounding in the applicant's record, and integrity (no invented
applicant facts, no invented or unrelated references).

**Who created it.** Proposia, which also builds one of the evaluated systems (see the disclosure
in the README and [`docs/decision-log.md`](../docs/decision-log.md)).

## Composition

- **Public split:** 60 tasks over 30 calls and 30 applicant dossiers. Each call is paired with a
  close and a stretch dossier, and each dossier with a close and a stretch call.
- **Pilot split:** 3 tasks, used for harness development only.
- **Task types:** 44 full proposals, 8 resubmissions, 4 concept notes and 4 stage-1 proposals.
- **Languages:** 54 English tasks and 6 Spanish (Agencia Estatal de Investigación).
- **Schemes:**

  | Scheme | Tasks |
  |---|---|
  | Horizon Europe RIA | 12 |
  | NIH R21 | 8 |
  | AEI Proyectos de Generación de Conocimiento | 6 |
  | MSCA Postdoctoral Fellowships | 4 |
  | NSF | 4 |
  | NSF CAREER | 4 |
  | ERC Proof of Concept | 4 |
  | Horizon Europe stage 1 | 4 |
  | Erasmus+ KA220 | 4 |
  | CERV | 4 |
  | Private foundations | 4 |
  | ERC Starting Grant | 2 |

- **Length limits:** from 1,500 to 35,000 counted words, adapted from each scheme's page limits.
- **Every task carries a brief.** 99 traps are spread across the tasks: requirements a careful
  writer must notice, such as an exclusion or a fact the brief gets wrong.
- **What a task contains:**
  - Each task is a call (`call.md`, given to systems; `call.yaml`, the hidden answer key) and a
    dossier (applicant files, given; `dossier.yaml`, hidden), plus a brief.
  - `pb tasks export` writes exactly what a system receives.
- **Anchors:** 11 real, published proposals under CC BY ([`data/anchors/`](anchors/)), with
  598 references. They are used only to calibrate the reference checker and the evidence-base
  thresholds. They are never tasks, inputs or outputs.
- **Results of the November 2026 run:**
  - every proposal generated by the 12 published systems, with its `meta.json`;
  - every judgment, reference verdict and score.
  - These are distributed with the release (see Distribution).

**Is anything real?**
- The calls are fictional, adapted from official funder documents: structure, criteria and rules
  are real, while topics, identifiers, budgets and deadlines are invented. Sources and licences
  are in [`ATTRIBUTION.md`](ATTRIBUTION.md) and [`seeds/manifest.yaml`](seeds/manifest.yaml).
- The applicants, institutions and projects in the dossiers are fictional. Their own
  publications use the reserved DOI prefix `10.99999/pb.<dossier>.*` in fictional journals.
- Dossier text cites real literature only where a real work is named as such.
- Every call, dossier and task file carries a canary string (`PROPOSALBENCH-CANARY <uuid>`), so
  that training-data contamination can be detected.

**Personal data.**
- None about real people in tasks or dossiers.
- The anchors are published proposals and carry their authors' names, as published under CC BY.
- **Errata watch:** a few dossiers use grant numbers in real formats and plausible institutional
  e-mail domains. They are invented. Any collision with a real grant or person found after
  release will be listed here and fixed in the next version.

## Collection process

**How the tasks were authored.**
- Calls, dossiers and tasks were drafted by models from three families (Anthropic, Google and
  OpenAI), rotated so that each family authored a share of each kind.
- Scheme rules come from hand-made skeletons ([`tools/authoring/schemes/`](../tools/authoring/schemes/)),
  not from the author models.

**How they were reviewed.**
- Each draft was reviewed by a model of another family against
  [`docs/task_review_checklist.md`](../docs/task_review_checklist.md).
- Each was then reviewed in depth by GPT-6 Astra with web search, checking:
  - the rules against the funders' current documents;
  - name collisions and scientific plausibility;
  - the hidden key and whether each trap can be decided.
- Blocking and major findings were revised by the original author and re-validated.

**Copying check.** A 12-word overlap check against the seed documents guards against copying
(`pb tasks validate --seeds`).

**Where the trail is.** Every draft and review is in [`tools/authoring/`](../tools/authoring/).

**No customer data.** No data from Proposia's users was used anywhere in the benchmark.

## Uses

**Intended uses.**
- Comparing proposal-writing systems under identical inputs.
- Studying failure modes: fabricated applicant facts, citation integrity, call compliance,
  length control.
- Studying LLM-judge behaviour on long documents.

**Not intended.**
- The score does not predict funding, and it has not been calibrated against human review
  panels ([`docs/limitations.md`](../docs/limitations.md)).
- The tasks must not be used to train systems that will then be reported on them. A system
  developed on public tasks must be reported only on tasks it was not developed on.

## Distribution

- **Code:** Apache-2.0.
- **Task data, proposals and judgments:** CC BY 4.0.
- **Where:**
  - the repository holds the tasks and the published numbers ([`release/`](../release/));
  - the full results bundle (proposals, judgments, verdicts) is deposited on Zenodo and mirrored
    as a Hugging Face dataset.

## Maintenance

- **Maintainer:** Proposia, through issues on the repository.
- **Changes:** changes to tasks or scoring are logged in
  [`docs/decision-log.md`](../docs/decision-log.md) and released as new versions. Published
  numbers are never edited in place.
