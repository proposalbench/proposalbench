# PB2-FOUNDATION-01: Recovering weak signals from incomplete observations

> This is a wholly fictional private research foundation concept-note call for ProposalBench. It makes no fidelity claim to any named funder. Every eligibility rule, award criterion, financial condition and length rule below is invented.

| | |
|---|---|
| Maximum award | EUR 100,000 |
| Duration | 12 to 24 months |

## Purpose and eligibility

Invite a concise research idea that merits consideration for a later full proposal. Applicants may be individuals or teams at any career stage, including doctoral researchers with named supervision, at a nonprofit research or higher-education host in any country. No international consortium is required. Request at least EUR 25,000 total including indirect costs, within the ceiling and duration in the table above. A concept-note invitation is not a grant award. The fictional deadline is 15 September 2027. Concept notes are welcome across research disciplines, including theoretical and qualitative projects; neither preliminary data nor a commercialisation route is required.

## Expected outcomes

Projects should help researchers and communities responsible for scientific or cultural records judge what incomplete observations can support. The intended advance is a defensible basis for interpreting weak signals and deciding whether further investigation is warranted.

- Researchers can better distinguish weak signals from noise and artefacts introduced by missing observations, with clearer grounds for accepting or rejecting an interpretation.
- Users of incomplete scientific or cultural records understand which conclusions remain credible under uncertainty and which require additional evidence.
- Teams and prospective research partners can make better-supported decisions about further observation, methodological development or discontinuing an unpromising approach.

## Scope

Incomplete observations can obscure a signal or create an apparent pattern through selective recording, uneven coverage or loss. Proposals should investigate a bounded recovery problem in which the distinction matters for scientific understanding or cultural interpretation.

Astronomy and cultural acoustics are illustrative domains, not required fields or a required pairing. Proposals may develop a method within one domain or examine its transfer between domains. Initial feasibility may be established through theoretical reasoning, documented source characteristics, simulation or existing evidence; new preliminary data are not required.

- Characterise the observation process and the mechanism producing missingness, identifying how each could obscure the signal or generate a misleading pattern.
- Develop or adapt a recovery method for a defined signal and source of incomplete observations.
- Evaluate the proposed method against a relevant alternative using an appropriate theoretical test, simulation, controlled omission or independent evidence.
- Assess how uncertainty in noise, missingness and modelling assumptions changes the interpretation of recovered signals.
- Identify the additional evidence or methodological change that would most usefully inform a subsequent research decision.

## Exclusions

- Routine service delivery without a research question is outside this fictional call.
- Purchase of equipment costing more than EUR 20,000 per item is excluded from this fictional call.
- Expenditure unrelated to the proposed inquiry is outside this fictional call.
- Methods evaluated only on complete observations, without investigating the consequences of missing evidence.
- Restoration undertaken solely for aesthetic enhancement or presentation quality, without evaluating whether recovered features are supported by the observations.
- Reconstruction that treats inferred cultural content or scientific measurements as authenticated observations without retaining their inferred status and uncertainty.

## Specific conditions and mandatory elements

- For each aim, identify the evidence or argument that will be used to assess it.
- Provide a budget outline linking the main cost categories of the requested foundation grant to the proposed activities.
- Specify the evidence-based criterion for deciding, at the end of the funded project, whether to continue, redirect or stop the research.
- Assign the proposed research tasks to team members with relevant capabilities.
- State a falsifiable central question and identify a result or observation that would count against the proposed explanation or method.
- Explain initial feasibility using available evidence or reasoned methodological grounds, and distinguish demonstrated capability from assumptions still to be tested.
- Identify the principal uncertainty, how it will be assessed, and any circumstances in which the signal would remain indistinguishable from noise or missingness artefacts.
- Provide an indicative sequence of activities and decision points, showing when feasibility and interpretation will be assessed.
- For each data source, archive, instrument or collaboration on which the project depends, state whether access is confirmed, requested or prospective.
- For a doctoral lead, explain the responsible faculty sponsor's oversight role at the established host and state whether that responsibility has been agreed; the named supervisor may fulfil this role.

## Required narrative

### Problem
Identify the bounded knowledge gap, why it matters, and what existing work leaves unresolved.

### Aims
State a focused question or hypothesis and a small number of assessable aims.

### Approach
Describe the main evidence, sources or experiments, analysis or argument, sequence, success/decision criteria and principal uncertainty. Keep detail proportionate to a concept note.

### Team
Explain who will do the work, the relevant capability, host and supervision where needed, and important gaps or dependencies.

### Budget outline
State the total request, duration, broad cost categories and how they serve activities. Include indirect costs within the ceiling. The requested funding is the grant sought from this foundation for the project described in the concept note. Separately identify additional contributions from other sources, distinguishing committed contributions from prospective contributions that are not yet secured; do not add prospective contributions to the foundation request.

### Expected impact
Identify the intended users or beneficiaries, the route from findings to possible benefit, and the research direction that the project’s findings could inform. Scientific advances can be the primary impact for theoretical work.

## Award criteria

Invented criteria: importance and foundation-call fit of the problem (30 points), value and clarity of the aims (20), credibility and focus of the approach (25), team and resource adequacy (15), and plausible expected impact and next step (10). The fictional invitation threshold is 70/100 with no subcriterion thresholds. These invented weights do not represent any named foundation.

## Length contract

Aim for two to three pages; two is guidance, not a minimum. The maximum is 3 notional pages at 500 words/page, or 1,500 words, with no tolerance. Count tables and captions; exempt the title and reference list. Exceeding the maximum makes this fictional concept note inadmissible. All limits and the conversion are invented benchmark conventions.
