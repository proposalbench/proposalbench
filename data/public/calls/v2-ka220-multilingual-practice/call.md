# Inclusive multilingual teaching and assessment in practical higher-education courses — PB2-KA220-HED-01

> Fictional ProposalBench KA220-HED partnership topic, adapted from the Erasmus+ Programme Guide 2026 and the 2026 application form. Theme, deadline and narrative word contract are invented.

## Purpose and eligibility

Support transnational cooperation that improves higher-education practices and institutional capacity. Choose one of the three fixed lump sums and 12–36 months. At least three organisations from three EU Member States or associated countries are required. Public and private organisations active in education or related sectors may participate; the coordinator must be based in an EU Member State or associated country and legally established for at least two years at the deadline. Participating HEIs in programme countries require ECHE. Organisations from eligible non-associated regions 1–3 (excluding Belarus) can be additional partners only when they bring essential added value, cannot coordinate, and do not satisfy the minimum. Associated partners receive no grant and do not substitute for the minimum beneficiaries. A consortium submits once to one agency per deadline. Apply through the coordinator's National Agency in this National Agency HED variant. The fictional deadline is 15 September 2027.

## Expected outcomes

Projects should change how participating institutions teach and assess the practical parts of their degree programmes for learners whose languages differ from the language of instruction. The changes should be written into curricula and supported by trained staff. Proposals should contribute to all of the following outcomes.

- Learners whose first or strongest language differs from the language of instruction take full part in practical teaching and are assessed on the competences the courses target. This includes international and migrant-background students, sign-language users and speakers of regional or minority languages.
- Teaching and technical staff at the participating higher-education institutions, and practice supervisors at organisations hosting pilot placements, use tested multilingual approaches in practical teaching and assessment.
- Participating higher-education institutions have written the approaches into approved module descriptors, programme specifications or assessment regulations, so that the approaches continue without project funding.
- Participating higher-education institutions have evaluation evidence on which multilingual approaches improve learner outcomes in practical courses, and for which learner groups.
- Participating higher-education institutions have revised their language, inclusion or teaching policies in light of the project's evaluation evidence.
- Higher-education institutions outside the partnership can adapt the teaching materials, assessment instruments and staff development resources to their own languages and disciplines.

## Scope

Practical components of degree programmes depend heavily on spoken instructions, safety briefings, immediate feedback and the working language of a profession. These include laboratory sessions, studio and rehearsal work, clinical and social-work placements, school practice and fieldwork. Learners who are still developing the language of instruction, who use a sign language, or who study in a regional or minority language setting often lose time, marks or placement opportunities for reasons unrelated to the competence being taught. Institutions commonly respond with separate language courses, which leave the practical teaching and its assessment unchanged.

Proposals should develop multilingual approaches to practical teaching and assessment together with the staff and learners concerned. They should pilot the approaches in credit-bearing courses, assess their effect on learner outcomes, train the staff who will keep using them and secure their adoption in approved curricula. Proposals may come from linguistics and language education, from the arts or from professional education such as health care, social work, teacher education and engineering. Research is supported where it improves teaching in the partnership, not as an end in itself. Besides higher-education institutions, partnerships may include practice organisations that host pilot placements, such as hospitals, schools or cultural institutions, and organisations that contribute other expertise, such as migrant-support associations or associations of sign-language users. Requirements on curriculum adoption, staff-development recognition and policy decisions apply to the participating higher-education institutions. Other organisations contribute according to the roles stated in the proposal. Faculties or departments of one higher-education institution count as a single organisation towards the minimum partnership.

- Map the language-related barriers learners meet in the practical courses of each participating higher-education institution, including placements hosted by partner practice organisations, consulting learners, teaching staff, practice supervisors and student support services.
- Co-design multilingual teaching and assessment approaches for practical courses with teaching staff and target-group learners, such as translanguaging tasks, multilingual glossaries, sign-language interpreting arrangements or plain-language briefings.
- Pilot the approaches in credit-bearing practical courses at each participating higher-education institution for at least one full teaching period, delivered by the staff who normally teach or supervise those courses.
- Assess learner outcomes in the pilot courses against their intended learning outcomes, using defined instruments and, where feasible, a comparison with earlier cohorts or parallel groups.
- Run staff development on multilingual practical teaching and assessment for teaching and technical staff of the participating higher-education institutions, with participation recognised in each institution's staff development or teaching-qualification framework.
- Where partner practice organisations host pilot placements, train the practice supervisors who teach and assess learners there in the approaches used in those placements.
- Revise module descriptors, assessment regulations or programme specifications through each participating higher-education institution's approval procedures, so that the tested approaches remain in the curriculum after the pilots.
- Where the project includes applied research, such as analysis of classroom or studio interaction, learner language corpora or practice-based enquiry, tie each research activity to a named change in teaching or assessment.
- Involve learners from the target groups in designing, piloting and evaluating the approaches, with arrangements that let them contribute in languages they use confidently.
- Prepare teaching materials, assessment instruments and staff development resources for reuse by other institutions, and test them with at least one higher-education institution outside the partnership.
- Present evaluation findings to the bodies responsible for language, inclusion or teaching policy at each participating higher-education institution, and record the decisions those bodies take.

## Exclusions

- Subcontracting cannot cover core activities on which the action objectives directly depend.
- Stand-alone benchmarks, test suites or leaderboards for large language models, machine translation or speech recognition that are not used to change teaching or assessment in the participating institutions.
- Research whose only results are publications or datasets, without piloted changes to teaching, assessment or curricula at the participating institutions.
- General language-proficiency or preparatory language courses taught separately from the practical components of degree programmes.
- Individual mobility of students or staff for study, traineeships or teaching assignments as a main activity, which is funded under Key Action 1 (KA131-HED).
- Development of proprietary software as a main project result.
- Purchase of interpreting or captioning equipment as a main project result.

## Specific conditions and mandatory elements

- State the selected lump sum: EUR 120,000, EUR 250,000 or EUR 400,000.
- Provide the allocation of the selected lump sum by work package and partner.
- Describe measures that make the planned activities accessible to the target participants.
- Describe environmental sustainability measures for the planned activities.
- Explain how digital tools support cooperation between the participating organisations.
- Define qualitative and quantitative indicators for assessing the work-package objectives.
- Explain how partners will sustain the project results after funding ends.
- Where applicable, specify open licences for the materials produced, without disproportionate restrictions on reuse.
- For each participating organisation, state its legal name, country, legal status and role (coordinator, partner or associated partner), and give the ECHE status of each higher-education institution.
- For each participating higher-education institution, specify the practical courses in which the approaches will be piloted, their language or languages of instruction and the main languages used by the learners they serve.
- Describe the instruments for assessing learner outcomes in the pilot courses, who applies them, and the comparison against which change will be judged.
- For each participating higher-education institution, name the modules or programmes where the approaches will be adopted and the internal approval each change requires.
- For each participating organisation that is not a higher-education institution, state whether it hosts pilot placements or contributes other expertise, and the work-package tasks it carries out.
- Describe consent and data-protection arrangements for collecting learner data, including recordings of teaching sessions and samples of learner work.

## Required narrative

### Project summary
Summarise objectives, activities and results for a general audience.

### Budget
State the selected lump sum and its allocation per partner and work package. Intermediate grant amounts are not available. Project management may receive at most 20 percent of the grant. Explain co-financing: the estimated total project cost must exceed the requested lump sum. Identify the sources of additional contributions and whether they are committed or prospective.

### Participating organisations
Identify the coordinator and partners, countries, legal status, relevant experience and ECHE status where applicable.

### Relevance of the project
Name at least one horizontal Erasmus+ priority or at least one higher-education priority of the Programme Guide that the project addresses, and connect it to the needs and target groups; explain innovation, complementarity and European added value.

### Partnership and cooperation arrangements
Explain why the partners are needed, task allocation, coordination, decisions and conflict resolution. Justify additional non-associated partners where relevant.

### Impact and dissemination
Specify how achievement will be assessed, how partners will embed results in routine work, sharing with external users, open licences where relevant, and continuation after funding.

### Project design and implementation

#### WP1: Project management
Describe monitoring, risk handling, financial control and the management allocation (at most 20%).

#### Other work packages and activities
For each work package describe objectives, results, activities, dates, participants, responsible partners, qualitative and quantitative indicators, and the rationale for its allocation. Describe inclusion, digital and green practices. For learning activities explain recognition of learning outcomes. The repeated WP2+ fields are consolidated under this narrative heading. Administrative context, declarations and annex uploads are outside this narrative.

## Award criteria

Relevance: 25 points; quality of design and implementation: 30; quality of partnership and cooperation: 20; impact: 25. Funding consideration requires at least 70/100 and minima of 13, 15, 10 and 13 respectively. Ties prioritise relevance, then impact.

## Length contract

The application is an online form, not a fixed-page narrative. For this benchmark only, allow 12 notional pages at 500 words/page = 6,000 words. This invented allowance consolidates the narrative fields; it is not an official page or character limit. Title and references are exempt; tables and captions count. Exceeding the ceiling is inadmissible with zero tolerance (a benchmark enforcement choice).
