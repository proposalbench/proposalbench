# Local heritage and community cultural spaces as routes into local democratic decisions for excluded residents — PB2-CERV-CIV-02

> Fictional ProposalBench action-grant topic modelled on CERV-2026-CITIZENS-CIV, sub-priority 2.1 (civil society organisations). This is not an open EU call; topic details, deadline and upper grant cap are invented.

## Purpose and eligibility

Support citizens' participation in democratic life through civil-society-led participatory and deliberative processes. The eligible-country basis is EU Member States (including OCTs) and countries associated to CERV; a country negotiating association must have an agreement in force before signature. Activities take place in eligible countries. The official minimum grant is EUR 75,000 and the official call sets no maximum; this fictional topic adds the ceiling in the table below. Use at least 12 months, up to the maximum duration in that table, without an extension in this benchmark. The lead may submit only one application across the official call's priorities; the benchmark uses one application. At least two applicants must be beneficiaries from two different CERV-eligible countries; affiliated entities and associated partners do not count towards this minimum. The coordinator must be a private nonprofit legal entity or a public university; co-applicants must be nonprofit public or private legal entities or international organisations. The fictional deadline is 15 September 2027.

## Expected outcomes

Projects are expected to contribute to all of the following outcomes in the localities where they work. The outcomes concern residents who are rarely heard in local decisions, the community cultural organisations that serve them, the authorities that take those decisions, and the policymakers responsible for inclusion and cultural participation. Proposals should explain how each outcome will be observed by the end of the project.

- Residents who are rarely heard in local decisions have shaped proposals on a current local decision. Examples are people on low incomes, migrants, Roma and other minorities, older people living alone, persons with disabilities, and young people not in education or employment.
- Local authorities in the participating localities have received residents' proposals and given a public, reasoned response stating what they will act on, what they will not, and why.
- Libraries, cultural and community centres, community museums and similar spaces in the participating localities use tested methods that link heritage interpretation with deliberation.
- Staff and volunteers of the participating community cultural spaces are able to facilitate deliberation and dialogue with local authorities after the project ends.
- Participating residents know how decisions affecting their area are made and whom to address about them.
- Participating residents are more confident about taking part in local decision-making again.
- Places and histories that matter to excluded residents, including histories of migration, work and minorities, are documented on residents' own terms and are publicly accessible as part of the locality's shared heritage.
- EU and national policymakers working on social inclusion and cultural participation have comparable evidence from different countries on the conditions under which excluded residents influence local decisions.

## Scope

Residents with the fewest resources are the least likely to attend council meetings, respond to consultations or join participatory budgets, and local decisions are often taken without their knowledge or input. Many of the same residents do use libraries, cultural and community centres, community museums and amateur arts groups. Local heritage is a subject on which every resident has something to say: a closed factory, a market, a street name, a migration story or a dialect. It often connects directly to decisions the local authority is about to take, such as reusing a building, redesigning a public space or deciding how a past is commemorated.

Proposals should use these spaces, and participatory interpretation of heritage in the spirit of the Council of Europe's Faro Convention on the Value of Cultural Heritage for Society, as the entry point to a civil-society-led process. The process should end in an actionable dialogue with the competent local authority. Watching a performance, visiting an exhibition or attending a festival does not by itself count as participation under this topic. Proposals must show how residents form proposals, how the authority responds, and how residents learn the outcome. They must also show how the combined experience informs EU policymaking on inclusion and cultural participation. Local authorities need not be beneficiaries, and the consortium follows the eligibility rules set out above.

- Identify, with community organisations in each participating locality, the residents least present in local decision-making and the practical, linguistic and social barriers that keep them away.
- Run participatory heritage interpretation in community cultural spaces, such as oral-history collection, neighbourhood walks or co-curated displays, in which residents define what local heritage means to them.
- Turn the results of heritage interpretation into facilitated deliberation in which residents agree concrete proposals on a local decision still open to influence, such as the use of a public building or space.
- Organise a structured dialogue in which residents present their proposals to the competent local authority, which responds publicly and within an agreed time on what it will take up and why.
- Report back to participating residents on the authority's response and on later follow-up, using the community cultural spaces and formats through which they took part.
- Train staff and volunteers of the community cultural spaces to facilitate deliberation and dialogue with authorities, so that they can repeat the process without project funding.
- Exchange methods and results between the participating localities, including joint activities in which residents from different localities compare their processes and the responses they received.
- Synthesise findings across localities into recommendations on inclusion and cultural participation, and present them to the EU and national policymakers responsible for those policies.
- Publish, with contributors' consent, the residents' heritage interpretations in accessible formats and the localities' languages.
- Publish a practical method guide for community cultural organisations and local authorities, in accessible formats and the localities' languages.

## Exclusions

- Financial support to third parties is not permitted.
- Purely academic research without direct citizen engagement is outside this fictional topic.
- Activities supporting a specific European Citizens’ Initiative are excluded.
- Activities rallying support for a single cause are excluded.
- Festivals, performances, exhibitions or other cultural events in which residents take part only as an audience, without a deliberative step leading to proposals addressed to a public authority.
- Restoration, conservation or digitisation of heritage sites, objects or collections as an aim in itself.
- Heritage tourism promotion, destination marketing or the production of commercial cultural products.
- Projects whose principal participation activities are conferences, seminars or study visits mainly for professionals, officials or established civic representatives rather than excluded residents. Staff training, methodological exchange and presentation of findings to policymakers remain eligible where they serve the resident participation process.
- Activities organised on behalf of, or promoting, a political party, candidate or electoral list.
- Participatory processes on a decision that the addressed authority has already taken or over which it has no competence.

## Specific conditions and mandatory elements

- Describe the participatory activities that constitute each event/work package.
- State the planned number of direct participants for each event, using the counting rules below.
- Identify the eligible countries represented in each event and give their number, using the counting convention below.
- State the share of the total requested EU grant assigned to each work package.
- Describe how the target participants can access the planned participation activities.
- Explain how the participation design addresses gender-related barriers.
- Explain how citizens’ contributions influence the deliberation and subsequent policy engagement.
- Where applicable, describe how personal data collected through the participation activities will be protected.
- Include, for each event/work package, a report on its implementation (the programme's event description sheet) as a deliverable.
- Include a project milestone for asking event attendees to take part in the EU Survey on Justice, Rights and Values once the granting authority provides the link.
- For each participating locality, identify the resident groups targeted and the evidence of their under-representation in local decision-making.
- For each participating locality, name the local decision that residents' proposals will address and the public authority competent to take it.
- Describe the arrangement agreed with each addressed authority on the form and timing of its public response to residents' proposals.
- Explain how passive audiences at performances, exhibitions or screenings are kept separate from direct participants in the planned participant counts.
- Describe how contributors decide whether their heritage contributions are published, attributed or anonymised, and how they can withdraw them.
- Describe how participants who could be harmed by visibility or disclosure, such as residents with insecure residence status, are protected during and after the activities.
- Explain how residents with disabilities, low literacy or limited command of the local language take part on equal terms in the dialogue with local authorities.
- Identify the EU-level policy processes or bodies on inclusion or cultural participation that will receive the project's findings, and the route by which they will receive them.
- List the public outputs, giving for each its format and languages and the place where it remains accessible after the project ends.

### 4. Workplan, work packages, activities, resources and timing

Show the schedule and dependencies.

Two components are mandatory for every project (call document, section 10): a report on the implementation of each event/work package (event description sheet) as a deliverable, and a milestone for asking event attendees to take part in the EU Survey on Justice, Rights and Values once the granting authority provides the link. Each work package is an event gathering citizens or stakeholders within a defined time frame towards an output; it may combine several activities and may take place in person or online.

Direct participants are citizens or stakeholders from the target groups who actively take part and whose participation is verifiable. EU officials do not count. Count each person once per event, even across several activities. When activities in different work packages share participants and overlap or occur on the same or consecutive days, count those participants under only one work package (call document, section 10, pp. 28–29).

For the narrative counts, use the following benchmark convention: count eligible countries by the residence of counted participants, irrespective of nationality or age. Record participation through attendance lists or online participation logs. Staff, facilitators, interpreters and speakers count only if they also actively participate as members of a target group, rather than solely delivering the event; passive audiences do not count. These residence, evidence and role conventions supplement the call document’s active-and-verifiable-participation rule; they are not official tariff rules.

## Award criteria

Relevance: 40 points (minimum 25); Quality: 40; Impact: 20. At least 70/100 overall and the Relevance threshold are required; no separate Quality or Impact minimum is specified. Relevance assesses needs, EU policy fit and transnational added value; Quality assesses coherent methods, delivery, monitoring, ethics and EU values; Impact assesses plausible longer-term effects, dissemination and sustainability. These are the selected call's criteria, not a universal CERV rule.

## Length contract

The selected 2026 call permits 70 Part B pages, overriding the generic CERV form's normal 45. At a practical 500 words/page, the benchmark ceiling is 35,000 words. References, tables and captions count; only the title is exempt by benchmark convention. Excess is disregarded with zero tolerance. The form permits Arial 9 point on A4 with 15 mm margins; 500 words/page is a conservative prose estimate, not a literal reconstruction of its instruction-heavy pages.
