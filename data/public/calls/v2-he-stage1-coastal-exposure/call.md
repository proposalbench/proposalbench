## Expected outcomes

Projects are expected to contribute to the protection of coastal communities and ecosystems by improving the predictability of compounding waterborne hazards. Successful proposals will deliver actionable evidence that enhances regional preparedness and supports the implementation of integrated coastal zone management policies.

- Coastal authorities and environmental agencies benefit from validated early-warning indicators that link upstream catchment runoff to coastal hazard exposure.
- Local decision-makers utilize improved predictive models that quantify the transport of pollutants and floodwaters through estuarine systems.
- Vulnerable coastal communities experience reduced risk through the timely deployment of evidence-based mitigation and evacuation strategies.

## Scope

The intensification of extreme weather events accelerates the transfer of hazardous runoff from inland catchments through estuaries to coastal zones. Current early-warning systems often treat riverine and coastal dynamics in isolation, leaving communities vulnerable to compounding waterborne hazards. There is a critical need to bridge this gap by tracing exposure pathways across the entire hydrological continuum.

Proposals should develop and test integrated monitoring and modelling frameworks that capture the complex transport mechanisms from catchments to the coast. Research must formulate clear hypotheses regarding estuarine transport dynamics and design robust sampling or modelling strategies to quantify uncertainty in early-warning evidence.

- Develop coupled hydrological and hydrodynamic models to simulate the transport of runoff and associated hazards from inland catchments through estuaries to coastal waters.
- Design and implement temporary monitoring campaigns to collect high-resolution field data on waterborne exposure pathways, validating the proposed transport models.
- Quantify the cascading uncertainties within the modelling chain, from catchment precipitation inputs to coastal exposure outputs, to ensure reliable early-warning thresholds.
- Co-design usable early-warning indicators with local authorities, translating complex estuarine transport data into actionable evidence for coastal risk management.

## Exclusions

- Standalone urban-layout or land-use optimisation without modelling catchment-to-coast waterborne exposure pathways is outside scope.
- The engineering, design, or construction of permanent flood-barrier infrastructure.

## Award criteria

Excellence and Impact alone are evaluated, each 0–5 with equal weight. Excellence covers the objectives, ambition beyond the state of the art and soundness of the overall methodology, including its concepts, models, assumptions, interdisciplinary approaches, sex/gender analysis and open science practices. Impact covers the credibility of the pathways to the specified outcomes and impacts. This fictional topic specifies its expected effects through those outcomes. Only the first-stage aspects of the General Annexes apply. Each criterion requires at least 4. The overall threshold is set per budget envelope to admit proposals requesting as close as possible to three times, and not less than twice, the available budget. It is normally 8 or 8.5, not a guaranteed fixed 8. Passing invites a full second-stage application; it is not an award.
