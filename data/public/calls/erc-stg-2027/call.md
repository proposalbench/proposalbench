# ERC Starting Grant — PB-ERC-STG-2027

> This call is fictional and was written for ProposalBench. It adapts the amended ERC
> Work Programme 2026. The identifier, submission date and eligibility reference date
> are invented. It is not the official ERC 2027 call: the 2026 eligibility window is
> deliberately retained for this benchmark. For this fictional call, the narrative must be in English;
> this is a benchmark restriction. The official ERC permits other official EU languages
> with an English abstract.

## Purpose and award conditions

Support an emerging investigator in pursuing an ambitious scientific idea and building
an independent team or programme. There is no prescribed topic or requirement to promise
near-term economic or societal benefits.

| Condition | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Applicant | One PI, of any nationality, with one host in an EU Member State or a country associated to the ERC part of Horizon Europe |
| Duration | Up to 60 months |
| Ordinary grant | Up to EUR 1,500,000 for five years; reduced proportionally for a shorter project |
| Additional funding | Up to EUR 1,000,000; up to EUR 2,000,000 instead for eligible relocation from a non-associated third country |
| PI effort | At least 50% of working time on the project and at least 50% in EU Member States or eligible Associated Countries |
| Fictional submission date | 31 March 2027 (invented) |
| Fictional eligibility reference date | 1 January 2027 (invented) |

The PI must be more than two and no more than seven years after successful defence of
their first PhD at the fictional reference date: normally 1 January 2020 to 31 December
2024 inclusive. Document any applicable extension under the retained 2026 rules: maternity
(18 months per child or a longer documented leave), actual paternity or parental leave,
long-term illness or national service, disability, clinical training (up to four years),
major disasters, seeking asylum, or violence. The real 2027 ERC programme uses a wider window; that is not this fictional call's
condition.

The host must support the PI's independence and provide the conditions needed to conduct
the project. A second PI is not allowed. Team members and collaborators may contribute
without becoming co-PIs.

Additional funding must be necessary for the project, for example start-up expenditure,
major equipment, access to large facilities, or major experimental or field work; personnel
costs may also be included under the 2026 rules. The EUR 2,000,000 route applies to a PI
moving from outside the EU and countries associated to the ERC part of Horizon Europe
to take up the grant. Top-ups are not reduced pro rata and require explicit justification
and panel approval. Thus EUR 3,500,000 is only the largest conditional total, not the
ordinary award ceiling. Present the base and additional requests separately.

Eligible direct costs are reimbursed at 100%, with 25% indirect costs on the eligible
base, excluding subcontracting and internally invoiced goods and services from that base.
The requested EU contribution must fit the relevant ceiling including indirect costs.

## Expected outcomes

- An ambitious advance at the frontier of knowledge, led by an emerging independent principal investigator who establishes or consolidates a research team or programme.
- An original research direction with a clear explanation of what it could change in the field and of the PI’s intellectual contribution.

## Scope

- Investigator-led frontier research in any field, without a prescribed thematic priority; interdisciplinary and unconventional approaches are welcome.

## Exclusions

- A second PI or a co-PI is not permitted in this Starting Grant; collaborators and team members do not become joint principal investigators.

## Mandatory elements

- Part I must stand on its own: explain the state of knowledge, scientific question, objectives and overall research strategy.
- Part II must set out methodology, work plan, scientific risks, alternatives and implementation feasibility.
- The CV and track record must distinguish the PI’s contribution from collective work.
- State a commitment of at least 50% of the PI’s working time to the project and at least 50% of working time in an EU Member State or a country associated to the ERC part of Horizon Europe.
- Identify one eligible host and one PI; explain the PI’s first-PhD date and any documented eligibility extension.
- Separate the ordinary budget (at most EUR 1,500,000 for 60 months, reduced pro rata for shorter projects) from any justified additional request: up to EUR 1,000,000, or up to EUR 2,000,000 for a PI relocating from a non-associated third country to take up the grant.
- Justify personnel, equipment and other resources against the work, with a budget table and total request; additional funding is assessed, not automatic.

## Required narrative

### Part I of the Scientific Proposal

Present the current state of knowledge, the central question, the objectives and the
research strategy. Explain what could change, become possible, or be understood differently
if the idea succeeds. Make the scientific case understandable without Part II. Explain
why the uncertain scientific step is worth attempting, rather than relying on labels such
as high risk or breakthrough.

### Part II of the Scientific Proposal

Explain the design, methods, analysis, work packages, responsibilities and schedule.
Address feasibility, critical scientific assumptions, risks, alternatives and decision
points. Connect the work to the personnel and facilities it needs. Clearly distinguish
established findings, planned work and pending access or funding.

### Curriculum Vitae and Track Record

Describe education, qualifications, positions, up to ten selected research outputs and
relevant recognition. Explain intellectual ownership and the
significance of selected contributions. Describe relevant career context.

### Resources and Time Commitment

Include a realistic EUR budget table, personnel by category, resource justification,
and the two explicit PI time commitments. Identify the host and proposed arrangements
for scientific independence. Explain any additional funding separately. This represents
the relevant Part A material; it does not enlarge Part II. Host support letters, ethics
forms and documentary proof are separate in a real application and are outside this
narrative deliverable.

## Review criteria

Scientific excellence is the sole ERC criterion: the project's ground-breaking nature
and ambition, and the PI's intellectual capacity and creativity. At Step 1, reviewers
consider Part I and the CV and track record. Methodological feasibility is assessed at
Step 2, when Part II, resources and time commitments are also considered. There are no
separate numerical weights for impact or implementation, and no mandatory standalone
societal-impact or dissemination plan.

## Length contract

The official allowances are 5 pages for Part I, 7 for Part II, 4 for the CV and track
record, and 2 for Resources and Time Commitment. **Benchmark adaptation:** at 500 words
per page these become **2,500**, **3,500**, **2,000** and **1,000 words**, respectively,
with a combined ceiling of **9,000 words**. These allowances cannot be pooled.
Words beyond a section ceiling or the combined ceiling are disregarded, with no percentage
tolerance. Count tables, captions and in-text citations; exclude the title and reference
list. This is a benchmark word contract, not an ERC word-count policy.
