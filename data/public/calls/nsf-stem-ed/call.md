# Mathematical Reasoning and Equitable Participation in Grades 6–12 STEM Learning — PB-NSF-27-512

> This call is fictional and was written for ProposalBench. It follows the standard
> narrative structure of NSF PAPPG 24-1, with its effective supplements. The research
> emphasis, award cap, duration and submission date are invented solicitation conditions;
> they do not describe an actual NSF funding opportunity.

## Purpose and award conditions

Support rigorous research that advances knowledge and provides credible societal benefits.
Proposals should connect the call's research emphasis to a question, a justified approach
and assessable outcomes. The planned work must fit the maximum award and duration below.

| | |
|---|---|
| Maximum award | USD 1,500,000 |
| Maximum duration | 48 months |
| Expected number of awards | 20 |

The fictional submission date is **15 September 2027 (invented)**; use it to determine
the five-year window for prior NSF support.

The lead must be a US institution of higher education or a US nonprofit research
organisation, an invented restriction for this solicitation. A multi-organisation
consortium is not mandatory. The stated award cap includes direct and indirect costs.
Budgets and budget justifications are separate submissions in the NSF process and are
outside this benchmark narrative; the funding ceiling and duration still bind the proposed
work. Explain the necessary people, facilities and access arrangements.

## Expected outcomes

Projects should advance fundamental understanding of how learning environments shape mathematical reasoning and participation in grades 6–12 STEM education. Successful research should produce knowledge that researchers and educators can use to explain learning, identify barriers and inform subsequent educational design.

- Learning researchers gain stronger explanations of how instructional interactions and representations influence students’ mathematical reasoning and transfer across STEM contexts.
- Educators and curriculum designers gain evidence about the conditions under which learning environments support sustained participation and conceptual understanding among students with differing educational opportunities.
- Researchers and education partners can distinguish broadly applicable findings from findings that depend on particular learners, instructional settings or implementation conditions.
- The research community gains usable measures, documented methods and findings that support replication and further investigation while protecting student privacy.

## Scope

Mathematical reasoning connects learning across STEM disciplines, yet students may use procedures successfully without understanding their underlying relationships or applying them in unfamiliar contexts. Opportunities to explain, represent and revise ideas also vary across classrooms and student groups.

This topic supports fundamental research on how learning environments influence these processes in grades 6–12, including mathematics classrooms and other STEM settings. Proposals should investigate explanatory questions about learning and participation through theoretically grounded designs that connect features of the environment to observable processes and outcomes.

- Develop a theoretical account linking specified features of a learning environment to mathematical reasoning, participation or transfer across STEM contexts.
- Investigate learning processes in grades 6–12 through a rigorous quantitative, qualitative or mixed-methods design appropriate to the research questions.
- Assess conceptual understanding or transfer using measures whose interpretation is justified for the participating learners and settings.
- Examine how access to learning opportunities and features of the environment contribute to differences in participation or learning.
- Analyse contextual and implementation evidence to establish where the proposed explanations apply and where they require qualification.
- Produce documented research materials and findings that enable subsequent inquiry, with access arrangements appropriate to consent, confidentiality and permitted reuse.

## Exclusions

- The Project Description contains no URLs and no appendices (PAPPG 24-1); web addresses in the References Cited list are allowed.
- Curriculum development, teacher professional development or service delivery whose principal purpose is implementation without an accompanying fundamental research question about learning.
- Technology development or product evaluation focused solely on usability, adoption or commercial performance.
- Studies confined to postsecondary education, adult workforce training or grades below grade 6.
- Studies limited to achievement comparisons without investigating learning processes or features of the learning environment.

## Specific conditions and mandatory elements

- Project Summary must contain distinct Overview, Intellectual Merit and Broader Impacts headings with substantive text.
- Project Description must include its own separate section headed exactly Broader Impacts, with activities and a means of documenting their outputs.
- Where any PI or co-PI has current NSF funding or an NSF award ending within the five years before submission, include Results from Prior NSF Support with award details and results; report the most closely related qualifying award for each such investigator.
- Identify the focal grades, STEM content, learning environment and proposed explanatory mechanism; explain how the research can refine or challenge existing theory.
- Justify participant and setting selection, measures and analysis. Address clustering, attrition, statistical power or qualitative evidentiary adequacy as applicable, and distinguish causal claims from descriptive or interpretive claims.
- Explain how equity informs the research questions, recruitment, measurement and interpretation. Justify any group comparisons and avoid treating group membership itself as an explanation of learning differences.
- Specify evidence that would support, qualify or contradict the proposed explanation, and describe how alternative explanations will be examined.

## Required narrative

### Project Summary

This is a one-page component, separate from the Project Description. Use three distinct
headings within it:

- **Overview:** the activity to be undertaken, its objectives and the approach.
- **Intellectual Merit:** how the work could advance knowledge or understanding.
- **Broader Impacts:** how the activity could benefit society or advance a societal goal.

Write a self-contained account understandable by researchers beyond the immediate specialty.
The three parts share the Summary allowance; they are not three separate pages.

### Project Description

Explain the scientific question, relation to existing knowledge, objectives and expected
contribution. Provide a reasoned research design, methods and analysis, a realistic work
plan, a way to assess success, and the qualifications and resources needed. Describe
uncertainties, feasible alternatives and relevant ethical or participant considerations
as they affect the design. Describe the status of relevant approvals, collaborations and access arrangements.

Include a separate section with the heading **Broader Impacts** on its own line within
the Project Description, even though the Summary has a part with the same name. Describe
concrete activities directed at societal goals, the rationale for them and how their
outputs will be documented. Broader impacts can arise through the research itself,
related activities or complementary activities; an unrelated outreach programme is not
mandatory. Project-level output records can accompany outcome evaluation at a larger
programme level.

#### Results from Prior NSF Support

Include this subsection only when applicable: a PI or co-PI has current NSF funding,
including a no-cost extension, or an NSF award ending within the five years before the
submission date stated above. Support need not be related to this proposal. For each
qualifying investigator, report the most closely related qualifying award; do not repeat
a shared award unnecessarily. Include its number, title, amount and support period;
accomplishments under separate **Intellectual Merit** and **Broader Impacts** headings;
publications with full citations here or in References Cited (or a statement that none were produced); research products and evidence of their availability;
and, for a renewal, how prior work relates to the request. For a recent award without
results, describe its goals and broader impacts. Identify any award-reporting details still to be confirmed. This account occupies at most five
of the Description's fifteen pages, not five extra pages.

A References Cited list may follow. Budget forms, biosketches, facilities statements,
mentoring plans and the Data Management and Sharing Plan required in the actual NSF
process are outside this benchmark deliverable. The PAPPG supplements update, among other
matters, data sharing and public access; their separate forms are not extra narrative
sections requested here. As in PAPPG 24-1, the Project Description must not contain URLs,
and appendices are not permitted.

## Merit review criteria

Both **Intellectual Merit** (advancing knowledge) and **Broader Impacts** (benefiting society)
receive full consideration. NSF specifies no numerical weighting between these criteria
for this standard review. Reviewers consider these five elements for both criteria:

1. The potential contribution to knowledge and understanding, and to desired societal outcomes.
2. The creativity, originality or potentially transformative character of the proposed concepts.
3. Whether the plan is reasoned, organised and justified, with a mechanism for assessing success.
4. The qualifications of the investigator, team and organisation for the proposed activities.
5. Whether adequate resources are available through the home organisation or collaborations.

An established broader-impact approach can be justified; every outreach activity need not
be novel. Explain what will be done, why it should work and what evidence will show progress.

## Length contract

NSF allows **one page for Project Summary** and **15 pages for Project Description**,
including at most five pages of applicable Results from Prior NSF Support. **Benchmark
adaptation:** at 600 words per page, the limits are **600 words for the Summary** and
**9,000 words for the Description**, with a combined ceiling of **9,600 words**.
The prior-support account is limited to **3,000 words within the Description**, not in
addition to it. The Summary and Description allowances cannot be transferred.

Exceeding either section limit, the applicable prior-support sublimit, or the combined
ceiling makes the submission **inadmissible**, with no percentage tolerance. Tables,
captions and in-text citations count; the title and References Cited list do not. The
Summary counts even if it resembles an abstract. This is a benchmark word contract,
not an NSF word-count policy; it adapts the strict page limits for plain-text comparison.
