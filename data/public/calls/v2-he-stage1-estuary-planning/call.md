## Expected outcomes

Project results are expected to contribute to all the following outcomes:

- Urban planning and water management authorities can use credible evidence on interacting estuarine flooding and urban drainage to choose spatial adaptation options under uncertain future conditions.
- Authorities and affected communities can identify how adaptation options redistribute flood exposure, benefits and burdens across neighbourhoods and population groups, supporting decisions that address existing inequalities.
- Residents, including groups poorly represented in planning processes, have an evidenced route to influence adaptation choices and understand the consequences for their neighbourhoods.
- Authorities in other estuarine urban areas can judge which findings are transferable and under what physical, social and planning conditions.

## Scope

Estuarine urban areas face interacting tidal, river and rainfall hazards whose consequences depend on drainage networks, land use and settlement patterns. Adaptation can reduce aggregate exposure while transferring risk or imposing costs on particular communities.

Proposals should combine estuarine flood and drainage science with urban planning and distributional analysis. Comparative research should connect physical evidence to spatial choices and residents’ exposure, using co-design to examine feasible adaptation options and their consequences.

- Characterise interactions between estuarine water levels, river flows, rainfall and urban drainage under current and plausible future conditions, including coincident events and drainage constraints.
- Establish physical evidence through observations or validated simulations, documenting fitness for the intended spatial decisions and limitations in representing relevant flood and drainage processes.
- Compare spatial adaptation options against an explicit reference scenario, including land-use planning, water retention, nature-based measures or changes to drainage operation.
- Translate physical evidence into spatially explicit estimates of residents’ exposure for each option, accounting for the location and use of housing, public space and essential services.
- Assess how exposure changes, adaptation costs, access to benefits and potential displacement are distributed across population groups and neighbourhoods, including effects transferred beyond intervention areas.
- Propagate uncertainty from physical processes, future development and social conditions through the comparison of options, identifying decisions that remain credible across plausible assumptions.
- Co-design adaptation options and assessment priorities with residents, planning authorities and drainage managers, providing accessible ways for underrepresented groups to participate.
- Compare findings across contrasting estuarine urban settings to identify transferable relationships and the contextual limits of applying them elsewhere.

## Exclusions

- Construction of permanent flood barriers; comparative assessment of such options and temporary monitoring installations remain within scope.
- Routine drainage infrastructure renewal or expansion presented as the principal project activity.
- Standalone flood forecasting or hazard mapping without an explicit connection to spatial planning choices and the distribution of residents’ exposure.

## Specific conditions and mandatory elements

- Explain how the methodological concepts, models and assumptions enable the project objectives.
- Address sex and gender analysis in the research content, or give a project-specific justification for its irrelevance.
- Explain the open science practices used in the methodology, with project-specific reasons for any exceptions.
- Explain the causal route by which the project results would deliver the stated topic outcomes for the target beneficiaries.
- Explain how resident-level data linked to spatial exposure estimates will be protected against re-identification of individuals or households in published maps, datasets and other outputs.
- Specify how disagreements among residents, planning authorities and drainage managers about adaptation options will be recorded and reflected in the comparison of options.

## Award criteria

Excellence and Impact alone are evaluated, each 0–5 with equal weight. Excellence covers the objectives, ambition beyond the state of the art and soundness of the overall methodology, including its concepts, models, assumptions, interdisciplinary approaches, sex/gender analysis and open science practices. Impact covers the credibility of the pathways to the specified outcomes and impacts. This fictional topic specifies its expected effects through those outcomes. Only the first-stage aspects of the General Annexes apply. Each criterion requires at least 4. The overall threshold is set per budget envelope to admit proposals requesting as close as possible to three times, and not less than twice, the available budget. It is normally 8 or 8.5, not a guaranteed fixed 8. Passing invites a full second-stage application; it is not an award.
