# PB-NIH-R21-2027-02: Objective Sleep Patterns and Cardiometabolic Risk in Adolescents and Young Adults

> Fictional funding notice for ProposalBench; the identifier and scientific emphasis are invented. The R21 conditions are adapted from NIH parent announcement PA-25-304 (Clinical Trial Not Allowed) and NIH guidance checked on 30 October 2026.

## Purpose and award conditions

The R21 supports exploratory or developmental research at an early conceptual stage. It can
support a risky idea, an unfamiliar model or population, or a new use of an existing method
when the potential scientific gain justifies the uncertainty. A small version of an
established, incremental research program is not the intended use of this mechanism.
Preliminary data are **not required**. A persuasive rationale may rest on published research;
any applicant-generated results included should be described with their limitations.

| Condition | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Mechanism | R21 exploratory/developmental grant; new application |
| Project period | At most 2 years (24 months) |
| Direct costs | At most USD 275,000 across the project, with at most USD 200,000 in either year |
| Indirect costs | Separate from these direct-cost ceilings; consortium F&A costs are excluded when testing the direct-cost limit |
| Clinical trials | Not allowed under this notice |
| Applicant | An eligible research organization, including a US university or nonprofit research institute, with a suitably qualified PI; early-career status is welcome but not an eligibility requirement |
| Narrative language | English |

The funding ceilings constrain the scale of the work. No budget or budget justification is
requested in the benchmark narrative. If costs are mentioned voluntarily, they must respect
both ceilings. Under the NIH definition, a clinical trial is a study in which one or more human
participants are prospectively assigned to one or more interventions to evaluate the effects
of those interventions on health-related biomedical or behavioral outcomes. Observational,
formative and secondary-data research with human participants is allowed; whether work
involves human subjects must still be assessed and described.

## Scientific emphasis and expected outcomes

This fictional ProposalBench topic is a benchmark research emphasis, not a real NIH priority. Sleep timing, duration, and regularity are potentially modifiable contributors to cardiometabolic risk during adolescence and young adulthood, when school, work, and social schedules can disrupt sleep. Associations with adiposity, blood pressure, and glucose regulation warrant investigation, but correlated sleep characteristics, confounding, and bidirectional relationships limit interpretation. Wider availability of wearable measurements and repeated cardiometabolic assessments creates an opportunity for exploratory studies that establish measurement feasibility and test bounded hypotheses before larger investigations.

Address all of the following outcomes:

- Investigators will have evidence of whether objective sleep assessment linked to cardiometabolic measurements yields sufficiently complete and interpretable data in the selected adolescent or young-adult population.
- Researchers will have estimates, with uncertainty, that clarify a specified relationship between sleep patterns and cardiometabolic risk, including what remains unresolved when findings do not support the proposed hypothesis.
- Investigators planning a larger study will have evidence-based criteria for proceeding, revising the hypothesis or measurement strategy, or discontinuing the proposed line of investigation.

Proposals should address an unresolved relationship between sleep patterns and cardiometabolic risk in adolescents, young adults, or a scientifically justified group spanning these developmental periods. The rationale should explain why the selected sleep characteristic and cardiometabolic measure can provide informative exploratory evidence.

Observational studies, analyses of existing objectively measured sleep data, and mechanistic investigations without prospective assignment to interventions are responsive. Designs should distinguish evidence consistent with a biological pathway from evidence establishing causation.

- Evaluate a bounded hypothesis linking objectively measured sleep timing, duration, or regularity to a specified cardiometabolic phenotype, explaining how correlated sleep characteristics will inform interpretation.
- Assess a plausible biological pathway or temporal relationship using observational comparisons, repeated measurements, or existing data, with explicit consideration of confounding and reverse causation.
- Determine whether the proposed measurement and recruitment or data-access strategy can support a larger investigation, identifying uncertainties that exploratory findings must resolve.

Not supported under this notice:

- Clinical trials as defined by NIH are not allowed.

Mandatory elements:

- Specific objectives and expected scientific contribution appropriate to an exploratory project.
- Critical appraisal of the rigor of supporting published or unpublished evidence and an Approach that addresses its weaknesses; preliminary data are optional.
- Units of analysis, sample-size rationale, comparisons or controls, measures against bias, and plans for data collection, analysis, and interpretation.
- Feasibility strategy, anticipated problems, alternatives, and success criteria achievable within 24 months.
- Relevant biological variables such as sex and age in design and analysis, with justified restrictions, and inclusion across sex/gender, race, ethnicity and age where human participants are involved.
- Explain the scientific use of personnel and resources, including relevant expertise, resource access and the status of planned arrangements.
- Use objective sleep measurements to derive the primary sleep exposure, and justify the instrument, recording schedule, and exposure definition for the selected population. Distinguish behavioral sleep timing from endogenous circadian phase; claims about circadian phase require an appropriate phase measure.
- Specify sleep-data quality procedures covering valid recording periods, nonwear or signal loss, sleep–wake classification, and missingness. Explain how sampling across school, work, and free days supports the proposed timing, duration, or regularity estimates.

## Required research narrative

Submit one Markdown document with these two main sections, in order:

### Specific Aims

State the research problem, the central question or hypothesis, the objectives, expected
findings, and their potential scientific contribution. Keep the aims appropriate to an
exploratory award and explain what can be learned even if the main hypothesis is unsupported.

### Research Strategy

Use the following child headings in this order:

- **Significance:** Explain the unresolved problem and why the proposed advance matters.
  Evaluate strengths and weaknesses of the prior published and unpublished evidence,
  including the applicant's own work where relevant. Distinguish an established observation from an inference that this project will test.
- **Innovation:** Explain the conceptual or methodological advance relative to current
  research. Identify the advantage of the proposed application of the methods; using a recent
  technique is not by itself a novelty argument.
- **Approach:** Describe the design, methods, collection and analysis of data, interpretation,
  comparisons or controls, and rationale for the sample size and unit of analysis. Address
  weaknesses in the supporting evidence, measures against bias, relevant biological variables,
  anticipated difficulties, alternatives, and criteria for success. For an immature method,
  explain how feasibility will be established and risk managed within the award period.
  Explain personnel roles, relevant expertise, resource access, and the status of any
  arrangements needed to execute the design. Identify risks to participants or
  hazards and the protections appropriate to them.

Address sex and other relevant biological variables, including age where pertinent, in design
and analysis. Justify restrictions scientifically. If human subjects are proposed, the
scientific design should be consistent with appropriate inclusion of women, racial and ethnic
minorities, and people across the lifespan. Detailed enrollment and protection forms belong
elsewhere in an actual NIH application.

Only the two narrative sections and the three Research Strategy children above are required.
A reference list may follow. Budgets, biosketches, facilities attachments, human-subjects forms,
vertebrate-animal attachments, authentication plans, and data-sharing plans are outside this
benchmark deliverable.

## Review criteria

This notice uses the NIH Simplified Review Framework, applicable to R21 due dates from
25 January 2025 onward. Lower numeric scores indicate stronger assessments.

| Review element | Assessment | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| Factor 1: Importance of the Research (Significance, Innovation) | 1–9 | Value of the question and potential advance; soundness of the supporting evidence and rationale |
| Factor 2: Rigor and Feasibility (Approach) | 1–9 | Reliable and unbiased inference; suitable comparisons, sample sizes and analysis; for human-participant research, inclusion plans appropriate to the scientific goals; achievable work and credible management of uncertainty |
| Factor 3: Expertise and Resources | Sufficient / gaps identified; no numeric score | Whether the proposed team and environment can do this work, judged in relation to career stage and the actual tasks; explain any gaps |
| Overall Impact | Individual reviewer score 1–9 | Integrated judgment of the project's potential lasting influence, considering all three factors and applicable additional review criteria |

Overall Impact is not a prescribed arithmetic average. The exploratory purpose matters: lack of
preliminary data alone is not a defect, and ambitious uncertainty should be assessed alongside
the possible scientific gain. Inclusion plans are assessed within Factor 2. Protections for
human subjects, vertebrate-animal welfare and biohazards are Additional Review Criteria when
applicable; this
narrative must describe a scientifically and ethically coherent design, not reproduce those
forms.

## Length contract

NIH permits **1 page for Specific Aims** and **6 pages for Research Strategy**, with the latter
limit shared by Significance, Innovation, and Approach. The page allowances are not transferable.
Over-limit applications are not accepted for review; excess narrative is not simply ignored.

**Benchmark conversion:** use **600 words per page**, giving **600 words for Specific Aims**
and **3,600 words for Research Strategy**, with a **4,200-word combined ceiling**.
Exceeding either section ceiling or the combined ceiling makes the submission
**inadmissible**. Tables, captions, and in-text citations count; the proposal title and
reference list do not.

This is a benchmark word contract, not an NIH word-count rule. Its format basis is US Letter,
Arial 11 pt, 0.5-inch margins, at most 15 characters per inch and six lines per vertical inch;
600 words is a practical narrative estimate per page.
