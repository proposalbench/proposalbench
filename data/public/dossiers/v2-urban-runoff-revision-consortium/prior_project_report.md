# Runoff–Reception Ledger: final scientific report

**Project period: 1 January 2022–31 December 2024**  
**Final report accepted: 28 February 2025**

Principal investigator: Elena Sorlí Valderra, Universidad de Valdebruma del Litoral. Collaborating institutions: Institut indépendant d’hydrologie de Rivenne, Instituto Costeiro da Enseada Clara and Associazione dei Comuni delle Acque Civiche.

## Award and objectives

The university's Territorial Systems Research Fund awarded EUR 420,000 under reference UVL-TS-2021-17. Recorded expenditure was EUR 250,000 for personnel, EUR 60,000 for instruments and consumables, EUR 45,000 for laboratory analysis, EUR 30,000 for travel and transcription, and EUR 35,000 for institutional support. These amounts total EUR 420,000. European Union contribution: EUR 0. Closing balance: EUR 0.

The project examined whether a common event record could connect urban runoff measurements, receiving-water observations and the responsibilities described by municipal staff. Its deliverables were an observational collection, analysis scripts and a comparative administrative study.

## Sites and measurement design

Three study catchments were coded A, B and C in the shared research files. They lie respectively on the eastern Spanish coast, the Portuguese Atlantic coast and the northern Italian Adriatic coast. Each includes an urban drainage outlet connected to an estuarine receiving reach. Detailed access coordinates are withheld in the public documentation because some observations were made from restricted maintenance sites.

The measured drainage areas were 2.1, 3.4 and 2.8 square kilometres. Their differing outlet geometry, tidal influence and drainage maintenance histories prevent interpreting them as replicate experimental units. The project obtained observations during 12 storms, four per catchment, between October 2022 and November 2024. Collection used manual timed sampling and depended on safe staff access. The collection excludes the most severe locally reported flooding.

Each storm contributed six timed sampling rounds at three stations: an upstream drain, the outlet and an estuarine receiving station. This produced 216 scheduled water samples. Twelve samples were excluded because of bottle damage or inadequate sample volume, leaving 204 valid suspended-solids measurements. All storms retained at least one valid measurement at each station. Fifteen-minute level records and discharge rating observations were associated with the sampling rounds. The event, rather than an individual time step or bottle, was the unit used for uncertainty estimation.

## Numerical findings

The mean outlet suspended-solids concentration across the 12 event means was 86 mg/L, with a 95% event-bootstrap interval of 61–111 mg/L. Each event mean was calculated first, so events with more surviving bottles did not receive greater weight. The estimate describes the sampled events rather than annual average conditions.

For the fitted rainfall–runoff model, discharge root mean squared error was 0.18 m³/s, with a 95% event-bootstrap interval of 0.12–0.25 m³/s. The calculation pooled residuals within events and resampled whole events for the interval. These are in-sample fit statistics: parameters and processing choices had been informed by every one of the 12 storms.

Turbidity observations supported the timing of suspended-material pulses, but the relationship with laboratory suspended solids differed between sites. The receiving-water work compared the order and approximate timing of observed pulses at outlets and estuarine stations. Sparse sampling and tidal reversals prevented reliable attribution of a concentration peak to an individual urban source. A transferable estuarine transport model remains unestablished.

The measured transport endpoint was suspended solids. Microbial, nutrient and chemical-toxicity endpoints were outside the analytical programme.

## Administrative study

The qualitative component comprised 24 semi-structured interviews with municipal technical or administrative employees, eight in each study country. The analysis used a shared coding guide and a second-reader review of passages concerning responsibility for action. Eighteen interviewees could identify an office that distributed environmental information; six could identify a documented route from a model output to an authorised operational decision. These are interview findings rather than an audit of legal competence.

Recruitment used institutional contacts and included municipal employees only. Interview consent covered research analysis and anonymised quotation. The resulting material describes staff perceptions and administrative arrangements.

## Research record

The Runoff–Reception Event Ledger release package contains event summaries, an instrument dictionary and analysis scripts. Public summaries exclude restricted source layers and personal data. Input permissions and transcript access are recorded in the separate resources register.
