## Criterion 1 — Excellence

**Score: 3.00 (Threshold: 3/5.00, Weight: -)**

The objectives are relevant and generally clear. The proposal addresses the connection between urban runoff and estuarine receiving waters and recognises that environmental information must be interpreted within municipal procedures. The combination of field measurements, modelling and administrative research is appropriate to this problem. The previous observations demonstrate that the partners can collect and organise comparable event records.

However, the validation methodology does not adequately separate model development from evaluation. Time steps described as withheld belong to storms that have already informed parameter choices, preprocessing and model selection. Consequently, the reported fit and the proposed interval-based tests do not establish independent forecasting skill. The new event campaign is not accompanied by a locked held-out-event allocation, an explicit rule preventing subsequent tuning on test events, or criteria for deciding whether performance supports demonstration. This is a shortcoming because credible warnings depend on performance outside the calibration collection.

The proposed link to receiving-water transport is scientifically relevant. Its assessment will depend on a clearer account of how uncertainty propagates through the coupled components, within the validation approach described above.

## Criterion 2 — Impact

**Score: 3.00 (Threshold: 3/5.00, Weight: -)**

The proposed outputs could be useful to municipal water managers and the association provides a credible route for professional exchange. The dissemination activities and comparison across administrative settings are appropriate. Open release of permitted software and documentation is positively assessed.

Nevertheless, municipal adoption is insufficiently substantiated. No responsible operational owner is identified for accepting or maintaining the demonstrations, and no decision threshold is linked to an authorised municipal action. Workshops and training alone do not establish an adoption route. The proposal leaves unclear who would accept a false-alarm burden, approve escalation or decide that a forecast is unsuitable for use.

In addition, underserved and vulnerable residents are absent from the co-design arrangements. Their needs are represented through municipal officers rather than through direct participation or a justified alternative. There is no recruitment pathway or provision addressing barriers to participation. The stated equity objective is therefore not supported by the activities and resources described.

## Criterion 3 — Quality and efficiency of the implementation

**Score: 2.50 (Threshold: 3/5.00, Weight: -)**

The consortium brings complementary competence in planning, hydrology, coastal processes and municipal administration. The distribution of broad scientific responsibilities is reasonable. The principal cost categories and partner totals are internally consistent.

However, the modelling and deployment dependencies are not adequately timed or costed. Receiving-water modelling and demonstrations depend on drainage inputs, permissions, interface checks and stable upstream outputs whose availability is assumed. Several handovers have no responsible delivery role, duration or identifiable resource provision. The schedule overlaps modelling and deployment without adequate gates or contingency, making the feasibility of the demonstrations and final transfer uncertain. This shortcoming is not resolved by the general coordination arrangements.
