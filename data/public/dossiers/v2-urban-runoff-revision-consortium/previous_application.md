# Catchment Warning Commons

## 1. Excellence

### Objectives and research questions

Catchment Warning Commons will examine how storm-generated material moves through urban drainage and into estuarine receiving waters, and how forecasts of that movement can inform municipal decisions. Three demonstration catchments, one each in Spain, Portugal and Italy, will connect drainage-outlet forecasts to receiving-water observations. Our research question is whether a shared forecasting structure can retain local hydrological detail while supplying information that municipal officers can interpret consistently.

The first objective is to develop a rainfall–runoff forecasting workflow that estimates outlet discharge and suspended-material pulses. The second is to connect those outlet estimates to a receiving-water transport representation. The third is to identify warning formats and administrative arrangements suitable for municipal use. A fourth objective is to compare performance and implementation conditions between the three sites.

The project will retain both the urban source and estuarine receptor. Urban land use affects runoff generation and mobilisation; receiving-water conditions affect the timing and interpretation of contamination signals. The intended outputs are research models, event records, warning demonstrations and administrative guidance. Their common event structure will allow partners to trace an observation from rainfall and drainage conditions through to the information presented to a municipal user.

### Preliminary evidence

The applicants completed Runoff–Reception Ledger with a university award of EUR 420,000. That study recorded 12 storms, four at each catchment. It provided 204 valid suspended-solids samples from 216 scheduled samples and a common structure for relating environmental observations to municipal responsibilities. Mean outlet suspended solids across event means were 86 mg/L, with a 95% bootstrap interval of 61–111 mg/L.

The fitted runoff model produced a discharge root mean squared error of 0.18 m³/s. The observation and modelling chain was implemented using the partners' field instruments and computational facilities. Twenty-four interviews established contacts with technical and administrative officers and documented differences in the organisation of public information. These contacts form the initial user network.

The action will extend this work from observation and simulation to a repeatable municipal demonstration. Suspended solids will serve as the first measured transport indicator, with additional contamination indicators considered where local analytical arrangements permit. Comparing the timing of outlet and receiving-water pulses will help identify where local tidal and drainage conditions require separate parameterisations.

### Approach

The environmental work combines rainfall records, drainage representation, level observations, discharge rating information and water sampling. Shared preprocessing will align timestamps, identify event boundaries and attach instrument-quality flags. Site-specific runoff parameters will be estimated before comparison of model structures across catchments. A transport component will receive predicted outlet hydrographs and material-pulse estimates.

During model development, the existing storm records will be divided into training and evaluation time steps. Selected intervals will be withheld from parameter fitting, while event-level observations will inform choices of lag, runoff representation and preprocessing. Validation will report prediction error on the withheld intervals and agreement between observed and simulated pulse timing. Performance on the withheld intervals will be re-assessed after each model refinement.

A new field campaign will seek 36 additional storms, 12 at each site, during the demonstration period. These events will extend the range of rainfall and receiving-water conditions. Successive event records will be incorporated into model comparison as laboratory results become available. The expanding collection will support adjustments to the model and examples of warning behaviour under contrasting conditions.

The social and administrative research will use interviews, workshops and comparative document analysis. Municipal technical officers will review warning displays and discuss which messages are useful. The association will convene exchanges between authorities and prepare guidance. Existing institutional contacts will provide the initial workshop participants. Municipal officers will relay the needs of older residents and households in low-lying areas, drawing on their experience of local communications and service delivery.

## 2. Impact

The expected scientific contribution is a common account of the chain from rainfall to outlet response, estuarine transport and public information. The expected administrative contribution is a set of warning demonstrations and procedures that can be adapted locally. The comparison will retain differences between sites in drainage geometry, tidal conditions and administrative practice.

Municipal uptake will be supported through demonstration meetings, training materials and the association's communications. Participating technical departments will identify operational users as results become available. Thresholds will be agreed with participating technical departments during the demonstrations. Workshop discussions will consider how officers interpret uncertainty and what information they require before escalating a warning.

Equity is treated as a requirement for accessible information. Warning formats will be assessed for clarity and translated for local use. Municipal officers will provide feedback on communication with older residents, residents with limited language proficiency and households in low-lying areas. Their comments will inform the wording and presentation of demonstration messages.

Dissemination will include journal articles, public research summaries and exchanges with water managers. Exploitation will centre on reusable software and administrative guidance. Model code developed by the partners will be released where rights permit. A data management plan will describe environmental data, documentation and access conditions. Interview material and third-party inputs will be managed under their respective consent and licensing arrangements. Training materials will include worked examples that can be discussed within municipal departments.

## 3. Implementation

The work is organised into five work packages. Coordination and data management run from M1 to M48. Catchment observations and runoff modelling run from M1 to M30. Receiving-water modelling runs from M10 to M36. Municipal demonstrations and warning interpretation run from M25 to M45. Synthesis, training and transfer run from M37 to M48.

Opening activities will assemble field-access documentation and harmonise input data. The receiving-water component will begin with provisional runoff outputs, allowing development to proceed alongside the catchment work. Municipal demonstrations will use successive versions of the coupled workflow. Regular partner meetings will review model changes, field progress and requirements emerging from user discussions. Delayed observations will be managed through adjustments to field scheduling and the sequence of demonstration examples.

Valdebruma coordinates the action and leads planning analysis. Rivenne leads hydrological modelling and shared computational processing. Enseada Clara leads receiving-water work and laboratory coordination. Acque Civiche leads municipal exchanges, training and administrative comparison. Each lead will report progress and resource use to the coordinator at the project meetings.

### Submitted resources

The requested contribution is allocated as follows: Valdebruma EUR 2,040,000; Rivenne EUR 1,275,000; Enseada Clara EUR 1,020,000; Acque Civiche EUR 765,000. The total is EUR 5,100,000. Reimbursement is requested at 100% of eligible costs for the four nonprofit applicants.

The aggregate cost estimate comprises EUR 3,060,000 personnel, EUR 1,020,000 other direct costs and EUR 1,020,000 indirect costs. The indirect estimate is 25% of EUR 4,080,000 eligible direct costs. No subcontracting is included. Other direct costs cover field equipment, consumables, laboratory access, travel and demonstration activities. Partner administrators will monitor expenditure against these categories throughout implementation.

Work-package allocations are EUR 510,000 for coordination and data management, EUR 1,530,000 for catchment observations and modelling, EUR 1,275,000 for receiving-water modelling, EUR 1,020,000 for municipal demonstrations, and EUR 765,000 for synthesis and transfer. Final training and transfer activities will consolidate the demonstration experience into documented research workflows and municipal guidance.
