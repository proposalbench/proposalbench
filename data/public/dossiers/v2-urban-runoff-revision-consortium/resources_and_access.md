## Data and permissions

The national Spanish catchment-data licence for the Drainage Atlas Series, issued by Cartografía Hídrica de Valdemarea, S.L., a Spanish geospatial data provider, covers catchment A only and ends on 31 December 2026; no renewal has been executed as of this register date. Its terms require cessation of analytical use at expiry, with a sealed audit copy retained solely for contractual record-keeping.

Catchments B and C used municipal pipe-and-outlet extracts supplied under data-sharing letters limited to the 2022–2024 measurement period. Those letters now permit retention for audit but do not authorise further analytical use. The partners retain rights to their own permitted event summaries and instrument records; public packages contain derived tables where release was authorised.

A data-inventory exercise completed on 15 July 2026 identified public rainfall observations and public elevation products as alternative inputs, with source-specific attribution and reuse conditions entered in the inventory. ERA5-Land was inspected for historical meteorological context. Its hourly, approximately nine-kilometre grid is too coarse to represent street-scale convective rainfall.

The exercise also assessed national radar products: AEMET radar composites for Spain, IPMA radar imagery for Portugal and the Italian Civil Protection Department's radar mosaic. Public display access was available for inspection. The inventory distinguished display access from permission and technical arrangements for automated retrieval, archival storage and redistribution. AEMET OpenData offers reusable meteorological information with attribution and API-key access; the suitability and terms of the particular radar output needed by the collaboration remained under review. For the Portuguese and Italian products, bulk retrieval and redistribution conditions had not been cleared by the partner data stewards.

For all three radar sources, end-to-end delivery latency at the research server was unmeasured. An operational quantitative nowcast feed had not been arranged. The inventory recorded radar extrapolation as a candidate research method, conditional on obtaining timestamped inputs with suitable coverage. Its assessment supplied neither a measured forecast lead time nor an operational availability estimate. The partners' existing rainfall holdings are observations.

The lower-detail route documented in the inventory combines permitted public rainfall records, public terrain information and prospective local-sensor observations. It represents surface drainage and measured outlet response at coarser resolution than the earlier connected-pipe analysis. Station coverage, installation access and rainfall-input latency constrain its use. Collection through this route had not started at the register date.
