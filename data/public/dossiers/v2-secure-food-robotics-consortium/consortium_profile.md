# Consortium Profile: Secure Food Robotics Initiative

This document outlines the core partners committed to the joint development of resilient AI decision support and robotic automation for food logistics. The consortium brings together expertise in computer security, robotics, food microbiology, and systems engineering. As of 10 September 2026, the Fictional German university coordinator (TUF), independent Italian robotics SME (ALV), Spanish food microbiology institute (IMAA) and Austrian logistics cooperative (AKK) have signed participation agreements for the upcoming proposal.

## Coordinator: Technical University of Falkenburg (TUF), Germany

**Role:** Project coordination, systems engineering, and computer security.

The Technical University of Falkenburg (TUF) is a leading research institution in Germany, hosting the Secure Cyber-Physical Systems Group led by Prof. Dr. Lukas Varnkötter. TUF will oversee the integration of security protocols and resilient AI decision-support systems. The group has extensive experience in designing algorithms that detect and mitigate sensor faults and adversarial data injections in distributed networks. TUF will ensure that the data pipelines connecting robotic platforms, environmental sensors, and decision engines remain secure and reliable under operational stress.

## Partner 1: Automazione Logistica Veloce S.r.l. (ALV), Italy

**Role:** Robotic hardware provision, autonomous navigation, and mechatronics.

ALV is an independent Italian robotics SME based in Turin, specialising in the design and deployment of Autonomous Guided Vehicles (AGVs) for industrial warehousing. Founded in 2015, ALV has a strong track record of developing agile robotic solutions for dynamic environments. Within the consortium, ALV is responsible for the physical robotic platforms and the low-level control systems. Their engineering team will implement the fault-tolerant navigation algorithms developed by TUF onto the robotic hardware, ensuring that the AGVs can safely operate even when individual sensors degrade or fail.

## Partner 2: Instituto de Microbiología Alimentaria de Aragón (IMAA), Spain

**Role:** Food microbiology, spoilage modelling, and hazard assessment.

IMAA is a Spanish public research institute located in Zaragoza, dedicated to advancing food safety and quality. The institute brings critical domain expertise in food microbiology to the consortium. IMAA will develop predictive models for microbial growth and spoilage under varying environmental conditions, specifically focusing on interrupted cold chains. Cold-chain adaptation can combine the Spanish laboratory and Austrian operator to create a comprehensive farm-to-fork validation pipeline. IMAA's researchers will define the biological constraints and safety thresholds that the AI decision-support system must respect when optimising logistics operations.

## Partner 3: Alpen-Kühlkette eG (AKK), Austria

**Role:** Logistics operations, cold-chain infrastructure, and end-user validation.

Alpen-Kühlkette eG (AKK) is an Austrian logistics cooperative headquartered in Innsbruck, operating a network of temperature-controlled warehouses and distribution centres across Central Europe. AKK represents the end-user perspective, providing the operational context and infrastructure necessary for testing and validating the consortium's technologies. They will supply historical environmental data, define operational requirements, and facilitate the deployment of prototype systems within their facilities to ensure that the developed solutions meet the rigorous demands of commercial food logistics.

## Informal Contact: University of Boreal Sciences, Canada

**Role:** Advisory on North American logistics standards.

Dr. Elias Thorne, a researcher at the University of Boreal Sciences in Canada, serves as an informal contact for the consortium. His research focuses on trans-Atlantic supply chain interoperability. He provides ad-hoc advice on North American regulatory frameworks and logistics standards. Because he is an informal contact and not a participating beneficiary, he does not count as an EU/associated-country entity for the stated minimum participation requirements, despite Canada's association to Horizon Europe Pillar II. His involvement is strictly advisory and involves no transfer of funds or formal contractual obligations.
