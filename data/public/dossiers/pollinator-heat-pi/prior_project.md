# Progress report: PB-FILATHERM — Heat waves and plant–pollinator networks along an elevation gradient

**Grant reference:** PB-CESERAL-2025-01 (CESERAL Institutional Emerging Groups Programme).
**Budget:** EUR 181,500 (including 21% indirect costs; the 21% rate is a condition of this institutional award). Period: 1 September 2025 – 31 August 2028 (36 months). Status: ongoing; this report covers the first 12 months (to 31 August 2026).
**Principal investigator:** Dr. Elena Miravet Solana, CESERAL, Almería.
**Team:** PI + 1 PhD student (Marcos Gallego Ruiz, institutional predoctoral contract).

## 1. Objectives

1. Characterise plant–pollinator network structure (connectance, nestedness, modularity, interaction evenness) at four elevations under ambient and heat-wave conditions.
2. Measure critical thermal maximum (CTmax) for the dominant pollinator species and relate it to observed visitation declines during natural heat waves.
3. Quantify the elevation-dependent effect of heat waves on pollination service (fruit set of focal plant species).

## 2. Study system and design

- **Study area:** Sierra de los Filabres, Almería province, south-east Spain.
- **Sites:** Four permanent plots along an elevation gradient: 450 m (lowland scrub), 950 m (open woodland), 1,450 m (montane scrub) and 1,900 m (oro-Mediterranean grassland). Each plot is a 50 × 50 m quadrat.
- **Sampling:** Pollinator–plant interactions recorded in standardised 15-minute observation bouts on all flowering species within each plot. Sampling conducted weekly from March to July in 2026 (first full season), yielding 18 weekly rounds. A second season (2027) is planned.
- **Heat-wave definition:** ≥ 3 consecutive days with daily maximum air temperature > 95th percentile of the 1991–2020 baseline for each elevation, measured by on-site iButton loggers.
- **Thermal tolerance:** CTmax measured using a dynamic ramping protocol (0.25 °C min⁻¹) in a programmable water bath. Insects collected at each site, acclimated for 2 h at site-specific mean temperature, then ramped until loss of righting response.
- **Fruit set:** Counted on 30 marked individuals per focal plant species per site (3 focal species per site, 12 species total; 360 marked plants across 12 focal species).

## 3. Results (Season 1, 2026)

### 3.1 Interaction data

- Total interaction events recorded: **18,742** across 4 sites and 18 weekly rounds.
- Pollinator species observed: **147**; plant species in flower: **83**.
- Unique pairwise links: **1,214**.
- Three natural heat-wave episodes occurred during the 2026 season (one in May, two in June), totalling **14 heat-wave days** across the four sites.

### 3.2 Network metrics

Network metrics were computed separately for heat-wave weeks and non-heat-wave weeks at each elevation.

| Elevation (m) | Connectance (non-HW) | Connectance (HW) | Nestedness NODF (non-HW) | Nestedness NODF (HW) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 450 | 0.148 | 0.109 | 38.2 | 29.4 |
| 950 | 0.162 | 0.131 | 41.7 | 34.5 |
| 1,450 | 0.174 | 0.158 | 44.1 | 40.8 |
| 1,900 | 0.191 | 0.183 | 47.3 | 45.6 |

- At 450 m, connectance dropped by **26% (95% CI 18–34%)** during heat waves relative to non-heat-wave weeks.
- At 1,900 m, the decline was only **4% (95% CI −2 to 10%)**, not statistically significant.
- Nestedness (NODF) declined significantly at 450 m (**−23%, 95% CI −31 to −15%**) and 950 m (**−17%, 95% CI −25 to −9%**), but not at 1,450 m or 1,900 m.

### 3.3 Pollinator visitation rates

- Overall visitation rate across all sites and weeks: **4.7 visits flower⁻¹ h⁻¹ (SE 0.3)**.
- During heat waves at 450 m, visitation rate fell to **2.1 visits flower⁻¹ h⁻¹**, a decline of **55% (95% CI 44–66%)** compared with non-heat-wave weeks at the same elevation.
- At 1,900 m, heat-wave visitation was **4.3 visits flower⁻¹ h⁻¹**, a non-significant decline of **8% (95% CI −3 to 19%)**.

### 3.4 Thermal tolerance (CTmax)

- CTmax measured for **64 pollinator species** (n = 812 individuals total; mean 12.7 individuals per species, range 5–31).
- Mean CTmax across all species: **46.8 °C (SD 3.1)**.
- Lowland (450 m) species had a mean CTmax of **47.9 °C (SD 2.4)**, but their thermal safety margin (CTmax minus maximum habitat temperature during heat waves) was only **3.2 °C (SD 1.8)**.
- Highland (1,900 m) species had a lower mean CTmax of **44.6 °C (SD 2.9)** but a thermal safety margin of **9.7 °C (SD 2.5)**.
- Thermal safety margin was the strongest single predictor of species-level visitation decline during heat waves (R² = 0.41, P < 0.001, n = 64 species).

### 3.5 Fruit set (preliminary)

- At 450 m, fruit set of the three focal species was **31% lower (95% CI 19–43%)** in plants whose main flowering period coincided with a heat wave, compared with plants flowering outside heat-wave windows.
- At 1,900 m, the difference was **7% (95% CI −4 to 18%)**, not significant.
- Full fruit-set data for all 12 focal species and 360 plants will be available after the 2027 season.

## 4. Limitations

- Only one full field season completed so far; inter-annual replication is essential and planned for 2027.
- Heat-wave episodes were natural (not experimentally imposed), so sample sizes for heat-wave weeks are small (3 episodes, 14 days). We plan to deploy open-top warming chambers at 950 m and 1,450 m in spring 2027 to supplement natural heat-wave data.
- CTmax was measured at a single ramping rate; results may differ under slower or faster rates.
- The study covers a single mountain range in south-east Spain; generalisability to other Mediterranean or temperate systems is untested.
- Each elevation is represented by a single site (one 50 × 50 m plot per elevation); spatial replication within elevations is lacking.
