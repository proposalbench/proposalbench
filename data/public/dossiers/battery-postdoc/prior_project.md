# SULVERNEX: final scientific report

**Batch-controlled measurements of resistance growth at sulfide composite cathode interfaces**

Project period: 1 October 2023–31 March 2026. Report approved on 30 April 2026; publication and archive status updated on 30 September 2026. Principal investigator: Professor Martelvia Bellorvessi, Università di Rocca Selvaronda. Experimental lead and report author: Dr Elisavra Vardessolvi.

## Funding and purpose

SULVERNEX received EUR 84,000 from the university's Materials Research Seed Fund, award URS-MAT-2023-017. Recorded expenditure was EUR 52,000 for research personnel, EUR 18,000 for consumables, EUR 10,000 for instrument access and EUR 4,000 for travel. These categories total EUR 84,000. Existing laboratory equipment and permanent staff time were institutional contributions outside this award.

The project examined whether a lithium niobate surface treatment of a nickel-rich layered oxide cathode was associated with lower resistance growth when the cathode was combined with Li6PS5Cl. The immediate objective was a controlled comparison under fixed laboratory conditions. Establishing the chemical sequence of interphase formation was outside the measurements available to the project.

## Materials and experimental design

Six independently synthesised Li6PS5Cl batches were prepared by the same mechanochemical and thermal protocol. Each batch supplied three conductivity pellets and four cycling cells. The conductivity dataset therefore contains 18 pellets. The cycling dataset contains 24 cells, with two coated-cathode and two uncoated-cathode cells from each electrolyte batch: 12 cells per condition.

The cathode active material was LiNi0.8Mn0.1Co0.1O2. A single parent powder lot was divided between the lithium niobate treatment and the uncoated control. The control underwent the same solvent exposure and thermal history without the niobium precursor. The comparison therefore controls those processing steps, although the coating preparation itself was not independently repeated across multiple cathode lots.

Cell assembly order was randomised within each electrolyte batch, and testing channels were balanced across conditions. Sample labels concealed condition during the first impedance-fitting pass. There was no preselection of cells on the basis of initial discharge capacity. All assembled cycling cells completed the specified cycling sequence and were included in the endpoint summaries.

Cells used lithium–indium negative electrodes and pressed sulfide separators. Cathode active-material loading was 2.0 ± 0.2 mg cm−2, and separator thickness was 500 ± 40 µm; these are means ± sample standard deviations across the 24 cells. Cycling was conducted at 25 °C under a nominal stack pressure of 10 MPa, between cell voltages of 2.2 and 3.6 V. After formation, the specific current was 18 mA g−1 of cathode active material, equivalent to C/10 using a reference capacity of 180 mAh g−1.

## Conductivity and capacity results

Room-temperature ionic conductivity was 3.1 ± 0.4 mS cm−1 at 25 °C. This is the mean ± sample standard deviation of the six batch means, each calculated from three pellets. Conductivity was calculated from pellet geometry and the fitted high-frequency electrolyte resistance using blocking electrodes. The 18 pellets were not treated as 18 independent synthesis replicates.

At cycle 5, discharge capacity was 154 ± 9 mAh g−1 for coated cathodes and 157 ± 10 mAh g−1 for uncoated cathodes. These descriptive statistics are means ± sample standard deviations across 12 cells per condition. Capacity was normalised to the mass of layered oxide, excluding the coating, electrolyte and conductive additive.

Capacity retention at cycle 100, relative to each cell's cycle-5 capacity, was 84.6 ± 4.3% for coated cathodes and 69.8 ± 6.1% for uncoated cathodes. The difference between condition means was 14.8 percentage points, with a batch-resampled 95% confidence interval of 9.6–20.0 percentage points. The standard deviations describe cell-to-cell variation; the confidence interval accounts for the shared electrolyte batch by resampling complete batch groups. It does not capture variation between cathode powder lots or coating-production runs.

## Resistance measurements

Impedance spectra were collected after cycles 5 and 100 at the same discharged endpoint, following a standard rest. The reported area-specific resistance is the fitted non-ohmic cell contribution after subtraction of the high-frequency electrolyte intercept. It is not a direct measurement of a chemically isolated cathode interphase.

For coated cathodes, area-specific resistance increased from 38 ± 7 Ω cm² at cycle 5 to 92 ± 18 Ω cm² at cycle 100. For uncoated cathodes, it increased from 41 ± 8 Ω cm² to 176 ± 31 Ω cm². Each value is a mean ± sample standard deviation across 12 cells. The corresponding mean increases were 54 Ω cm² and 135 Ω cm². Their difference was 81 Ω cm², with a batch-resampled 95% confidence interval of 58–104 Ω cm².

The coated condition therefore showed 60.0% less mean resistance growth under this protocol, calculated as (135 − 54)/135. This relative reduction concerns growth between the two measured endpoints, not a reduction in final resistance or a directly measured reduction in interphase thickness.

Equivalent-circuit fits used the same circuit family across conditions. The archive includes fitted parameters, residuals and alternative fits for spectra with overlapping features. Several circuit parameters were correlated. The fitted resistance contrast was more stable than the individual capacitance and dispersion terms, which were not assigned to specific reaction products.

## Structural observations and interpretation

Laboratory diffraction confirmed the dominant argyrodite phase before cell assembly. Ex situ synchrotron diffraction on selected recovered specimens provided checks for crystalline secondary phases. It did not resolve the amorphous interphase or establish when any decomposition product formed during cycling. Surface spectra were consistent with altered sulfur-containing environments after cycling, but air-free transfer and reference selection remained sources of uncertainty.

The electrochemical results support an association between cathode surface treatment and improved retention under the tested conditions. They do not establish whether chemical passivation, mechanical contact preservation or another treatment-related change dominated. Contributions from the lithium–indium electrode and composite contact network could not be uniquely separated from the measured non-ohmic resistance.

## Limitations and archived outputs

The study used one cathode powder lot, one coating preparation and a low-loading pellet-cell geometry. It did not test practical thin separators, pouch cells, lithium-metal compatibility or performance under low stack pressure. No cell-level energy-density estimate was produced. Conductivity at one temperature does not establish an activation energy or low-temperature performance.

The deposited dataset contains raw files for all 24 cycling cells and all 18 conductivity pellets, preparation logs, sample manifests, fitting scripts and the analysis environment specification. Data and metadata are released under CC BY 4.0; analysis code is released under the MIT licence. The institutional archive performs checksums and retains the raw vendor files alongside open-format exports. No patent application was filed for these results.

The main comparative manuscript remained under review on 30 September 2026. Its reported numerical results match this final report. The project award has closed, and its expenditure cannot be treated as an available balance.
