# Sensor Systems Laboratory: teaching and assessment record

**Instructor:** Tessa Maren Halwick, Alderquay University  
**Record updated:** 30 September 2026

## Course context and instruction

The undergraduate Sensor Systems Laboratory connects measurement, estimation, and security through small experiments with recorded and live sensor observations. Halwick taught the completed fall 2024 and fall 2025 offerings. Enrollment was 24 students in 2024 and 28 students in 2025, for 52 students across the two offerings. Students worked in rotating laboratory groups because research and teaching shared the six bench robots. The assessment unit was the individual student.

The fall 2026 offering began on 24 August 2026 with 26 enrolled students. Entry assessments have been administered. Consent decisions remain sealed until grades are final. Assessment collection follows the same course protocol covered by AQU-ED-2024-017; research analysis of the current cohort has not begun. The completed-cohort results below include only the 2024 and 2025 offerings.

The course has three stated learning objectives: identify assumptions in an observation model; construct a comparison that isolates an estimator change from a data change; and interpret an uncertainty interval without turning it into a claim of universal reliability. Students encounter these objectives in exercises, written explanations, and the assessment rubric.

Early exercises ask students to examine clean recordings and explain how sensor measurements differ from reference position. Later exercises introduce dropout and biased readings. Students compare two estimators on the same recording and account for differences in initialization or preprocessing. They identify the trajectory as the independent comparison unit and explain how treating every timestamp as an independent replicate affects estimated uncertainty.

## Relationship to research practice

The course uses the group's replay framework and simplified examples derived from its research workflow. Teaching recordings are kept separate from the held-out research evaluation set. The research screening threshold was selected using the project's development recordings.

Students produce a manifest recording the input recording, injected fault, estimator configuration, and output files. A second group attempts to rerun the comparison from that manifest. The submitted work includes the original analysis, the reproduction record, and a short explanation of any discrepancy. The instructor assesses whether the comparison can be reconstructed and whether the reported conclusion follows from the documented analysis.

Discussions of screened observations emphasize that rejecting a measurement may reduce one error summary while worsening another trajectory or excluding valid information. Students explain this limitation alongside their plotted results. Instruction covers empirical comparisons and statistical uncertainty.

## Learning assessment

An institutional determination dated 12 August 2024, reference AQU-ED-2024-017, classified the specified analysis of normal educational assessments as exempt educational research. Its scope includes repeated offerings of this course using the specified assessment and consent procedures. It does not authorize unrelated human-participant studies. Participation in research analysis is voluntary, and consent decisions are withheld from the instructor until course grades are finalized.

Of the 52 enrolled students in the completed offerings, 44 consented to use of their assessments: 20 from the 2024 offering and 24 from the 2025 offering. All 44 consenting students had paired entry and exit assessments. The other eight students completed ordinary course activities but were excluded from the research analysis.

The assessment used parallel forms scored with a 12-point rubric covering model assumptions, comparison design, and uncertainty interpretation. Deidentified responses were shuffled before scoring so the scorer did not see student names or whether a response was from entry or exit. The mean paired gain was 2.1 points, with a bootstrap 95% confidence interval of 1.4 to 2.8 points. Resampling used paired student records within course offering.

This is a descriptive result from consenting students in two classes. There was no randomized comparison group. Other instruction, practice effects, and maturation remain possible explanations for part of the observed change. Selective consent remains uncontrolled, and scoring judgment adds uncertainty beyond the bootstrap interval. No retention assessment or external transfer task was administered.

## Access and public engagement

Recorded-data versions of the exercises were available to students who could not attend an equipment session. Instructions included text descriptions of plots and required numerical summaries alongside color-coded graphics. These provisions are documented course practices; their effects were not separately estimated.

The department held a public robot demonstration in April 2025. Visitors watched a robot follow an indoor route and viewed its estimated position. The event record consists of the route script and display materials.
