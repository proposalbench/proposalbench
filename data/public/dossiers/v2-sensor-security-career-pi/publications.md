# Publications and contribution record

**Tessa Maren Halwick — checked 30 September 2026**

Peer-reviewed research articles: four published. Additional manuscripts under review: none. The dissertation and institutional project report are catalogued separately from journal articles. Dates below are publication dates.

## 1. Sensor corruption and residual screening

**Halwick, T. M.; Westrall, L. C. (2021). “Residual screening under structured sensor corruption.” Journal of Sensor Integrity and Estimation.**  
DOI: 10.99999/pb.v2-sensor-security-career-pi.2021.1

Published in November 2021. This article reports dissertation experiments on residual screening when the assumed observation model is violated. The evidence consists of simulation and recorded sensor-stream analyses. It distinguishes isolated outliers from sustained corruption and documents circumstances in which screening removes useful information.

Halwick designed the corruption experiments, implemented the analysis, and wrote the initial manuscript. Westrall supervised the dissertation and contributed to interpretation. The article's empirical comparisons use fixed corruption processes and describe performance conditional on those processes.

## 2. Experimental comparison of replayed faults

**Halwick, T. M.; Westrall, L. C. (2023). “Paired replay designs for comparing localization filters under observation faults.” Transactions on Experimental Robot Security.**  
DOI: 10.99999/pb.v2-sensor-security-career-pi.2023.1

Published in June 2023 during Halwick's postdoctoral appointment. The article develops an experimental workflow in which competing estimators receive identical corrupted recordings. Its contribution is control of comparison conditions and explicit identification of the experimental unit. It explains the distinction between repeated deterministic execution and independent observations.

Halwick implemented the replay workflow and performed the comparisons; Westrall contributed the study design and methodological critique. The article uses material collected during the Dunmere Crest appointment, preceding the Alderquay evaluation archive.

## 3. Completed indoor-robot evaluation

**Halwick, T. M. (2025). “Trajectory-level evaluation of residual screening for indoor robot localization.” Journal of Reproducible Autonomous Systems Experiments.**  
DOI: 10.99999/pb.v2-sensor-security-career-pi.2025.1

Published in December 2025. This sole-authored article reports the held-out replay evaluation summarized in the final project report. Its primary outcome is the median of trajectory-level median horizontal errors. The article and final report describe the same evaluation. The February 2026 repository deposit supplies the frozen data and scripts supporting the published analysis.

Halwick performed the study design, estimator development, analysis, and writing. Funding: Alderquay University Faculty Inquiry Fund, AQU-FIF-24-018. The methods section documents trajectory pairing, threshold selection on development recordings, and bootstrap resampling within robot.

## 4. Calibration across fault conditions

**Halwick, T. M.; Pelloran, J. E.; Braskett, N. C. (2026). “Residual calibration across scripted sensor-fault regimes.” Annals of Computational Fault Analysis.**  
DOI: 10.99999/pb.v2-sensor-security-career-pi.2026.1

Published in August 2026. This article reanalyzes residual behavior in the released indoor replay material. Halwick performed the data analysis and drafted the experimental sections. Jonah Emrys Pelloran, a mathematics faculty member at Alderquay University, contributed the conditional-probability formulation and critique of the calibration comparisons. Nadia Celene Braskett, a staff scientist at Calderfen National Laboratory, reviewed the fault taxonomy and security terminology.

Funding and data statement: No external funding; no Calderfen data, systems or facilities were used. The source recordings were originally collected under AQU-FIF-24-018. The reanalysis used the released archive and ordinary university computing resources.
