# Final report: NEVRAMAT — Community health worker-led screening for postpartum haemorrhage risk in rural Ghana

**Funded by:** Drelvani University PhD fellowship and supplementary fieldwork grant from the DU Global Health Research Fund.
**Period:** September 2019 – June 2022 (PhD project); fieldwork October 2020 – December 2021.
**Principal investigator / PhD candidate:** Dr. Abena Owusu-Darko
**Supervisors:** Prof. dr. Hendrel Vossenkade (DU), Dr. Comfort Asante-Mensah (Neranvi Community Health Research Institute, NCHRI)

## 1. Background

Postpartum haemorrhage (PPH) is a major preventable cause of maternal death in Ghana. In the Bono East Region, many women deliver at home or in health posts without skilled birth attendants. Community health workers (CHWs) conduct home visits during pregnancy and the early postpartum period, but their role in PPH risk screening had not been formally evaluated.

## 2. Objectives

1. Evaluate whether trained CHWs can screen for PPH risk factors (anaemia, prior PPH, grand multiparity, multiple gestation) using a simplified checklist and point-of-care haemoglobin testing.
2. Measure the effect of CHW-led screening on referral completion to higher-level facilities.
3. Explore women's and CHWs' experiences of the screening programme through qualitative methods.

## 3. Study design and setting

- **Design:** Stepped-wedge cluster-randomised trial with four sequences and five time periods (each period = 3 months, total 15 months). Twelve community health planning and services (CHPS) zones in three districts of the Bono East Region were randomised.
- **Facilities:** 12 CHPS compounds and 4 district/sub-district hospitals serving as referral points.
- **Participants:** All consenting women in their third trimester or within 48 hours postpartum, resident in the study zones.
- **Intervention:** CHWs (n = 36, 3 per zone) received a 5-day training on a simplified PPH risk checklist (8 items) and use of a portable haemoglobin photometer (HemoCue Hb 301). Women identified as high-risk were given a referral slip and a transport voucher (value GHS 30) for the nearest hospital. The 72-hour referral-completion endpoint concerns scheduled referral of clinically stable women with identified risk factors. Suspected active haemorrhage or other obstetric emergency triggered immediate emergency assessment and transfer, outside this scheduled-referral endpoint.
- **Primary outcome:** Referral completion within 72 hours among women identified as high-risk.
- **Secondary outcomes:** PPH incidence at delivery among facility deliveries; women's satisfaction.

## 4. Sample and data collection

- **Total enrolled:** 2,150 women were enrolled across all 12 CHPS zones over the 15-month trial period.
- **Intervention periods:** In the intervention periods, 1,214 women were screened by CHWs using the risk checklist and point-of-care haemoglobin testing. Of these, 327 (26.9%) were classified as high-risk by CHWs.
- **Control periods:** In the control periods, 936 women received standard care (routine antenatal and postnatal visits without the CHW screening checklist). High-risk women in control periods were identified retrospectively from routine antenatal and delivery records at the same CHPS compounds and referral hospitals; 261 women met the same risk criteria based on these records.
- **Referral completion:** 248 of 327 high-risk women completed referral within 72 hours in intervention periods (75.8%, 95% CI 71.0–80.2%). In control periods, 98 of 261 retrospectively identified high-risk women completed referral within 72 hours (37.5%, 95% CI 31.7–43.7%).
- **Adjusted effect:** In the mixed-effects logistic regression (accounting for clustering by zone and period), the intervention increased referral completion by 34.2 percentage points (95% CI 25.8–42.6 pp; p < 0.001).
- **Screening accuracy:** There were 588 paired CHW/physician assessments. The archived summary does not document selection of this subset or the reference-standard cross-tabulation, so population sensitivity and specificity cannot be established from these materials.
- **PPH incidence:** Among facility deliveries, PPH (blood loss ≥ 500 mL) occurred in 8.3% of intervention-period deliveries (n = 41/494) versus 11.7% in control-period deliveries (n = 38/325). The adjusted odds ratio was 0.68 (95% CI 0.42–1.10; p = 0.12), not statistically significant. The archived summary does not stratify the 494 and 325 facility deliveries by whether initial enrolment preceded delivery; these are descriptive facility-period rates, not an established causal effect of screening. A new analysis must restrict the risk set to women enrolled before delivery and account for selection into facility birth.
- **Qualitative component:** 42 in-depth interviews (24 women, 18 CHWs) and 6 focus group discussions (4 with women, 2 with CHWs; total 48 participants). Thematic analysis identified five themes: trust in CHWs, transport as a barrier, fear of hospital costs, empowerment through knowledge, and workload concerns among CHWs.

## 5. Key findings

1. Feasibility of CHW risk assessment was demonstrated; diagnostic accuracy requires validation in a sample with reference assessment independent of the screening result.
2. The combined intervention (screening + referral slip + transport voucher) nearly doubled referral completion (from 37.5% to 75.8%).
3. The reduction in PPH incidence (OR 0.68) was in the expected direction but the study was underpowered to detect a significant effect on this secondary outcome, and the facility-delivery denominators have not been restricted to women enrolled before delivery.
4. Qualitative findings highlighted that transport vouchers were the single most valued component; CHWs reported increased community respect but also time burden.
5. CHW attrition during the study was 11% (4 of 36 CHWs), mainly due to relocation.

## 6. Limitations

- The stepped-wedge design is susceptible to time trends; a concurrent national safe-motherhood campaign may have contributed to improved referral in later periods.
- The control-period denominator of high-risk women was identified retrospectively from routine facility records, which may undercount women who neither sought care nor delivered at a facility. This could overestimate referral completion in the control arm relative to the true population of high-risk women.
- Haemoglobin measurement was missing for 7.2% of screened women due to device malfunction or supply stock-outs.
- The study was powered for referral completion, not PPH incidence; the non-significant PPH result should be interpreted cautiously.
- Generalisability is limited to rural Bono East; urban and peri-urban settings were not included.
- No formal cost-effectiveness analysis was conducted.
- The evaluation used before-after comparisons within the stepped-wedge framework but did not employ advanced causal-inference techniques (e.g., instrumental variables, synthetic control) that could strengthen internal validity.
