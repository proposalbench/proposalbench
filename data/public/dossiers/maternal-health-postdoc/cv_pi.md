## Research profile

My research focuses on the design, implementation and evaluation of community-based maternal health interventions in sub-Saharan Africa, with emphasis on postpartum haemorrhage (PPH) prevention and early detection. I use mixed methods: facility-based cohort studies, stepped-wedge cluster-randomised designs, and qualitative approaches (in-depth interviews, focus group discussions). My PhD evaluated the NEVRAMAT programme in Ghana's Bono East Region, demonstrating that trained community health workers (CHWs) can screen for PPH risk factors and improve referral completion.

I have limited formal training in quantitative causal-inference methods (e.g., instrumental variables, regression discontinuity, difference-in-differences with staggered adoption). My statistical coursework covered regression, survival analysis and mixed-effects models, but I have no experience with advanced econometric or quasi-experimental techniques applied to health programme evaluation.
