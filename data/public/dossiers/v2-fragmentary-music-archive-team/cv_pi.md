# Curriculum vitae: Dr Maarja Sirelkoit

Updated 18 September 2026

## Current appointment

Associate Professor of Musicology, School of Cultural Evidence, Rannakaar University, Tartu, Estonia. Permanent appointment since September 2021; employment fraction 1.0 FTE. Coordinator of the university's Fragmentary Music Evidence team. My work concerns musical notation in damaged manuscripts, the evidential status of reconstructed readings, and the relationship between written musical sources and performed examples.

Rannakaar University is my employing institution and the custodian of the manuscript material used in our completed pilot. The team is an internal university group. My appointment includes research, teaching and collection-related service.

## Education and appointments

- 2005–2008: BA in Music Studies, Rannakaar University.
- 2008–2010: MA in Historical Musicology, Rannakaar University. Dissertation on attribution practices in unsigned domestic song manuscripts.
- 2010–2014: Doctoral researcher, Rannakaar University. PhD awarded June 2014 for *Reading musical remnants: evidential boundaries in incomplete household manuscripts*.
- September 2014–August 2017: Research Fellow in Manuscript Music, Rannakaar University.
- September 2017–August 2021: Lecturer in Musicology, Rannakaar University.
- September 2021–present: Associate Professor of Musicology, Rannakaar University.

## Research responsibilities

I was principal investigator of the university-funded project *Fragmentary Music Evidence Pilot*, award RU-CE-2024-07, from January 2024 to December 2025. I held responsibility for expenditure, access permissions, the classification protocol, performer recruitment and submission of the final report. The university accepted the report in February 2026 and closed the financial account in March 2026.

For that project I wrote the musicological annotation guide and served as one of the independent annotators. I also documented relationships between fragment identifiers, manuscript folders, image derivatives and interpretive statements. The final catalogue preserves the independent labels alongside any later commentary.

My current teaching covers source criticism, musical palaeography and documentation of editorial interventions. I have supervised completed master's dissertations involving manuscript descriptions and performance-based interpretation. I have not supervised a doctoral thesis to completion.

## Methods and contribution

My practical methods include close reading of notation, comparison of scribal habits, source description, structured annotation and qualitative analysis of reasons for disagreement. I use scripted catalogue checks prepared with colleagues. Historical language assessment is undertaken by our employed historical linguist; acoustic processing and numerical implementation are undertaken by our employed acoustic analyst.

The distinctive contribution of my work is the separation of what survives on the page from what an editor supplies. A legible note, an uncertain note, a missing passage and a plausible supplied passage have different statuses in our records. A performance of a supplied passage remains an interpretation, even when it sounds coherent.

## Research outputs and service

The accompanying publication record contains four published articles, including the jointly authored pilot methods and results paper published in July 2026. These publications concern manuscript evidence, linguistic attribution and documented audio processing.

I maintain the team's annotation vocabulary and chair its internal source-reading meetings. The university collection staff retain authority over handling and reproduction of originals.
