# Publications and associated research outputs

Fragmentary Music Evidence team, Rannakaar University

Record checked 18 September 2026. All four articles listed below are published. There are no submitted or under-review manuscripts in this record. Titles and identifiers are given as registered in the university's publication catalogue.

## Published articles

**Sirelkoit, M. (2018). Reading absence in domestic song manuscripts: a layered description protocol.** *Rannakaar Papers in Musical Evidence*, 3(1), 21–39. DOI: 10.99999/pb.v2-fragmentary-music-archive-team.2018.1

This single-author article separates physical description, diplomatic transcription and editorial supplementation in descriptions of incomplete musical manuscripts. Its contribution is a source-reading protocol and worked examples. It predates the pilot corpus. The protocol provided the conceptual basis for retaining visible evidence and supplied material in different catalogue fields.

**Lehevald, T., and Sirelkoit, M. (2021). Language attribution when song texts survive as isolated syllables.** *Baltic Review of Manuscript Linguistics*, 6(2), 77–96. DOI: 10.99999/pb.v2-fragmentary-music-archive-team.2021.2

This article examines the distinction between a readable character sequence and an attribution of that sequence to a language or dialect. It documents cases in which musical underlay, abbreviation and later handwriting prevent a secure attribution. The authors argue for preserving alternative readings with their evidence. Lehevald led the linguistic analysis; Sirelkoit analysed notation and placement of text beneath the musical line.

**Vahenurm, K., and Sirelkoit, M. (2024). Documenting denoising decisions in short performed manuscript examples.** *Journal of Annotated Sound Provenance*, 2(1), 11–28. DOI: 10.99999/pb.v2-fragmentary-music-archive-team.2024.3

This methods article describes retention of originals, parameter logging and reversible comparison of processed audio derivatives. It reports workflow development during the pilot's early recording work. Vahenurm implemented the processing and file-validation procedures; Sirelkoit supplied the musicological requirements and checked links to source readings. The article concerns auditable processing decisions.

**Sirelkoit, M., Lehevald, T., and Vahenurm, K. (2026). Agreement without adjudication: linked evidence for fragmentary musical sources.** *Rannakaar Papers in Musical Evidence*, 11(2), 44–68. DOI: 10.99999/pb.v2-fragmentary-music-archive-team.2026.4

Published July 2026. This is the pilot's joint results article. It reports the independent classification exercise, preservation of unresolved disagreements and linkage between source descriptions and audio derivatives. The numerical results are those in the final report; the publication is not an independent replication. Sirelkoit coordinated the study and manuscript, Lehevald co-designed and undertook annotation, and Vahenurm implemented the processing and numerical summaries. All authors reviewed the interpretation of uncertainty.

## Associated deposits

The *Fragmentary Music Evidence Pilot Catalogue*, release 1.0, is held in the Rannakaar University repository. Its public component contains catalogue metadata, independent classifications, the annotation guide and processing scripts. Its restricted component contains licensed source images, consent records and the complete audio examples.

The team's publication record establishes experience with musical source criticism, linguistic ambiguity and reproducible audio documentation.
