# Team roles and research practice

Fragmentary Music Evidence team, Rannakaar University

Statement dated 18 September 2026

## Composition and employment

The team comprises three employed researchers: Dr Maarja Sirelkoit, musicologist and coordinator; Dr Tõnis Lehevald, historical linguist; and Kadri Vahenurm, acoustic signal analyst. Sirelkoit and Lehevald each hold permanent 1.0 FTE appointments. Vahenurm holds a permanent 0.8 FTE research software and acoustics appointment. These are employment fractions across their institutional duties, not fractions already committed to another research award.

Sirelkoit is responsible for musical source criticism, interpretation of notation, coordination and accountability for research decisions. Her CV accompanies this statement. She was an annotator in the pilot.

Lehevald joined the university in September 2018 after completing a doctorate in historical linguistics at Rannakaar University in 2016. His work concerns fragmentary written Estonian, orthographic variation and language attribution from limited verbal evidence. He designed the linguistic evidence fields and was the second independent pilot annotator. His assessments record alternative readings and the limits of attribution from surviving syllables.

Vahenurm completed an MSc in acoustic signal processing at Rannakaar University in 2019, worked as a fixed-term technician in the university's media unit from 2019 to 2021, and took up her permanent research software and acoustics post in January 2022. She maintains the audio-processing scripts, checks file integrity and documents how processing choices affect derivatives. She has experience with digital filtering, spectral analysis and reproducible numerical workflows. She does not hold a doctorate.

## Collaboration and responsibilities

The university collection staff provided supervised access and digitisation services during the pilot. Repository staff administer the public and restricted deposits. These are internal service relationships. There are no external institutional partners, subcontracted research teams or signed external research collaboration agreements in the current group.

The eight performers contributed the licensed contemporary examples as participants. Their participation does not constitute an agreement to provide further recordings.

Interpretive changes require an entry explaining what evidence was reconsidered and who made the change. Original observations and independent labels remain recoverable. Vahenurm checks the technical integrity of a new deposit; Sirelkoit and Lehevald check whether its descriptions preserve the distinction between observed and inferred content.

## Established practice and limits

The team combines qualitative source reading with structured annotations and quantitative summaries. Its strongest completed evidence concerns the practicability of documenting incomplete sources and processing linked audio on ordinary desktop equipment. The unresolved classifications show that a complete catalogue record can still contain substantial interpretive uncertainty.

The existing recordings retain unprocessed references and documented licences for research transformations. The software can mix recorded audio with generated noise and retain parameters and derivative identifiers. No controlled synthetic-masking recovery study, calibrated uncertainty model or independent comparison of recovery methods has been completed.

Within the musical material, modern performances provide examples of particular readings, while the catalogue records the evidence and uncertainty behind those readings. The team retains the original audio alongside processed derivatives so that changes to acoustic features can be inspected.
