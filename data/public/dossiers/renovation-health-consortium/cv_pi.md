# Dr Néavessa Kelvarneth — curriculum vitae

**Associate Professor of Environmental Epidemiology, University of Carraventh, Ireland**  
**Record updated: 30 September 2026**

## Education and appointments

Kelvarneth completed a BSc in Environmental Science at the University of Carraventh in 2006, an MSc in Epidemiology at the same institution in 2008, and a PhD in Environmental Epidemiology in 2012. Her doctoral thesis examined the relationship between residential dampness, ventilation practices and respiratory symptoms in rented housing. The doctorate combined repeated household surveys, environmental measurements and interviews with tenants about heating and maintenance.

She held a postdoctoral appointment at the Carraventh Centre for Population Health from October 2012 to August 2016, followed by a research fellowship at the University of Carraventh from September 2016 to August 2018. She was appointed Lecturer in Environmental Epidemiology in September 2018 and Associate Professor in September 2023. She remains in that appointment and coordinates the university's Housing Exposure and Health Group.

Her research concerns the measurement of indoor exposures and their relationship to respiratory health in households with limited control over housing conditions. She has particular experience in linking dwelling surveys, continuous environmental measurements and repeated symptom questionnaires without disclosing identifiable household locations.

## Research leadership

Kelvarneth was principal investigator of CALDERVANE Ireland, the completed national cohort study described in the accompanying final report. She held responsibility for the study protocol, sponsor reporting, recruitment oversight, statistical analysis plan and integration of qualitative findings. The study was funded by the Dervaneth Housing and Health Research Fund, a national research funding programme, under grant DHHRF-2020-061. The award ran from 1 January 2021 to 31 December 2025.

Her management responsibilities included agreements with participating housing providers, procurement of monitoring equipment, supervision of field staff and coordination of the clinical referral procedure. She chaired the study management group and reported to an independent steering group with tenant, clinical and housing-maintenance representation. The university was the research sponsor and data controller; participating landlords did not receive identifiable health records.

The study involved sites in Dublin, Cork and Limerick. Its geographic coverage did not constitute a probability sample of Irish social housing. Kelvarneth has consistently described its estimates as cohort findings rather than national prevalence estimates.

## Methods and supervision

Her quantitative work includes longitudinal regression, household-clustered inference, inverse-probability weighting for attrition and sensitivity analyses for exposure misclassification. She works with building physicists on the interpretation of temperature, humidity and ventilation measurements. Her qualitative experience includes semi-structured interviews, framework analysis and mixed-method interpretation of disagreements between measured conditions and occupants' accounts.

She supervises research staff working on exposure assessment and housing-related health inequalities. Her teaching covers environmental epidemiology, causal diagrams, research ethics and the interpretation of observational intervention studies. She is not a registered medical practitioner. Clinical assessments and decisions about referral are undertaken by the consortium's respiratory clinician and research nurse.

## Scope of experience

Kelvarneth has led a national research award and coordinated scientific work across the institutions listed in the consortium record. She has not previously coordinated a Horizon Europe grant. The partner institutions have completed shared analyses and methods exchanges, but this collaboration has not held a joint European research award.

Her energy-related experience is confined to household heating practices, indoor thermal conditions and interpretation of dwelling energy records. She has no professional experience designing or operating district heating, seasonal thermal storage or energy-system dispatch models. She has not developed heat-network tariffs or undertaken regulated utility accounting. Her contribution to questions of affordability has been through household interviews, expenditure questionnaires and analysis of unequal exposure to cold and heat.
