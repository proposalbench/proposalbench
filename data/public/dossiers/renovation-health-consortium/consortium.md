# Institutional collaboration record

**Status at 30 September 2026**

The collaboration comprises five institutions established in five EU Member States: Ireland, Portugal, Slovenia, Belgium and Italy. Its working relationship developed through CALDERVANE Ireland methods exchanges, equipment support and joint publications. The Irish award holder retained responsibility for the national cohort. Inclusion in this collaboration does not mean that every institution held a share of that grant or recruited participants into the Irish study.

## University of Carraventh — Ireland

The University of Carraventh is the coordinating institution and the employer of Dr Néavessa Kelvarneth. Its Housing Exposure and Health Group combines environmental epidemiology, field monitoring and respiratory assessment. The university sponsored CALDERVANE Ireland and holds its research records. It maintains the ethics submissions, participant-contact controls and agreements governing secondary use of identifiable information.

Its building-physics work concerns occupied dwellings, ventilation behaviour and interpretation of temperature and humidity records. It has no district heating plant, thermal storage installation or energy-system simulation group within the collaborating unit. Access to social housing for past research was permission to undertake specified measurements, not operational control of the buildings or their heating equipment.

## Instituto Público de Saúde de Valdrenoura — Portugal

This public-health research institute is based in Coimbra. Its participating team is led by Dr Leovélia Matavrença, an environmental epidemiologist, with a public-health nurse and a qualitative researcher. The institute contributed questionnaire harmonisation, advice on vulnerable-participant consent and independent review of the Irish attrition analysis.

The team has conducted community respiratory surveys and evaluated communication between municipal housing services and residents. It can interpret health endpoints and differences in access to care. Its contribution to the present collaboration has involved methods and aggregate outputs; it does not hold an independently recruited renovation cohort for this collaboration. It has no utility-operation or thermal-storage engineering staff.

## Inštitut za Stavbno Okolje Veltravno — Slovenia

This building research institute is based in Maribor. Dr Tiloven Vezmarnik leads its participating building-physics group. Its work includes envelope surveys, airtightness testing, indoor moisture diagnostics and dynamic simulation of room-level thermal conditions. It checked the CALDERVANE sensor-placement protocol and reviewed the treatment of occupancy and weather in the renovation analyses.

Its laboratory supports material and room-scale building-envelope investigations. It has no seasonal storage test facility and has not validated a district heating network model. Experience with thermal behaviour of building fabric does not extend to hydraulic network design, heat-source dispatch or commercial storage operation.

## Fédération de l'Habitat Social de Rovélance — Belgium

This federation is based in Namur and is represented by housing-programme manager Maëvélise Varnelcour. Its membership consists of social housing providers. It contributed a review of tenant information materials and a workshop on maintenance reporting and ventilation handover. Its staff have experience discussing rent, service charges and energy hardship with member organisations and tenant representatives.

The federation is not itself a landlord or an energy supplier. Membership does not confer authority to commit member properties, occupant records or investment programmes to research. It neither owns nor operates a heat network. Its service-charge experience has not included designing district heating tariffs, administering heat-supply contracts or demonstrating how storage revenues are distributed to households.

## ReteSensori Valneriva Srl — Italy

This sensor-network SME is based in Perugia. Technical director Dariello Selvanicci leads its contribution. The company integrated commercial sensors with encrypted gateways, maintained device inventories and supported clock synchronisation and missing-data diagnostics during CALDERVANE Ireland. It supplied telemetry services under a university procurement contract; it was not a beneficiary of the national grant.

The company develops monitoring firmware and data-transfer software. It does not manufacture heat-pump compressors, thermal storage vessels or heat-network control equipment. Its dashboards display observations and device status; they have not controlled a live district heating system or implemented an energy-market optimisation algorithm.

## External institutional contact and collective limits

The Centro de Estudos do Ambiente Doméstico de Arvoredalva, based in Curitiba, Brazil, took part in a remote scientific seminar on household exposure questionnaires in May 2026. It is a Brazilian legal entity with no establishment in an EU Member State or Horizon Europe associated country. It is not a member of this consortium, has signed no participation agreement, contributes no committed staff or facilities and has no access to participant data. Seminar contact is the full extent of the institutional relationship recorded here.

No consortium member operates a heat network. Collectively, the participating teams have no completed work on seasonal thermal storage, low-temperature district heating integration, waste-heat recovery engineering, energy-system dispatch optimisation or heat-network tariff design. There is no contracted network operator or energy-system engineering partner. The collaboration's established strengths are exposure measurement, observational health research, building-envelope assessment, tenant engagement and sensor telemetry.
