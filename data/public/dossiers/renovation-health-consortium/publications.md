# Publications and research outputs

**Bibliographic record checked: 30 September 2026**

The following five articles are published outputs of the collaborating investigators. All were accepted and published before the record date. They are distinct publications; the methods papers and results papers reuse aspects of the same CALDERVANE Ireland cohort and should not be counted as independent cohorts. Author names are given in the order printed by the journals.

## Published articles

1. Kelvarneth, N.; Matavrença, L.; Vezmarnik, T.; Selvanicci, D. (2023). **A linked protocol for indoor exposure and respiratory symptom measurement in Irish social housing.** *Carraventh Journal of Housing Exposure Methods*. DOI: 10.99999/pb.renovation-health-consortium.2023.1

   This methods article describes the household recruitment procedure, environmental-monitoring schedule, symptom instruments and rules for separating identifiable contact records from analysis data. It reports the protocol as implemented at baseline. It contains no estimate of renovation effectiveness and does not establish recruitment beyond the completed national study.

2. Selvanicci, D.; Vezmarnik, T.; Kelvarneth, N.; Matavrença, L. (2024). **Quality assurance for distributed temperature, humidity and carbon-dioxide monitoring in occupied homes.** *Veltravno Building Measurement Practice*. DOI: 10.99999/pb.renovation-health-consortium.2024.1

   This article documents checks for clock drift, implausible values, sensor relocation and incomplete records. It distinguishes laboratory calibration from field checks and explains why room-level measurements cannot automatically be treated as personal exposure. The telemetry system described is observational. The publication contains no control strategy for heating networks or storage systems.

3. Kelvarneth, N.; Matavrença, L.; Fenrathen, C.; Vezmarnik, T. (2025). **Visible mould and recent wheeze in a social housing cohort: a household-clustered analysis.** *Valdrenoura Residential Environment and Health*. DOI: 10.99999/pb.renovation-health-consortium.2025.1

   This baseline analysis reports the association between inspector-recorded visible mould and recent wheeze. Its adjustment set and sensitivity analyses are described in the article. The odds ratio is an observational association rather than a causal estimate, and the study does not identify fungal species or assess a medicinal intervention.

4. Kelvarneth, N.; Vezmarnik, T.; Matavrença, L.; Fenrathen, C.; Selvanicci, D. (2026). **Indoor conditions and respiratory symptoms before and after deep renovation of social housing.** *Rovélance Journal of Renovation and Population Health*. DOI: 10.99999/pb.renovation-health-consortium.2026.1

   This longitudinal article reports the retained cohort, renovation comparison and adjusted differences in changes. It includes the uncertainty interval spanning no difference for wheeze. The article also reports the overheating analysis, whose complete-case population differs from that of the main renovation comparison. Neither analysis supplies evidence about district heating or seasonal storage performance.

5. Matavrença, L.; Varnelcour, M.; Kelvarneth, N. (2026). **Ventilation handover, maintenance reporting and household constraints after housing renovation.** *Arvoredalva Journal of Housing Services and Public Health*. DOI: 10.99999/pb.renovation-health-consortium.2026.2

   This qualitative article draws on occupant and housing-staff interviews from CALDERVANE Ireland. It examines how instructions, maintenance processes and household constraints influenced reported use of ventilation. Interview themes are presented with their sampling context; they are not estimates of the prevalence of particular behaviours among all social housing residents.

## Other completed outputs

The accepted funder report, exposure-quality manual, participant results leaflet and maintenance briefing are institutional outputs without DOIs. They are not additional peer-reviewed publications. The institutional archive holds the approved analysis scripts and a data dictionary. Individual-level health data are available only through controlled access; no article authorises unrestricted redistribution of those records.

No publication in this record reports results from the separately approved cohort extension. That extension had not recruited participants by the record date.
