## Published articles

**Pellavreth, A.; Norravick, C.; Ellsvareth, N.; Arvendrel, L.; Brindelvane, T. (2022).** Pharmacist-led blood-pressure management in rural primary care: protocol for the V-RMAPS cluster-randomized trial. *Veylorn Journal of Rural Cardiovascular Practice*, 7(2), 81–93. DOI: 10.99999/pb.rural-hypertension-group.2022.1

This protocol describes clinic allocation, adult eligibility, pharmacist responsibilities, the standardized blood-pressure endpoint, and the original analysis specification. It contains no outcome results. The participant age range begins at 30 years. Its description of community health worker involvement concerns appointment support and contact through adult clinics, not recruitment through educational institutions.

**Brindelvane, T.; Densavren, R.; Ellsvareth, N.; Pellavreth, A. (2024).** Medication access and follow-up in rural hypertension care: interviews with patients, clinic staff, and community health workers. *Ordelvane Community Care Methods*, 11(3), 144–158. DOI: 10.99999/pb.rural-hypertension-group.2024.1

This article reports the interview component of V-RMAPS. It includes the sampling rationale, coding process, and accounts of transport, cost uncertainty, telephone access, and medication reconciliation. The findings describe experiences among purposively selected interview participants. The article does not estimate the proportion of all clinic patients who encounter each barrier.

**Pellavreth, A.; Arvendrel, L.; Norravick, C.; Ellsvareth, N.; Brindelvane, T.; Densavren, R. (2025).** Six-month blood-pressure outcomes of pharmacist-led management in eight rural clinics: the V-RMAPS trial. *Veylorn Journal of Rural Cardiovascular Practice*, 10(1), 15–30. DOI: 10.99999/pb.rural-hypertension-group.2025.1

This is the primary outcome article. It reports the adjusted between-arm systolic blood-pressure difference, participant retention, and missing-data sensitivity analysis. The primary adjusted contrast was −6.0 mmHg (95% CI −9.8 to −2.2) among the 408 participants with observed endpoints, distinct from within-arm descriptive changes. The four deaths were treated as terminal events without assigned or imputed six-month blood-pressure values. Multiple imputation covered the 68 missing measurements among the 476 participants alive at six months, yielding a survivor-conditional adjusted contrast of −5.5 mmHg (95% CI −9.6 to −1.4) under an observed-variable missingness assumption among survivors. Both analyses may be affected by post-randomization survival and attendance selection and do not estimate a treatment effect for all 480 enrollees. Follow-up ends at six months; it does not establish durability after intervention completion or effects on cardiovascular events.

**Ellsvareth, N.; Arvendrel, L.; Pellavreth, A.; Densavren, R. (2026).** Reconciling research and electronic clinical records in a rural hypertension cohort. *Keldavren Health Services Measurement and Linkage*, 4(2), 62–76. DOI: 10.99999/pb.rural-hypertension-group.2026.1

This methods article describes verified record matches, unresolved identifiers, and differences between routine clinical readings and standardized research measurements. Its linkage success denominator is the enrolled cohort, not only participants retained at the endpoint visit. Successful linkage is not treated as evidence of complete longitudinal data. The paper documents the approved matching procedure without releasing direct patient identifiers.
