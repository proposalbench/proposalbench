## Education and appointments

Dr. Pellavreth received a BS in statistics from Keldavren College in 1996, an MPH in epidemiology from Ordelvane Institute of Public Health in 1998, and a PhD in epidemiology from the same institution in 2003. Her dissertation examined differences between routinely recorded and standardized blood-pressure measurements in adult outpatient care. She completed a postdoctoral appointment in health services research at Veylorn Ridge Academic Health Center from 2003 to 2005.

Her appointments at Veylorn Ridge have been Assistant Professor, 2005–2011; Associate Professor, 2011–2018; and Professor, 2018–present. She has directed the Rural Cardiovascular Research Unit since 2019. Veylorn Ridge is an academic health center with outpatient services, graduate research training, and an institutional review board. It is organizationally separate from the community clinic network described in this dossier.

## Research experience

Dr. Pellavreth studies hypertension care in rural adult populations, with particular attention to measurement conditions, medication access, follow-up, and the interpretation of routine clinical records. Her work combines clinic-based quantitative studies with interviews conducted by community health workers and qualitative researchers. She has served as principal investigator on pragmatic intervention research and as an epidemiologic collaborator on observational analyses of adult primary care.

She was principal investigator of the Veylorn Rural Medication and Pressure Study, abbreviated V-RMAPS. This completed study examined pharmacist-led management in rural primary-care clinics. She was responsible for the protocol, allocation procedures, analysis oversight, research governance, and final reporting. A licensed pharmacist directed medication management; Dr. Pellavreth did not prescribe or make individual treatment decisions. Her main analytic responsibilities included defining the blood-pressure endpoint, assessing differential follow-up, and interpreting the limitations of a small number of randomized clinics.

Her qualitative experience includes developing interview guides with community health workers, reviewing coding frameworks, and integrating interview findings with clinic-level implementation records. She is not the team's lead qualitative interviewer. That responsibility belongs to Dr. Tessavelle Brindelvane, whose training and work are described in the team record.
