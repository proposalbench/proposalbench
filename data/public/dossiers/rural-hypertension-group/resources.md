# Existing facilities, equipment, and data holdings

Veylorn Ridge Academic Health Center, Rural Cardiovascular Research Unit  
Inventory and access statement: 30 September 2026

## Measurement equipment and space

The unit owns 24 automated upper-arm blood-pressure monitors of a model independently validated for adult clinical measurement. Sixteen are assigned to clinic measurement rooms, with two at each participating clinic, and eight are stored centrally for training and replacement. The inventory includes adult cuffs covering arm circumferences from 22 to 52 cm. Equipment logs record cuff condition, device checks, and removal from use when readings or physical inspection indicate a problem. Adult validation and cuff availability are not documented as pediatric validation.

All 24 monitors remain in the inventory after V-RMAPS closeout. They are spot-measurement instruments, not ambulatory blood-pressure monitors. Their readings do not measure sleep, physical activity, or circadian phase. Research assistants have a written seated blood-pressure procedure and competency checklist used during the completed study. Community readings require documentation of location, posture, cuff size, and recent activity because these conditions are not uniformly controlled outside clinic rooms.

The health center provides secure interview rooms, an encrypted audio recorder, transcription software, and a restricted-access analysis environment. Clinic rooms belong to Celdavreth Trace and remain subject to clinical scheduling. Their use during the completed trial does not reserve them for other research activities.

The unit owns no actigraphs, polysomnography equipment, sleep-specific wearable devices, or ambulatory blood-pressure monitors. No such equipment is on order. There is no sleep laboratory within the unit, and no signed access agreement with an external sleep laboratory. The equipment inventory contains no instruments represented as already available for objective sleep assessment.

## V-RMAPS data archive

The retained archive contains 480 baseline participant records, 408 standardized six-month blood-pressure endpoints, and verified electronic record links for 456 participants. These counts refer to overlapping groups rather than additive samples. A participant can have a verified clinical record link while lacking the standardized endpoint. The archive retains flags distinguishing research measurements from routine readings and documents the reasons for unresolved matches.

Available baseline fields include age, sex recorded in the clinical record, hypertension treatment, standardized blood pressure, and reported difficulty attending appointments. Routine records contain encounter dates, medication lists, and clinical blood-pressure entries with variable completeness. The archive also includes the interview coding framework, redacted interview transcripts, workflow observation sheets, the analysis specification, and executable analysis scripts. Interview transcripts are not treated as anonymous merely because names have been removed; accounts of local services can still be identifying.

No participant in the archived cohort was younger than 30 at enrollment. The archive contains no adolescent observations, no participants aged 18–29, and no school-level information. It has no objective sleep records, sleep diaries, sleep timing variables, sleep-duration variables, or sleep-regularity measures. Encounter timestamps describe healthcare contacts and have not been validated as proxies for sleep or circadian behavior. There are no retained research biospecimens or standardized research lipid or glycemic measurements.

## Access, permissions, and present status

The clinical record custodian is Celdavreth Trace Community Clinics. Veylorn Ridge maintains the research copy under the completed study's data-use agreement and institutional review board approval. As of 30 September 2026, that agreement and approval remain applicable to record retention and approved secondary analyses; they do not authorize resumed enrollment or intervention delivery. The linkage key is held separately from the analysis files. Approved unit analysts have access through named accounts; the community health worker network has no general access to research analysis files.

The original consent covered the completed trial, its specified interviews, record linkage, and approved secondary analyses of hypertension care. It did not authorize unrestricted recontact, school recruitment, or collection of additional physiological measurements. New participant contact and new data collection require their own institutional determination and applicable permissions. The archived dataset is controlled access and is not an openly downloadable resource.

The original study is closed to enrollment and intervention delivery. Retained records remain subject to the approved retention schedule, access controls, and disclosure review. Existing institutional computing support covers storage and maintenance of the completed archive. The closed foundation award provides no continuing funds, and clinic partnership status does not transfer ownership of clinical records to the research unit.
