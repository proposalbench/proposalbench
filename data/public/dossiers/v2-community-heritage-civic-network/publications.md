# Selected Publications
**Date: 30 September 2026**

**1. Morante, L., & Ruggianese, V. (2026). "From Melody to Mandate: Adapting Cultural Facilitation for Service-Access Deliberation."**
*Journal of Civic Heritage*, 14(2), 112-130.
DOI: 10.99999/pb.v2-community-heritage-civic-network.2026.1
*Abstract:* This paper examines the methodological transition from informal cultural gatherings to structured democratic deliberation. Drawing on 48 snowball-recruited interviews and facilitators' qualitative observations during the 2025 listening workshops in Palermo, we report that musical heritage sharing helped establish initial rapport with residents facing severe language and migration-related obstacles. While the exploratory design precludes causal claims about lowering participation barriers, the study details the process of translating qualitative community narratives into 120 coded recommendations, highlighting the necessity of integrating public-service specialists to bridge the gap between cultural facilitation and formal public administration.

**2. Voulgaris, Y., & Morante, L. (2025). "Acoustic Agoras: Shared Cultural Spaces as Venues for Democratic Inclusion."**
*Qualitative Sociology of Music*, 9(4), 45-62.
DOI: 10.99999/pb.v2-community-heritage-civic-network.2025.1
*Abstract:* Shared cultural spaces often serve as informal sanctuaries for marginalized communities. This research explores how these spaces can be activated as "acoustic agoras"—venues where participatory interpretation of local heritage fosters civic identity. By comparing case studies from Athens and Palermo, the authors argue that attendance at performances alone is insufficient for democratic inclusion; rather, residents must be actively involved in shaping the cultural narrative, which in turn empowers them to influence local public-service decisions.

**3. Carballo, I., Olaberdiza, J., & Morante, L. (2024). "Measuring Municipal Uptake: The Efficacy of Community-Led Dossiers in Southern Europe."**
*Public Administration and Community Dialogue*, 22(1), 78-95.
DOI: 10.99999/pb.v2-community-heritage-civic-network.2024.1
*Abstract:* How do local authorities respond to demands generated outside formal democratic channels? This article analyzes the reception of community-led dossiers by municipal governments in Spain and Italy. We track the trajectory of civic recommendations generated by excluded residents, finding that while comprehensive uptake is rare, targeted recommendations regarding language services and housing often receive formal written responses. The paper emphasizes the importance of documenting these responses to inform broader EU policymaking on inclusion.

**4. Morante, L. (2023). "The Ethics of Voice: Data Protection and Vulnerability in Migration Sociology."**
*Mediterranean Migration Studies*, 11(3), 201-218.
DOI: 10.99999/pb.v2-community-heritage-civic-network.2023.1
*Abstract:* Conducting qualitative research with residents possessing precarious migration statuses requires stringent ethical safeguards. This paper discusses the complexities of managing audio archives of spoken deliberation. It argues against the reliance on cloud-based transcription services, advocating instead for local, offline processing to ensure absolute data sovereignty. The author presents a framework for contributor agreements that permit internal noncommercial analysis while strictly forbidding third-party uploads, ensuring that public outputs rely solely on authorised aggregate themes.

**5. Samaras, E., & Voulgaris, Y. (2022). "Harmonizing the Margins: Ethnomusicology and Civic Action in Athens."**
*Journal of Civic Heritage*, 10(1), 33-50.
DOI: 10.99999/pb.v2-community-heritage-civic-network.2022.1
*Abstract:* This article documents the work of Koinonia Politismos in utilizing ethnomusicological methods to stimulate civic action among migrant populations in Athens. By analyzing three years of participatory music workshops, the authors illustrate how shared musical practices create a foundation of trust that enables subsequent discussions on municipal service access and social inclusion.
