# GUICHETS: Annual report 2025 (programme year 2)

**Resident deliberation on access to public services in Liège, Roubaix and Porto**

CERV grant agreement 101143871, project period 1 January 2024 – 30 June 2026.  
Reporting period: 1 January – 31 December 2025.  
Coordinator: Seuil Commun asbl (BE). Beneficiaries: Les Guichets Ouverts (FR), Associação Rede Balcão Aberto (PT).

## 1. Summary

In 2025 the consortium ran eight deliberation sessions: three in Liège, three in Roubaix and two in Porto. 84 unique residents took part. The sessions produced 41 recommendations addressed to 9 public bodies. By 31 January 2026, written responses had been received for 33 of 41 (80%).

## 2. Objectives

1. Enable residents facing language and migration-related barriers to deliberate on how local public services treat them.
2. Obtain and document written responses from the authorities concerned and return them to residents.
3. Test whether interpreted deliberation improves residents' knowledge of complaint routes.

## 3. Methods

- **Recruitment.** Residents were recruited through the partners' advice desks, language classes, community and religious associations, and sports clubs, with outreach material in 11 languages. Eligibility: living in the municipality and facing language or migration-related barriers in using public services. Sessions were capped at 30 residents.
- **Format.** Three-hour sessions on Saturdays or weekday evenings. An official from the service concerned presented the procedure (30 minutes, interpreted). Residents then deliberated at language tables, each led by a facilitator and an interpreter. Recommendations were agreed in plenary and sent in writing within 10 working days.
- **Accessibility.** Whispered and consecutive interpretation in 11 languages: Arabic, Dari, Pashto, Tigrinya, Ukrainian, Russian, Romanian, Turkish, Kriol (Guinea-Bissau), Bengali and Nepali. Childcare was provided at 7 of 8 sessions, with transport tickets and easy-read summaries in French and Portuguese.
- **Response tracking.** Each recommendation was logged. Authorities were asked to reply in writing within 60 days, and replies were coded as accepted, partly accepted or declined. Replies were presented to residents at feedback meetings.
- **Evaluation.**
  - Exit questionnaire: 7 agree/disagree items, anonymous, handed to every resident present at the end of each session. Residents who attended more than one session were invited to complete it each time. Forms carry no identifier, so n counts forms, not distinct respondents.
  - Knowledge cards on complaint routes (5-point scale): a pre card at the start and a post card at the end of a session, matched within the session by a number printed on both cards. Residents who attended more than one session completed new cards each time, so n counts session-level pairs.
  - Proportions are reported with Wilson score 95% intervals and the mean change with a paired t interval. Both treat forms or pairs as independent observations and are not adjusted for repeat respondents.
  - 14 semi-structured interviews with officials of the bodies addressed; facilitator observation notes after each session.
- **Safeguarding.** Participant protocol version 2 (January 2025): written participation consent, no audio or video recording, a safeguarding lead at each site, and separation of deliberation from individual casework. Facilitators referred 17 individual situations raised in sessions to the partners' advice desks; none was handled in session.

## 5. Results

**Recommendations and responses.** Of 41 recommendations, 12 were accepted, 14 partly accepted and 7 declined; 8 received no response. Median time to a written response was 47 days (interquartile range 29–71). Examples: following L1, the CPAS added an interpreter-booking line to its appointment letters; following P1, the local health unit published a registration checklist in four languages; following R3, the CCAS introduced written acknowledgement of complaints within 15 days. Responses were returned to residents at five feedback meetings held between December 2025 and January 2026.

**Exit questionnaire (61 forms).**
- 74% (45 of 61; 95% CI 62–83%) agreed with the statement 'I could express my views in a language I am comfortable with'.
- 66% (40 of 61; 95% CI 53–76%) agreed with 'Our recommendations will reach the authority concerned'.
- 39% (24 of 61; 95% CI 28–52%) agreed with 'I expect the authority to change something'.

**Knowledge of complaint routes (48 session-level pairs).** The mean score rose from 2.3 to 3.6; mean difference 1.3 (SD 1.0; 95% CI 1.0–1.6).

**Officials (n = 14).** Ten of 14 considered the recommendations specific enough to act on. Five reported that their service had an internal procedure for answering collective recommendations.

**Case stories.** 19 case stories were collected in 2025 under internal-use consent. They were used for facilitator training and internal analysis.

**Dissemination.** The policy brief *Interpreted access to public-service complaint procedures* (December 2025) was sent to the municipal and national authorities concerned in Belgium, France and Portugal, and presented at the READ network meeting in Brussels on 4 December 2025. No contribution was made to an EU consultation or EU institution in 2025.

## 6. Limitations

- There was no comparison group, so changes in services cannot be attributed to the sessions alone.
- Questionnaires and cards were anonymous and could not be linked to attendance; respondents self-selected.
- The 61 forms and 48 card pairs may include more than one response from the same resident; the number of distinct respondents is unknown. The intervals are unadjusted and should be read as exploratory.
- Responses are as reported by the authorities. Implementation was checked on site for 3 recommendations only.
- Facilitators observed under-representation of residents under 25 and of residents working evening shifts.
