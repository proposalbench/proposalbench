**Internal Status Report and Resubmission Preparations**
**Project:** Stress, Attention, and Medication Routines (R21 Resubmission)
**Date:** 28 September 2026
**Prepared by:** Elias V. Thorne, PI

This report outlines the changes and preliminary work completed since receiving the summary statement for our R21 A0 submission in June 2026. We are preparing for an A1 resubmission and have systematically addressed the major criticisms raised by the review panel.

**1. Attention-Task Reliability**
*Criticism:* The reliability of the mobile attention task in this specific clinical population is untested.
*Action Taken:* To establish the psychometric properties of our custom mCPT, we conducted a separate 24-adult task-retest pilot study in August 2026. Participants were recruited from the Oakhaven clinic and completed the mCPT twice, two weeks apart. 
*Result:* The pilot estimates the mCPT test-retest reliability at an ICC of 0.62 (95% CI 0.37 to 0.79; two-way mixed-effects, absolute agreement, single measurement) based on 24 complete pairs. This provides preliminary retest evidence, but we acknowledge it does not resolve the limitation regarding reliable measurement of within-person attention fluctuations required for mediation. A formal measurement-error analysis is planned to address this.

**2. Confounding Variables**
*Criticism:* The study fails to measure and account for potential confounding by medication characteristics and baseline severity.
*Action Taken:* We have amended the study protocol. Medication coding (categorizing by class and dosing frequency), a psychological baseline-severity instrument (the Brief Symptom Inventory, BSI), and a medical comorbidity index (Charlson Comorbidity Index) have been added to the protocol. Medication class and baseline severity will be included as covariates in planned multilevel mixed-effects models. While these additions improve adjustment, we acknowledge that residual medical-severity confounding remains a limitation of the observational design.

**3. Recruitment and Retention**
*Criticism:* Recruitment from one clinic lacked a feasible retention plan.
*Action Taken:* We have developed a comprehensive retention strategy. Reminder scripts (delivered via SMS and phone calls by the research nurse) and a $50 travel reimbursement per in-person visit are planned. We acknowledge in the revised approach that these retention improvements have not been tested in our specific population yet, but they align with best practices for longitudinal observational research.

**4. Causal Inference**
*Criticism:* The causal language exceeded an observational design.
*Action Taken:* We have completely revised the narrative to remove deterministic causal language. The revised DAG distinguishes association from mediation assumptions. We explicitly state the assumptions required for causal inference in an observational design. Furthermore, sensitivity analyses are planned to quantify the potential impact of unmeasured confounding.

**5. Missing Data and Power**
*Criticism:* The statistical power analysis does not adequately account for the expected missing data inherent in a 6-month EMA study.
*Action Taken:* A revised power simulation incorporating realistic missing data patterns is planned but not yet completed.

**6. Adherence Measurement Bias**
*Criticism:* The reliance on self-report for medication adherence remains susceptible to social desirability bias.
*Action Taken:* This criticism remains unresolved. We will rely on daily self-reported adherence and monthly aggregate corroboration via in-person pill counts. We acknowledge that monthly pill counts cannot independently verify daily doses, and self-report remains susceptible to bias.

**Current Status of Cohort and Clinic Access**
- **Cohort Status:** The proposed 80-person cohort has zero recruited participants. Recruitment will be entirely prospective upon funding. The 24 adults from the reliability pilot will not be included in the main cohort.
- **Clinic Access and Linkage:** Our existing clinic access covers adult observational work. However, we discovered that electronic health record linkage requires a new consent form; the currently approved IRB form does not authorise pharmacy-record linkage. Given the timeline, a no-linkage observational alternative relying on self-report and pill counts is feasible.
- **Care-Transition Subgroup:** To align with emerging funding priorities, the clinic maintains an existing registry of patients recently discharged from inpatient care. Planned sampling will focus on the post-discharge subgroup for the care-transition emphasis. This allows us to retain the original research question regarding stress and medication routines while specifically observing patients navigating the vulnerable post-discharge period.
