**Title:** Stress, Attention, and Medication Routines in Adult Outpatient Follow-up
**Principal Investigator:** Elias V. Thorne, Ph.D.
**Submission Date:** February 16, 2026
**Application Type:** NIH R21 (A0)

## SPECIFIC AIMS

Medication nonadherence is a pervasive problem in adult outpatient care, leading to poor clinical outcomes and increased healthcare utilization. While chronic stress is a known risk factor for nonadherence, the daily, dynamic mechanisms linking transient stress to missed medication doses remain poorly understood. Traditional models often rely on retrospective self-reports, which fail to capture the real-time cognitive fluctuations that occur during stressful episodes. We hypothesize that acute daily stress depletes attentional capacity, which in turn disrupts the execution of established medication routines. 

To test this hypothesis, we propose an intensive longitudinal observational study utilizing Ecological Momentary Assessment (EMA) and mobile cognitive testing. We will recruit 80 adults from the Oakhaven Medical Center Outpatient Clinic and follow them for 6 months. By capturing daily variations in stress, attention, and adherence, this study will establish the causal effect of daily stress on medication adherence and identify the cognitive pathways involved.

**Aim 1: Quantify the association between daily stress and medication adherence during a 6-month outpatient follow-up.**
We will use EMA to measure daily stress levels and daily self-reports combined with monthly pill counts to track medication adherence. We will determine the causal impact of stress on adherence, hypothesizing that days characterized by higher-than-average stress will be significantly associated with missed medication doses.

**Aim 2: Determine if attention capacity mediates the stress-adherence relationship.**
Participants will complete a custom mobile Continuous Performance Test (mCPT) on their smartphones to assess sustained attention during high- and low-stress periods. We hypothesize that acute stress will cause a measurable deficit in mCPT performance, and that this attentional deficit will fully mediate the causal pathway between daily stress and medication nonadherence.

## RESEARCH STRATEGY

### A. Significance
Medication adherence is critical for the management of chronic conditions in outpatient settings. Approximately 50% of patients do not take their medications as prescribed. Nonadherence is a complex behavior influenced by patient, provider, and systemic factors. Among patient-level factors, psychological stress is consistently cited as a primary barrier. However, the mechanisms by which stress disrupts adherence are not fully elucidated. 

Current literature suggests that stress impairs executive functioning, particularly sustained attention and working memory. Managing a daily medication routine requires prospective memory and attentional control—cognitive resources that are easily depleted by acute stressors. If attention mediates the stress-adherence relationship, interventions could be designed to either bolster attentional capacity or simplify medication routines during periods of high stress. This project addresses a critical gap by measuring these variables in real-time, naturalistic settings.

### B. Innovation
This study is innovative in its use of high-frequency, mobile-based cognitive testing alongside EMA. While EMA has been used to track stress and mood, the integration of a mobile Continuous Performance Test (mCPT) allows for the objective measurement of attentional capacity in the patient's natural environment. This approach moves beyond static, in-lab neuropsychological assessments, providing a dynamic picture of cognitive functioning as it fluctuates with daily stress. Furthermore, the intensive 6-month follow-up period provides unprecedented longitudinal data on the stability of medication routines over time.

### C. Approach

**Participants and Setting**
We will recruit 80 adults receiving care at the Oakhaven Medical Center Outpatient Clinic. Inclusion criteria: (1) age 18-65; (2) prescribed at least one daily oral medication for a chronic condition; (3) own a smartphone compatible with the study application. Exclusion criteria: (1) diagnosed cognitive impairment (e.g., dementia); (2) non-English speaking. Recruitment will occur during routine clinic visits, facilitated by the clinical research nurse.

**Procedures**
Following informed consent, participants will complete a baseline assessment capturing demographic information and clinical history. They will then install the study application on their smartphones. The observation period will last for 6 months. 
- **EMA Protocol:** Participants will receive two prompts per day (randomly scheduled within morning and evening windows) to report their current stress levels.
- **Mobile Cognitive Testing:** Twice a week, immediately following an EMA prompt, participants will be prompted to complete the 3-minute mCPT.
- **Adherence Tracking:** Participants will log their daily medication intake in the app. Additionally, they will return to the clinic monthly for a pill count conducted by the research nurse.

**Measures**
- *Daily Stress:* Measured using a validated 4-item EMA stress scale (e.g., "Right now, I feel overwhelmed").
- *Attention:* Measured using the mCPT. The task requires participants to tap the screen when a target letter appears and withhold tapping for non-targets. The primary metric is the d-prime (d') sensitivity index.
- *Medication Adherence:* Calculated as the percentage of prescribed doses taken, derived from a combination of daily self-report and monthly pill counts.

**Statistical Analysis**
Data will be analyzed using multilevel mixed-effects models to account for the nested structure of the data (days nested within individuals). 
- For Aim 1, we will model daily adherence (binary: missed vs. taken) as a function of daily stress, controlling for time and weekend effects. 
- For Aim 2, we will conduct a multilevel mediation analysis. We will test the a-path (stress predicting mCPT performance), the b-path (mCPT performance predicting adherence), and the c'-path (direct effect of stress on adherence controlling for mCPT). 
We anticipate that the 80-person cohort, providing up to 180 days of data each, will yield sufficient power (>0.80) to detect small-to-medium causal effects. 

**Potential Pitfalls and Alternative Strategies**
Participant burden is a potential issue with a 6-month EMA protocol. To mitigate this, the mCPT is kept brief (3 minutes) and is only administered twice weekly. If compliance drops below 70%, we will reduce the EMA frequency to once daily.
