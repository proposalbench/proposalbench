**RESUME AND SUMMARY OF DISCUSSION:**
This R21 application proposes an intensive longitudinal observational study to examine the relationship between daily stress, attentional capacity, and medication adherence in 80 adult outpatients over a 6-month period. The panel noted that the research question is highly significant, as medication nonadherence remains a major clinical challenge, and the use of mobile cognitive testing (mCPT) alongside Ecological Momentary Assessment (EMA) is an innovative approach to capturing real-time data. The Principal Investigator has a solid track record in EMA methodologies. 

However, enthusiasm was significantly dampened by several major methodological and conceptual flaws. First, the reliability of the mobile attention task in this specific clinical population is untested, raising concerns about the validity of the mediation analysis. Second, the study fails to measure and account for potential confounding by medication characteristics and baseline severity. Third, recruitment from one clinic lacked a feasible retention plan for the demanding 6-month follow-up. Finally, the causal language exceeded an observational design, as the study cannot definitively establish causality without experimental manipulation. Overall, while the premise is intriguing, the application requires substantial revision to address these critical weaknesses.

**Overall Impact:** The project addresses an important clinical issue using novel mobile health tools. The PI is well-qualified. However, the approach has significant gaps that threaten the interpretability of the data.

**Factor 1: Importance of the Research:**
*Strengths:*
- The focus on real-time cognitive fluctuations as a mechanism for nonadherence is a fresh perspective.
- The theoretical model linking stress, attention, and routine execution is well-supported by background literature.
*Weaknesses:*
- The causal language used throughout the proposal exceeded what can be inferred from an observational design. The PI repeatedly states the study will "establish the causal effect" and "determine the causal impact." As an observational study, unmeasured confounding cannot be ruled out, and such deterministic causal claims are inappropriate.

**Factor 2: Rigor and Feasibility:**
*Strengths:*
- The integration of objective cognitive testing with self-reported stress is highly innovative.
*Weaknesses:*
- The reliability of the mobile attention task in this specific clinical population is untested. Without established test-retest reliability for the mCPT in this cohort, the mediation analysis (Aim 2) rests on shaky ground.
- The study fails to measure and account for potential confounding by medication characteristics and baseline severity. Different medications have different side effect profiles and dosing complexities, and baseline psychiatric or medical severity likely drives both stress and adherence.

**Expertise and Resources:**
*Comments:*
- The PI has demonstrated expertise in deploying EMA protocols. The clinical environment at Oakhaven Medical Center is appropriate for the study.

**Factor 2: Rigor and Feasibility:**
*Strengths:*
- The 6-month follow-up period provides unprecedented longitudinal data on the stability of medication routines over time.
*Weaknesses:*
- Recruitment from one clinic lacked a feasible retention plan. Expecting 80 outpatients to complete daily EMA and twice-weekly cognitive tests for 6 months with only monthly clinic visits for pill counts is unrealistic. Attrition is likely to be high, and no specific retention strategies are proposed.
- The statistical power analysis does not adequately account for the expected missing data inherent in a 6-month EMA study.
- The reliance on self-report for medication adherence, even when supplemented by monthly pill counts, remains susceptible to social desirability bias.
