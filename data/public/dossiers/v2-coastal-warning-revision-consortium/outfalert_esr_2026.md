The proposal passed all thresholds. It was ranked below the budget available for the topic and is not proposed for funding.

## Criterion 1: Excellence (score 3.50)

The objectives are clear, pertinent to the topic and include a measurable skill target. The preliminary data from 142 paired samples are credible, and combining fluorescence and enzymatic signals with hydrodynamic travel times is a sound approach that goes beyond current practice for overflow-affected beaches. Chemistry, oceanography and planning are integrated.

The following shortcomings are identified:

- The calibration of the faecal-contamination proxy rests entirely on samples from one estuary (Corrib). The proposal applies the same coefficients unchanged at the Charente sites and plans only 30 verification samples in year 2. Given the known dependence of fluorescence and enzymatic signals on salinity and organic-matter background, transferability is assumed rather than tested, and the verification set is too small to estimate performance with useful precision.
- Warning thresholds are set at a fixed predicted probability of 0.5 without justification. The proposal does not identify who would act on a warning, what action would follow, or how the consequences of false alarms and missed events would determine the threshold. A decision rule for users is lacking.
- Open science practices are described in general terms. Preregistration of the verification analysis is a positive element, but beyond a data management plan announced for month 6 the proposal gives no concrete approach to managing and sharing research outputs.

## Criterion 2: Impact (score 3.00)

The pathways to impact are relevant to the implementation of the Bathing Water Directive. Target groups (local authorities, wastewater operators, bathers) are identified, and communication measures are adequate.

The following shortcomings are identified:

- The headline impact of about 3,400 avoided bather exposures per season is given as a single value. It does not account for proxy misclassification, uncertainty in travel times or in beach attendance, and no range is given. The scale and significance of the expected impacts are therefore not credibly substantiated.
- The dissemination plan commits to publishing all project data, including overflow telemetry, radar inputs and survey data, under an open licence, while the same section states that these data are held under data-sharing agreements and commercial licences. The open-data commitment is not credible as written, and the data management approach is insufficiently thought through.
- The replication target of 20 sites by 2030 is not substantiated, and no end-user organisation has committed to the project.

## Criterion 3: Quality and efficiency of the implementation (score 4.00)

The work plan is coherent, with clear lead responsibilities. The partners are complementary, and CICS and URC have collaborated before. Resources are appropriate overall.

The following shortcomings are identified:

- The risk table is thin. Event-triggered sampling is proposed against a shortage of exceedance events, but the Charente verification relies on 30 samples collected in a single season; if heavy-rainfall events are few in that season, the proposal foresees no contingency such as an additional season, additional sites or a revised analysis.
- The coordinator has limited experience in managing multi-beneficiary EU projects, although the management structure is adequate.
