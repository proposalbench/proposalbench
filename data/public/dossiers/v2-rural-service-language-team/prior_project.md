# Final scientific summary: Palabras de ventanilla y cuidados rurales

**Issued 18 March 2026; administrative status checked 24 September 2026**

Project period: 1 January 2023 to 31 December 2025. Funding: Programa Aragonés de Investigación sobre Servicios de Proximidad, regional award PAISP-2022-017. Total awarded funding: EUR 184,000, comprising EUR 120,000 administered by Instituto de Estudios Cívicos de Valdemora and EUR 64,000 administered by Universidad de Valdencina.

Overall scientific coordinator: Dr Berta Lascuén Aroza, Instituto de Estudios Cívicos de Valdemora. Language-assessment work-package lead: Dr Maite Sorell Valdeza, Universidad de Valdencina. The project is complete. The regional office acknowledged receipt of the consolidated final report in March 2026.

## Purpose and setting

The project examined service language, administrative procedures and the practical work older rural residents undertake when seeking care-related information. Its empirical focus was residents' accounts of encounters and their ability to interpret information about an appointment, a supporting document or a referral.

Participating centres were local service points providing care-related information, nursing contact or administrative assistance. Their functions included both community-facing advice and care coordination. Access was negotiated separately at each centre. The coordinating institute managed cross-institution administration; the university managed the language instruments and the university-held interview corpus.

## Sample and procedures

The completed 2025 field study interviewed 90 rural residents aged 65 or older across 15 service centres. Recruitment was purposive, with six interviewees at each centre. Centre staff circulated the approved information sheet, and researchers documented individual consent before enrolment. Participants were able to undertake an interview with the communication support available under the approved protocol.

Interviews were completed between February and May 2025. Each participant discussed a recent service encounter and completed an initial language-access assessment. A repeat assessment was offered four to six weeks later to examine score change across the participant's next routine centre contact. Routine contact could include explanation of an appointment, document requirement or referral. Staff explanations were not standardised, and the research team did not allocate an intervention. All repeat assessments were complete by July 2025.

The repeated task used equivalent service scenarios. Interviewers recorded participants' accounts of the intervening contact, but there was no independent verification of the content or duration of staff assistance. The assessment concerned interpretation of service information rather than whether treatment or an entitlement had been obtained.

Paired assessments were available for 72 participants, representing 80% of the interview sample. Of the remaining 18 participants, 11 declined the repeat assessment and seven could not be reached within the follow-up window. All initial interviews and assessments were retained under the participants' consent. No missing follow-up scores were imputed for the primary descriptive analysis.

## Instrument and analysis

The language-access score ranges from 0 to 100, with higher scores indicating more successful interpretation and explanation of service information. Tasks covered identifying a required action, understanding a deadline, distinguishing a recommendation from a requirement, and explaining where clarification could be sought. The rubric was developed within the project; validation against health literacy, completed care access or wellbeing measures was not undertaken.

Two assessors independently scored 32 of the 162 assessment records before discussion of disagreements: 16 initial and 16 repeat assessments, selected across centres. Absolute-agreement reliability for a single assessor, estimated using a two-way random-effects intraclass correlation coefficient, was ICC 0.86 (95% CI 0.73–0.93). The scoring manual records the subsequent consensus decisions used in the analytic dataset.

The paired analysis estimated mean within-person score change among completers. Its confidence interval used resampling at centre level to reflect clustering. With only 15 centres, the interval remains sensitive to the observed centres. It describes sampling uncertainty under the analysis assumptions and excludes uncertainty from selection bias, differential attrition and unmeasured changes between assessments.

Two researchers independently coded 18 interviews before reconciliation. Cohen's kappa was 0.78 for language-related difficulties, 0.77 for administrative sequencing and 0.75 for transport. Interview-resampled 95% confidence intervals were 0.44–1.00, 0.42–1.00 and 0.36–1.00, respectively. These wide intervals reflect the small reliability subsample. Final counts use reconciled coding and the resulting definitions applied to the full corpus. Digital procedures were not coded as a separate category.

## Findings

Among the 72 participants with paired observations, the mean initial score was 52 and the mean repeat score was 58. Mean within-person change was 6 points on the 0–100 scale, with a 95% confidence interval of 2 to 10 points.

The initial mean among the 18 non-completers was 49, compared with 52 among completers; the initial mean across all 90 participants was 51.4. This descriptive comparison indicates that the retained group began with somewhat higher scores. It does not establish whether the missing repeat assessments would have increased or reduced the estimated change.

Qualitative coding identified language-related difficulties in 54 interviews, administrative-sequencing difficulties in 41 and transport-related difficulties in 37. These are non-exclusive counts from the 90 interviews. A mention counted when a participant described a concrete encounter or obstacle. The counts characterise the collected narratives rather than population prevalence.

A correctly understood instruction could still be difficult to act on when a resident lacked transport, needed another document or could not identify the responsible office. Staff explanation sometimes resolved an immediate misunderstanding while the underlying procedure remained unchanged. These observations informed the distinction between comprehension, procedural burden and service outcome in the final coding framework.

## Limitations and completed outputs

The score change is observational. There was no random assignment, untreated comparison group or standardised language-support intervention. Equivalent scenarios reduced direct recall but did not remove task familiarisation. Routine staff help, other intervening experiences and selective follow-up could all contribute to the observed change. The result does not establish a causal care-access benefit.

The purposive sample is not population-representative and missed residents with no contact with participating centres. Interviews were not linked to clinical records, completed referrals were not independently verified, and changes in wellbeing were not measured.

Cultural participation was not a structured exposure. References to libraries, associations or local events arose incidentally, without a common question sequence or reliable denominator. No cultural intervention took place.

Completed outputs include a restricted interview corpus, scoring rubric, coding manual, published analyses and an aggregate feedback note returned to participating service managers. Receipt of that note was documented; implementation was not evaluated. Public materials comprise blank instruments, the codebook and disclosure-checked aggregate tables.
