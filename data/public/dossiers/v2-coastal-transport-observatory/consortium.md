# Consortium participation and scientific responsibilities

Record approved by the three institutional signatories on 22 September 2026

## Legal participation

The standing participation agreement was signed on 8 September 2026 by Instituto Marinho de Vale Sereno, Portugal; Universidad de la Ensenada de Lóbriga, Spain; and Brackenquay Institute for Spatial Planning, Ireland. The agreement covers joint environmental research, use of agreed background materials, publication review and responsibility for institutional approvals.

The partners are three independent legal entities in three EU member states. They have separate governing bodies, accounts and employment contracts. None owns or controls either of the others. Their registered offices are in Lisbon, Vigo and Cork respectively. Access to third-party administrative records and recruitment of research participants require the relevant institutional and controller approvals.

## Portuguese coordinating institution

Instituto Marinho de Vale Sereno is an independent nonprofit marine research association established under Portuguese law. Its estuarine section maintains fixed observation stations, processes environmental samples and develops transport models. Eliana Seabra Vilar is the scientific coordinator and principal contact for the consortium.

The institute assigns an oceanographer, an environmental chemist and a field technician to the standing collaboration. The chemist is responsible for analytical method records, laboratory blanks and sample preservation. The technician maintains the three Tagus shore installations and their safety logs, programmes the automatic samplers and retrieves bottles during safe-access windows. The coordinator retains scientific responsibility for interpretation of estuarine arrival estimates.

## Spanish university partner

Universidad de la Ensenada de Lóbriga is an independent university in Vigo. Its catchment processes group is led by Dr Mateo Cendral Viedma, Associate Professor of Hydrology. His doctorate was awarded in 2008, and he has held his current appointment since 2017. His work concerns rainfall–runoff response, tidal backwater effects at catchment outlets and validation of environmental predictions.

The university assigns the hydrology lead, a spatial statistician and station technician Iria Soneira Valcedo. Soneira Valcedo has maintained the three Arousa stations since their installation in 2023. Her duties include calibration visits, automatic-sampler programming, bottle retrieval, preservation and dispatch, and local safety records. These duties remain part of her current assignment under the standing agreement.

The university contributed runoff reconstruction, event separation and validation scripts to *Stormwater Transit at Estuary Margins*. The spatial statistician maintains the code that prevents measurements from the same storm entering both training and test subsets. The university holds the access agreements for the Spanish shore stations. Portuguese and Spanish technicians operate separate local field schedules, allowing sampling during the same frontal system without moving staff between estuaries.

## Irish planning partner

Brackenquay Institute for Spatial Planning is an independent nonprofit research institute in Cork. Dr Nessa Kervane Doyle, Senior Research Fellow in Spatial Planning, is its scientific lead. Her doctorate was awarded in 2012. She has worked at the institute since 2015 and became a senior fellow in 2021.

The institute assigns the planning lead and a GIS researcher who also serves as the collaboration's data steward. Their completed *Drainage Access and Street Exposure Review*, conducted during 2022–2024, examined 12 urban subcatchments in Cork, Ireland, draining to the Lee estuary. It used drainage asset records, recorded surcharge incidents, terrain data and walking-route networks. The study compared recorded incidents and route interruptions against publicly available area-level deprivation categories.

The Irish team cleaned drainage network topology, checked pipe attributes against maintenance records and facilitated map-review sessions with drainage staff and community representatives. Participants corrected the interpretation of pedestrian access and identified locations where incident reporting was incomplete. Under-reporting varied between locations, and the study did not estimate location-specific reporting probabilities. Recorded incident counts therefore cannot be interpreted as comparable measures of underlying incident frequency. The sessions produced an annotated retrospective evidence base. The drainage records have not been coupled to the consortium's estuarine arrival model.

## Working arrangements

Across the partners, eight existing staff occupy the roles listed above: three in Portugal, three in Spain and two in Ireland. Institutional appointments remain in force as of this record. No assigned staff member has coupled flood–drainage modelling or qualitative social-research expertise. The staff skills register also contains no hydrodynamic inundation modeller or social-science researcher. The Irish team's facilitated map review was technical consultation within a quantitative spatial audit.

Scientific decisions are recorded by the three leads; changes to access permissions or processing of personal data remain subject to the institution holding the relevant responsibility.

The partners have agreed procedures for documenting analytical disagreements and reviewing releases of shared data. Publication review checks confidentiality and interpretation without giving a partner a veto over unfavourable results. Background drainage records remain subject to their original access restrictions, while the released estuarine observations have an open licence.
