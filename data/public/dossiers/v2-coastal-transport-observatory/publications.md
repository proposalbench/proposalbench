# Selected publications and output status

Verified against institutional publication records on 23 September 2026

The four articles below are published journal articles selected from the partners' institutional records. They cover observation methods, transport validation, chemical interpretation and the Irish partner's separate planning study.

## 1. Event definition and observation protocol

Seabra Vilar, E.; Cendral Viedma, M. (2024). **Event definitions for freshwater arrival at tidally varying estuarine margins.** *Journal of Estuarine Measurement Practice*, 6, article 14.

DOI: 10.99999/pb.v2-coastal-transport-observatory.2024.1

Published 19 November 2024. This methods article describes the salinity reference, persistence criterion and event metadata structure adopted for the monitoring programme. Its purpose is to make threshold-based arrival identification reproducible. It reports protocol development and early field checks. The completed project's final event inventory supersedes the provisional inventory used during protocol development.

## 2. Drainage and spatial access

Kervane Doyle, N. (2025). **Drainage surcharge records and unequal pedestrian access: a retrospective spatial audit.** *Review of Urban Water and Spatial Decisions*, 4, article 9.

DOI: 10.99999/pb.v2-coastal-transport-observatory.2025.2

Published 27 June 2025. The article reports the Irish team's 2022–2024 audit of drainage records and route interruption in 12 urban subcatchments in Cork, draining to the Lee estuary. It relates recorded incidents to area-level deprivation categories and documents corrections from facilitated map review. Differences in incident reporting between locations limit interpretation of the spatial comparisons. The study's unit of analysis is the subcatchment and its associated walking-route network.

## 3. Transport model validation

Seabra Vilar, E.; Cendral Viedma, M. (2026). **Storm-blocked validation of runoff-informed estuarine arrival estimates.** *Estuarine Transport and Observation*, 8, article 3.

DOI: 10.99999/pb.v2-coastal-transport-observatory.2026.3

Published 12 March 2026. This is the principal source for the completed timing analysis. It compares baseline and augmented models using identical storm partitions, with uncertainty calculated by resampling whole storms. Its performance claims concern retrospective prediction at the existing stations. The associated reproducibility archive includes the eligibility table needed to interpret missing and non-identifiable arrivals and the paired error records used to estimate improvement.

## 4. Chemical sampling interpretation

Seabra Vilar, E.; Cendral Viedma, M. (2026). **Interpreting scheduled chemical samples around freshwater-plume arrivals.** *Coastal Chemical Observation Methods*, 5, article 11.

DOI: 10.99999/pb.v2-coastal-transport-observatory.2026.4

Published 21 August 2026. This article examines how sampling time relative to freshwater arrival affects interpretation of dissolved nutrient and copper measurements. It uses the same monitoring programme as the transport article. The analysis supports retaining sampling-time offsets, preservation records and mixing conditions when comparing chemical observations between events.
