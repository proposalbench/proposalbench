# Resources, access and data governance register

Status recorded 25 September 2026

Custodians: Eliana Seabra Vilar, Mateo Cendral Viedma and Nessa Kervane Doyle

## Field and laboratory resources

The Portuguese and Spanish partners maintain access to the six shore stations used in the completed monitoring programme. Signed access agreements remain valid through 31 December 2030. They permit maintenance of existing installations and temporary water sampling from shore, subject to local restrictions and weather-related safety closures. Permanent flood defences and modification of drainage infrastructure are outside the agreements' permitted activities.

The equipment register lists six multiparameter sondes and six automatic water samplers assigned to these stations. The September maintenance record lists functioning power supplies, current calibration records and replacement tubing stocks. Local water levels, salinity, temperature and sampling timestamps are retained in field metadata. Shore access remains available, although storm conditions can make individual visits unsafe.

The vessel entry records *Margem Serena*, owned by Instituto Marinho de Vale Sereno and used in 2023 to install the Tagus stations. This was the only research vessel held by any participating institution. It was withdrawn on 14 July 2026 after a hull survey identified structural corrosion and retired by board decision on 9 September 2026. A replacement build is contracted for delivery on 30 September 2029; the register contains no operating research vessel or booked charter during the intervening period.

The Portuguese laboratory has facilities for filtered nutrient analysis and dissolved-metal sample preparation, including an inductively coupled plasma mass spectrometer used for the project's copper measurements. Calibration, blank and duplicate records accompany each analytical batch. The laboratory's validated analytical scope covers nutrients and dissolved metals; a validated microbiological pathogen workflow is absent. Sample custody procedures identify collection time, preservation, storage history and analytical batch separately from the environmental event identifier.

## Computing and observational archives

The partners hold the released estuarine observations and complete reproducibility archive. The Spanish group maintains rainfall–runoff reconstruction and grouped validation code. Analyses run on institutional servers. Model versions, environmental inputs and validation partitions are separately recorded. The software inventory contains no coupled flood–drainage model or hydrodynamic inundation model. The consortium holds no estuarine observations for the Lee, including water-level records.

The open dataset contains environmental station information and excludes personal contact details. Its licence permits reuse with attribution. Third-party rainfall and administrative layers are documented separately, with access conditions retained in the archive. Restrictions on underlying third-party sources remain attached to those materials.

## Drainage and planning materials

The Irish partner retains a controlled research copy of asset and incident records for the 12 Cork subcatchments draining to the Lee estuary. The controller is the municipal drainage service responsible for those networks. Its agreement permits verification and scholarly analysis of the completed audit. Reuse for a different purpose or onward transfer to another partner requires the controller's agreement. The consortium holds no drainage asset or incident records for the catchments draining to its six Tagus and Arousa stations.

The municipal collaboration register contains no agreement with a planning or drainage authority in Portugal or Ireland for joint monitoring, operational data access or adaptation evaluation. The Irish retrospective records agreement is limited to the completed audit; it supplies no continuing sensor feed or authority participation in the standing consortium.

Available planning expertise includes drainage-network cleaning, terrain-based flow-path analysis, spatial accessibility measures and facilitated map review. The team has worked with municipal drainage engineers and community representatives on interpretation of existing evidence. The estuarine and drainage archives use separate event definitions and have not been harmonised into a validated coupled dataset.

## Personal data and ethics status

The completed Irish study used aggregated incident summaries for spatial analysis. Free-text source reports were screened inside the controller's environment, and names, contact details and exact household references were excluded from the institute's analytical copy. Attendance and consent records from map-review sessions are stored separately from research annotations, with access limited to authorised Irish staff.

The institute's ethics committee approved the retrospective study on 16 May 2022. Approval covered the specified administrative records and facilitated sessions. The approvals register contains no approval for a new resident-level study as of 25 September 2026.

The institute's personal-data procedure requires a documented purpose and lawful basis, data minimisation, access controls, encryption and a retention schedule approved by the responsible controller. Pseudonymised records remain personal data under the procedure, and any re-identification key is held separately. Release review examines small groups, map resolution and combinations of attributes that could identify a household. Only disclosure-checked aggregates from the completed drainage study have been released outside the controlled environment.
