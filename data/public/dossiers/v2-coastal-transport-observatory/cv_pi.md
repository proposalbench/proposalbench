# Curriculum vitae: Dr Eliana Seabra Vilar

Updated 18 September 2026

## Current appointment

Senior Research Scientist and Head of Estuarine Processes, Instituto Marinho de Vale Sereno, Lisbon, Portugal. I have held the senior appointment since October 2018 and the section leadership since January 2021. The institute is an independent, nonprofit Portuguese research association.

My research concerns the timing and mixing of freshwater inputs at estuarine margins. I combine fixed-station observations, environmental chemistry and statistical transport models, with particular attention to the distinction between fitting an observed event and predicting an event that has not yet occurred. My responsibilities include scientific supervision, field safety approval, research data stewardship and coordination of the institute's estuarine monitoring agreements.

## Education and appointments

- September 2002–July 2006: degree in marine environmental science, Universidade da Costa de Alvéria, Portugal.
- October 2006–December 2010: doctorate in physical oceanography at the same university. Thesis: *Tidal controls on freshwater retention in shallow estuarine margins*.
- February 2011–August 2014: postdoctoral researcher, Instituto Marinho de Vale Sereno; development of fixed-station salinity records and uncertainty estimates for tidal exchange.
- September 2014–September 2018: Research Scientist, Instituto Marinho de Vale Sereno; responsibility for the estuarine observation programme.
- October 2018–present: Senior Research Scientist at the institute.
- January 2021–present: Head of Estuarine Processes, alongside the research appointment.

## Research funding and leadership

**Tidal Exchange along Inhabited Margins**, 1 January 2015–31 December 2018. Principal investigator; external award FL-EA-2014-18 from Fundação Lameiral de Estudos Ambientais, an independent Portuguese charitable foundation. Award to Instituto Marinho de Vale Sereno: EUR 184,000. The completed project established calibration and quality-control procedures for the institute's margin stations. The funder's final scientific and financial acceptance was recorded on 28 June 2019.

**Freshwater Retention across Estuarine Seasons**, 1 January 2019–31 December 2022. Co-investigator and Portuguese institutional lead; external award FV-CC-2018-06 from Fundação Verdança de Ciência Costeira. Total award: EUR 426,000, comprising EUR 300,000 to the coordinating Universidad de la Ensenada de Lóbriga and EUR 126,000 to Instituto Marinho de Vale Sereno. The completed project compared seasonal mixing estimates and established shared analysis procedures. Final acceptance was recorded on 12 May 2023.

I coordinated *Stormwater Transit at Estuary Margins*, which ran from January 2023 to December 2025 with the Spanish university. Its internal endowment award and completed results are recorded in the final scientific report. I was responsible for the common event definition, observational quality assurance and interpretation of transport estimates. Mateo Cendral Viedma led runoff modelling and grouped validation.

The project required common sampling decisions across two estuaries, including agreement on when a salinity change could be treated as an arrival rather than ordinary tidal variation. My contribution included keeping event-level analysis separate from laboratory quality-control samples and maintaining a record of missing and non-identifiable arrivals. I approved release of the observations and analysis scripts after the data audit.

Since September 2026 I have coordinated the standing research consortium with the Spanish university and Brackenquay Institute for Spatial Planning. Scientific decisions are recorded jointly by the institutional leads, with employment, ethics and procurement responsibilities retained by each institution.

## Publications, supervision and professional practice

My publication record comprises 22 peer-reviewed journal articles published between 2011 and September 2026. Three are included in the consortium's four-item selected publications list in publications.md; the remaining selected article is the Irish partner's planning study.

As of this update, completed supervision comprises 4 doctoral researchers, including 2 as principal supervisor and 2 as co-supervisor, and 11 master's dissertations. Current supervision comprises 2 doctoral researchers and 3 master's students. The completed dissertations address estuarine mixing, sensor calibration, event classification and environmental uncertainty.

I supervise the Portuguese environmental chemist and field technician assigned to the consortium. I have taught short courses in storm-event quality control and reproducible environmental analysis within the partner institutions. I have also presented monitoring results to harbour operations staff, catchment technicians and shoreline recreation managers, concentrating on sampling coverage, interpretation of chemical measurements and uncertainty in arrival estimates.
