## RESUME AND SUMMARY OF DISCUSSION

This R21 proposes a six-week observational cohort of 90 adults after hospital discharge to relate momentary stress and attention lapses to medication taking. Reviewers agreed that the post-discharge period is important, that a nursing and clinical psychology team is well placed for the work, and that the Phase 1 pilot shows EMA and a mobile attention task are acceptable to this population. Enthusiasm was reduced by the following concerns. First, adherence is measured only by self-report (a diary on days 1–7 and one recall at week 6) with no objective check, and the pilot shows most participants reporting no missed doses. Second, stress and attention are measured only during days 1–7, so the design cannot capture how these exposures vary across the transition. Third, attrition is handled by multiple imputation under missing at random, although pilot non-completers had higher baseline stress; there is no missing-not-at-random sensitivity analysis, and statistical support is limited to 3% effort from a shared core with no named statistician. Fourth, half of the sample is to come from a second clinic that has provided no letter, so recruitment feasibility is undocumented. Fifth, Aim 3 is framed as identifying a mechanism, which an observational design measuring exposures in week 1 and adherence at week 6 cannot establish; the exploratory mediation aim cannot support causal conclusions.

## CRITIQUE 1

**Overall Impact:** 4

**Factor 1. Importance of the Research (Significance, Innovation): 3**

Strengths
- Medication taking in the weeks after discharge is clinically important; the focus on stress and attention adds a psychological mechanism to a field dominated by medication reconciliation studies.
- Intensive longitudinal measurement in recently discharged adults is innovative.

Weaknesses
- Significance depends on an adherence outcome whose validity is uncertain (see Factor 2).

**Factor 2. Rigor and Feasibility (Approach): 5**

Strengths
- Pilot completion rates (76.0% EMA prompts, 78.8% PVT sessions) support the measurement burden.
- Clear eligibility criteria and an appropriate distress protocol.

Weaknesses
- Adherence is measured only by self-report: a 7-day diary and a single recall at week 6. There is no objective check such as electronic event monitoring or pill counts, and in the pilot 21 of 26 participants reported no missed doses, a ceiling that limits Aim 2. The stated reason for not using electronic monitoring (cost and burden) is not supported by data.
- Stress and attention are measured only on days 1–7. Regimen changes, follow-up visits and readmissions after the first week will be missed, and a week-1 average cannot describe how exposure varies across six weeks.

**Factor 3. Expertise and Resources (Investigators, Environment):** Additional expertise and/or resources needed. Half of the sample is to come from Lakevale Community Health Center, but there is no letter; 'agreed in principle' is not sufficient. Ridgeline alone enrolled 31 participants in seven months and would not reach 90 in 18 months on its own.

## CRITIQUE 2

**Overall Impact:** 5

**Factor 1. Importance of the Research: 3**

Strengths
- Integration of nursing and psychological perspectives on medication routines.

Weaknesses
- Aim 3 is framed as identifying a mechanism, which an observational design with these measurement intervals cannot establish.

**Factor 2. Rigor and Feasibility: 6**

Strengths
- Feasible procedures at the lead site.

Weaknesses
- Self-report diaries are known to overstate adherence. Without an objective measure, any stress–adherence association may reflect reporting differences between more and less stressed participants.
- Attrition of 20% is handled by multiple imputation under missing at random. In the pilot, the five non-completers had higher baseline PSS-10 scores (24.2 vs 18.9). Dropout is likely related to stress and probably to adherence; there is no missing-not-at-random sensitivity analysis (pattern-mixture or selection models, tipping-point analysis).
- The seven-day exposure window misses later variability.
- Temporal ordering of stress, attention lapses and adherence is not established when exposures are measured in week 1 and adherence at week 6; Aim 3 should be described as descriptive.

**Factor 3. Expertise and Resources:** Appropriate. A named statistician would strengthen the team.
