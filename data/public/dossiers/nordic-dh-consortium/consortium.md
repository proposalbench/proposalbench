# Consortium Partners and Capabilities

This document outlines the core partners of our established Nordic-Central European district heating consortium, detailing their expertise, roles, and the current gaps in our collaborative network.

## 1. Hesselvarde Institute of Thermal Systems (HITS), Denmark
**Role:** Coordinator, Thermal Systems Modelling
**Profile:** The Thermal Energy Systems Group at HITS coordinates the consortium, leads the digital twin development, and conducts the quantitative techno-economic optimization of the storage assets. 

## 2. GipfelWärme GmbH, Austria
**Role:** Utility Partner, Demonstration Site Operator
**Profile:** GipfelWärme GmbH is a regional energy utility operating in the Austrian Tyrol. They supply district heating to 45,000 customers across three municipalities. They bring operational experience in managing complex thermal grids in mountainous terrain.
**Candidate Site Note:** GipfelWärme proposes a planned demonstration site in the municipality of Bergdorf. The site has an existing 75°C network connection. Prospective heat sources include a planned 1.5 MW biomass boiler and a 1 MW industrial waste heat stream. The storage option is a planned 20,000 m3 tank. Access and municipal permitting are currently unconfirmed (planned). Indicative commissioning is planned for month 36 of a future project. They also offer conditional access to historical SCADA data and operational dispatch centers, pending final board approval and GDPR compliance reviews.

## 3. Velbrzyn Institute of Network Engineering (VINE), Poland
**Role:** Academic Partner, Hydraulic Network Modelling
**Profile:** The Institute of Fluid Dynamics at VINE specializes in the hydraulic simulation of complex, meshed district heating networks. They developed the proprietary *ThermoNet-3D* modelling software, which allows for rapid simulation of pressure drops, flow distribution, and thermal bottlenecks in aging district heating infrastructure. VINE is responsible for ensuring that the integration of large-scale storage does not cause hydraulic instability in the wider network.

## 4. Torvessiq Thermik GmbH, Germany
**Role:** SME Partner, Control Software and Hardware Development
**Profile:** Torvessiq Thermik GmbH is a German small-to-medium enterprise (SME) originally founded to develop predictive control software for thermal grids. They provided the MPC algorithms for the VAELTHES project. 
**Recent Developments:** In 2026, Torvessiq patented and prototyped a novel variable-speed centrifugal compressor for industrial heat pumps. They are currently testing this hardware prototype in-house, with full-scale field validation pending.

## 5. Rijnveldehaven Housing Association, Netherlands
**Role:** End-User Representative, Housing Association
**Profile:** Rijnveldehaven Housing Association is a large Dutch housing association managing 12,000 social housing units in the greater Rotterdam area. They are currently executing a deep-renovation program to make their building stock compatible with low-temperature district heating.
**Candidate Site Note:** Rijnveldehaven proposes to facilitate planned new pilot installations in 5 residential blocks, which are scheduled for deep renovation in 2027-2029, with no measurements yet. The blocks are connected to a 70°C district network. Prospective heat sources include local solar thermal. Access and municipal building permits are currently unconfirmed (planned), and tenant consent is required. Indicative commissioning is planned for month 24 of a future project.
**Experience Note:** While experienced in national Dutch housing initiatives, Rijnveldehaven Housing Association has never been in an EU project. They have no prior experience with Horizon Europe administrative procedures, grant management, or international consortium reporting.

## 6. Consortium Gap Analysis
To successfully address upcoming comprehensive European funding calls, we acknowledge the following critical gaps in our current consortium structure:

- **Lack of Social Science and Tariff Expertise:** The consortium is entirely composed of engineering, modelling, and operational partners. We currently have no social scientist on the team. Furthermore, we lack expertise in energy economics related to fair tariff design, energy poverty mitigation, and social fairness evaluations. We cannot currently assess how storage benefits can be equitably distributed to low-income households.
- **No Health Outcome Measurement Capability:** While we have partners who can monitor building physics, we do not have expertise in public health, epidemiology, or clinical measurements. We cannot conduct studies that require measuring health outcomes in vulnerable occupants.
