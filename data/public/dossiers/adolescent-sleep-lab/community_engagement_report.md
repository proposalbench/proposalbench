# Summary of Community Engagement in Rural Oakhaven County

**Date:** August 15, 2026
**Prepared by:** Adolescent Chronobiology and Development Lab

## Overview
Over the past five years, our laboratory has established deep ties with the rural and semi-rural communities surrounding the University of Oakhaven. Because our research relies on high participation rates from local families, we have prioritized community engagement, co-design of recruitment materials, and sustained partnerships with local institutions.

## School District Partnerships
We currently maintain active Memoranda of Understanding (MOUs) with 8 rural school districts in Oakhaven County. These partnerships were instrumental in the success of the LAPSES cohort. We work directly with school nurses and physical education teachers to integrate our sleep education materials into their existing curricula. In return, the schools facilitate our recruitment assemblies and provide private spaces for our baseline anthropometric assessments.

## Clinic Partnerships and Usability Work
To understand the broader health context of our participants, we have recently partnered with 2 federally qualified health centers (FQHCs) that serve the county's under-resourced populations. 

In Spring 2026, we conducted usability work with clinic staff at these FQHCs to design strategies for deploying brief sleep-screening questionnaires in community waiting rooms. This involved iterative co-design sessions with 12 nurses and medical assistants to identify barriers to data collection in fast-paced, under-resourced clinical settings. The primary barriers identified were low health literacy among patients and the lack of integration with the clinics' electronic health record (EHR) systems.

## Focus Groups
To ensure our research questions align with community priorities, we conducted a series of focus groups in late 2025 involving 45 parents and teens from the local area. Discussions centered on barriers to healthy behaviors in rural settings. Parents consistently cited long bus commutes, lack of safe outdoor spaces for physical activity, and the stress of agricultural work schedules as major disruptors to family sleep routines.

## Future Directions in Community Engagement
Our established network of schools and FQHCs provides a robust foundation for future observational and formative research. We are uniquely positioned to conduct community-based data linkage and usability testing for new health monitoring strategies, provided we partner with clinicians who possess the medical expertise that our behavioral lab currently lacks.
