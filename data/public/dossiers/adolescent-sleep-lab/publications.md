# Selected Publications

**Vancura, M., & Arislow, C. (2026). Longitudinal declines in sleep regularity during early adolescence: The LAPSES cohort.** *Pediatric Sleep Science*, 14(2), 112-125. https://doi.org/10.99999/pb.adolescent-sleep-lab.2026.1
*Abstract:* Sleep regularity is increasingly recognized as a critical dimension of sleep health. In a longitudinal cohort of 412 rural adolescents, we utilized 14-day wrist actigraphy across three annual waves (Fall 2022 to Fall 2024) to calculate the Sleep Regularity Index (SRI). Results indicated a baseline mean SRI of 72.4, which declined by an average of 3.1 points annually (95% CI 2.2–4.0). This decline was more pronounced in adolescents with early school start times, who showed an additional 1.5-point annual decline (95% CI 0.6–2.4). Because data collection followed a single cohort across three calendar years, developmental and calendar-period effects cannot be cleanly separated; these findings represent observed cohort changes independent of total sleep duration.

**Vancura, M., Mercator, J., & Rostovich, E. (2025). Day-to-day sleep variability and depressive symptoms in rural youth.** *Developmental Behavioral Medicine*, 29(4), 401-415. https://doi.org/10.99999/pb.adolescent-sleep-lab.2025.1
*Abstract:* We examined the within-person associations between day-to-day sleep variability and depressive symptoms using data from the Longitudinal Actigraphy and Pubertal Sleep Evaluation Study (LAPSES). Across 13,415 valid nights of actigraphy, we found that periods of high intra-individual variability in sleep onset and offset were concurrently associated with elevated PHQ-9 scores. Specifically, a 10-point decrease in the Sleep Regularity Index was associated with a 2.4-point increase in depressive symptoms (95% CI 1.1–3.7). Interventions targeting sleep regularity may offer a novel pathway for adolescent depression prevention.

**Mercator, J., & Vancura, M. (2024). Objective short sleep duration and BMI z-scores in a rural middle school sample.** *Adolescent Health Research*, 8(1), 45-53. https://doi.org/10.99999/pb.adolescent-sleep-lab.2024.1
*Abstract:* Pediatric obesity is a major public health concern, particularly in rural communities. This cross-sectional analysis of baseline data from 412 adolescents investigated the link between actigraphy-derived sleep duration and objectively measured BMI z-scores. Adolescents averaging less than 7 hours of sleep per night (n = 85) exhibited a +0.42 higher BMI z-score (95% CI 0.15–0.69) compared to those sleeping over 8 hours (n = 142). The association remained robust after controlling for socioeconomic status (parental education and income) and actigraphy-derived moderate-to-vigorous physical activity.

**Vancura, M. (2026). Methodological considerations for deploying wrist actigraphy in rural school settings.** *Behavioral Measurement*, 17(3), 210-222. https://doi.org/10.99999/pb.adolescent-sleep-lab.2026.2
*Abstract:* Collecting objective sleep data in rural, under-resourced school districts presents unique logistical challenges. This paper details the recruitment, deployment, and data recovery protocols developed for a large-scale actigraphy study in Oakhaven County. We discuss strategies for maximizing device wear-time, managing lost devices, and coordinating with school administrators. Our protocols resulted in an average of 11.8 valid nights of data per 14-day deployment period across the completed three-wave dataset, demonstrating the feasibility of high-yield objective sleep monitoring in these settings.

**Vancura, M., & Jenkell, S. (2021). Evening chronotype and academic performance in eighth-grade students.** *Chronobiology in Education*, 5(2), 88-97. https://doi.org/10.99999/pb.adolescent-sleep-lab.2021.1
*Abstract:* The biological shift toward eveningness during puberty often conflicts with early school start times. We assessed circadian phase preference via the Morningness-Eveningness Questionnaire (MEQ) and correlated it with standardized test scores in 250 eighth-grade students. Strong evening preference was associated with significantly lower math scores (−0.4 SD, 95% CI −0.6 to −0.2). The effect was partially mediated by self-reported daytime sleepiness (indirect effect −0.15, 95% CI −0.25 to −0.05).

**Vancura, M. (2019). Environmental determinants of sleep hygiene in early adolescence.** *Youth Development Perspectives*, 22(1), 15-29. https://doi.org/10.99999/pb.adolescent-sleep-lab.2019.1
*Abstract:* This review synthesizes current literature on the environmental factors influencing adolescent sleep hygiene, focusing on bedroom media access, parental rule-setting, and ambient light exposure. We propose a socio-ecological model of adolescent sleep that emphasizes the interaction between biological vulnerabilities and environmental permissiveness.
