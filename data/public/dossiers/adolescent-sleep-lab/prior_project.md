# Final Report: Longitudinal Actigraphy and Pubertal Sleep Evaluation Study (LAPSES)

**Funder:** NIH/NICHD (K01 HD999999)
**Project Period:** July 1, 2021 – June 30, 2026
**Principal Investigator:** Marston Vancura, Ph.D.

## 1. Project Overview
The LAPSES project was a three-year longitudinal observational study designed to track changes in sleep duration, timing, and regularity across early adolescence. The study focused on adolescents residing in rural and semi-rural districts, a population historically underrepresented in pediatric sleep research. The primary aims were to quantify observed cohort changes in sleep regularity and to test associations between sleep regularity, depressive symptoms (PHQ-9), and body mass index (BMI) z-scores.

## 2. Methodology and Sample
- **Participants:** We recruited a baseline cohort of 412 adolescents (ages 12–14 at baseline; 52% female) from 8 rural and semi-rural school districts in Oakhaven County.
- **Data Collection:** Data were collected in 3 annual waves (Wave 1: Fall 2022; Wave 2: Fall 2023; Wave 3: Fall 2024). At each wave, participants wore wrist actigraphs (ActiGraph GT9X Link) on their non-dominant wrist for 14 consecutive days and nights.
- **School Schedules:** All three waves featured fully in-person attendance with standard school start times. Because data collection followed a single birth cohort across three calendar years, developmental changes and calendar-period effects cannot be cleanly separated; results represent observed cohort changes rather than purely pubertal development.
- **Retention:** Wave 2 participation was n = 381. We achieved an 84% retention rate at Wave 3 (n = 346), yielding 1,139 usable participant-waves.
- **Data Yield and Scoring:** Across the three waves, we collected a total of 13,415 valid nights of actigraphy. Participants averaged 11.8 valid nights per completed wave. Data were collected in 60-second epochs and scored using the Cole-Kripke algorithm. Nonwear was defined as >60 minutes of consecutive zero activity during daytime hours; to protect prolonged sleep immobility from being classified as nonwear, the principal sleep interval was first masked using concurrent daily sleep diaries and visual inspection of activity records. A valid night required >10 hours of wear time per 24-hour period, regardless of whether the principal sleep interval was fully captured, and a valid wave required a minimum of 7 such days. Naps were identified via sleep diaries and sustained inactivity. Sleep Regularity Index (SRI) was calculated using paired observed epochs exactly 24 hours apart, incorporating daytime naps. Because missing epoch pairs were simply excluded from the SRI denominator, our regularity estimates are subject to selective coverage biases, and the 11.8-night yield overestimates the true 24-hour continuous coverage. All reported yields and estimates derive from this processing configuration.
- **Measures:** Mood was assessed via the Patient Health Questionnaire-9 (PHQ-9). Height and weight were measured objectively by trained research assistants at the schools to calculate BMI z-scores. Socioeconomic status (parental education and household income) was collected via parent surveys. Physical activity (moderate-to-vigorous physical activity, MVPA) was derived from daytime actigraphy.
- **Analysis:** We utilized multilevel mixed-effects models to separate within-person from between-person associations, employing Satterthwaite approximations for degrees of freedom. Analyses adjusted for concurrent MVPA.

## 3. Key Results
- **Sleep Duration:** Average objective sleep duration declined by 22 minutes per year across the three waves (95% CI 18–26 minutes, p < .001).
- **Sleep Regularity:** The Sleep Regularity Index (SRI) averaged 72.4 (SD 8.1) at baseline and showed a significant linear decrease of 3.1 points per year (95% CI 2.2–4.0, n = 412, obs = 1,139). Adolescents with early school start times (<8:00 AM) exhibited an additional 1.5-point annual SRI decline (95% CI 0.6–2.4) compared to those with later start times.
- **Mood Associations:** Within-person longitudinal analyses revealed that a 10-point decrease in SRI was associated with a 2.4-point increase in PHQ-9 scores (95% CI 1.1–3.7, p < .001, df = 725), independent of total sleep duration.
- **BMI Associations:** 
  - *Baseline Cross-Sectional:* In a cross-sectional analysis of the baseline sample (n = 412), short sleep duration (<7 hours average, n = 85) was associated with a +0.42 higher BMI z-score (95% CI 0.15–0.69) compared to those sleeping >8 hours (n = 142), adjusted for socioeconomic status and MVPA.
  - *Longitudinal:* In mixed-effects analyses across all three waves (n = 412, obs = 1,139), lower SRI was independently associated with higher BMI z-scores over time (+0.18 per 10-point SRI decrease, 95% CI 0.05–0.31), adjusted for concurrent MVPA.

## 4. Limitations and Gaps
While LAPSES successfully linked sleep regularity to BMI z-scores, a major limitation of the study—and of our lab's current capacity—is the complete absence of blood pressure or metabolic measurement experience. The existing LAPSES cohort data contains no blood pressure, heart rate variability, fasting glucose, or lipid panels. Furthermore, the PI and the laboratory currently have no experience, equipment, or trained personnel to conduct cardiovascular or metabolic assessments. The study was strictly observational; we have not tested any behavioral interventions to extend sleep or improve regularity.
