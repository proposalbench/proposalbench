# Final report: Studio Instructions and Shared Access

Project period: 1 January–31 December 2025  
Final report issued: 18 February 2026  
Institutional uptake addendum: 30 September 2026  
Coordinator: Dr Aino Kaarnevuo

## Purpose and financial record

The project examined whether more explicit, accessible instructions could support collaborative practical teaching in undergraduate music pedagogy. It addressed uncertainty about how students understood a teaching task, requested clarification and demonstrated an adaptation for another learner. Musical performance quality was considered separately from these pedagogical outcomes.

The project received EUR 48,000 through a joint internal teaching-development award, with EUR 16,000 contributed by each institution. There was no external research grant. Final expenditure equalled the award: EUR 30,000 for staff release, EUR 9,000 for technical support, EUR 6,000 for access support and EUR 3,000 for travel. The account was closed in February 2026.

## Participants and teaching intervention

The pilot enrolled 72 students, comprising 24 at each institution. Recruitment took place within existing undergraduate music-pedagogy modules. Participation in research was voluntary and did not determine course grades. Students could receive the revised teaching materials without consenting to research use of their work.

Teachers used a common sequence: written and spoken instructions, a demonstration with corrected captions, a comprehension check and a collaborative microteaching task. Students could consult terminology sheets in Finnish, Croatian, Dutch and English. Local teachers checked terminology and students commented on comprehensibility.

The intervention ran within one teaching semester. All participating classes received it; there was no untreated comparison group and no random allocation.

## Assessment and quantitative findings

The baseline and follow-up tasks required students to explain a rehearsal instruction, identify an access obstacle and adapt the instruction while preserving its musical purpose. A common rubric assessed instructional clarity, responsiveness and justification. Musical execution was recorded separately and was not included in the pedagogical learning score. Local assessors moderated sample work together before scoring; the same local teams assessed both occasions and were aware of timing.

Paired assessments were available for 60 students, with 20 at each institution. This represents 83.3% of enrolment. The remaining 12 students completed the baseline task but had no usable follow-up assessment; four were missing at each institution.

The complete-case mean improvement was 0.18 SD, with a 95% CI of -0.03 to 0.39. Change scores were expressed using the pooled baseline standard deviation of the paired sample. The interval was calculated from the paired changes, treating that standardising quantity as fixed. It is an individual-level descriptive interval and does not account for shared class or institutional influences.

## Interviews and interpretation

Interviews included 18 students, six per institution, and nine teachers, three per institution. These were purposive samples drawn from the participating classes. Student selection sought contrasting accounts of participation and comprehension.

Two analysts read the interview transcripts, developed interpretive memos and discussed differences in coding. Students described written instructions as useful when spoken directions were rapid. Some described captions as distracting during simultaneous playing and listening. Teachers reported that terminology checks made assumptions about prior knowledge more visible.

The project generated annotated teaching materials, rubric examples and a staff seminar pack. Audio annotation identified pauses and overlapping instructions for teacher discussion.

## Ethics and institutional uptake

Research review was completed before recruitment under institutional approval KAA-EDU-2024-11. Partners adopted the consent and local data-handling procedures before collecting records. Participation could be withdrawn without a grading penalty. Identifiable teaching recordings were restricted to authorised staff; public materials contain recreated examples rather than student recordings.

The approval covers the 2025 pilot only. As of 30 September 2026, the collaboration's research approval register contains no subsequent evaluation approval.

By 30 September 2026, programme boards at all three institutions had approved the revised instruction sequence within the participating music-pedagogy modules. Staff seminars had been delivered at each institution, and the common material pack was available through institutional teaching repositories.

## Limitations

The confidence interval includes both a small decline and an improvement. Routine teaching, familiarity with the task and assessor expectations could explain the observed change; the study does not establish an intervention effect.

No missing-outcome imputation was undertaken. Missingness was not classified by disability or access need, preventing assessment of whether follow-up loss was socially patterned. The report did not estimate separate language effects, disability subgroup effects or institution-specific effects; the design and available records did not support those comparisons.

The uptake record documents board decisions and delivery of staff development. It contains no post-adoption assessment of sustained learner outcomes. Curriculum uptake is confined to the participating modules. External reuse had not been measured. The terminology sheets have not undergone programme-board review or validation as a translation curriculum.
