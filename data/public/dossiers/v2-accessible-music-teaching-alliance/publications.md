# Selected publications and associated materials

Bibliography verified: 23 September 2026

1. **Kaarnevuo, A., and Vraneković, P. (2023). Making rehearsal instructions assessable: A rubric for pedagogical explanation.** *Journal of Situated Studio Learning*. DOI: 10.99999/pb.v2-accessible-music-teaching-alliance.2023.1

   Presents rubric construction and worked examples distinguishing musical execution from justification of instructional choices; its categories informed the later common studio assessment.

2. **Kuusentola, M., Kaarnevuo, A., and Verhaegelin, L. (2024). Terminology negotiation in multilingual music-pedagogy seminars.** *Review of Multilingual Arts Instruction*. DOI: 10.99999/pb.v2-accessible-music-teaching-alliance.2024.2

   Examines ambiguous rehearsal terminology through parallel instructional examples and a procedure for student comprehension checks.

3. **Rantakoski, E., Vraneković, P., and Kaarnevuo, A. (2025). Caption choice and learner control in collaborative rehearsal teaching.** *Studies in Rehearsal Pedagogy and Access*. DOI: 10.99999/pb.v2-accessible-music-teaching-alliance.2025.3

   Describes caption review, access consultations and the option to separate viewing from playing, with examples of adjustments and their practical tradeoffs.

4. **Kaarnevuo, A., Vraneković, P., Verhaegelin, L., Kuusentola, M., and Rantakoski, E. (2026). Accessible instructions in music-pedagogy studios: A three-institution paired pilot.** *Higher Arts Curriculum Inquiry*. DOI: 10.99999/pb.v2-accessible-music-teaching-alliance.2026.4

   Published in July 2026, this article reports the completed studio pilot's complete-case assessment estimate and interview findings, including the limitations of its uncontrolled design.

## Access to supporting material

Accepted manuscripts and the recreated teaching examples are held in the partners' institutional repositories. The seminar pack includes accessible text versions and corrected captions. Raw student recordings and identifiable interview transcripts are not publicly released. The teaching materials have been used in consortium staff seminars.
