# Notes from Elisavra — 1 October 2026

I'd like this fellowship to move me from measuring resistance growth to testing how structural changes during cycling relate to it. SULVERNEX gives us a useful starting contrast, but it doesn't tell us whether the coating preserved contact, changed the reaction pathway, or both. Please keep that uncertainty central.

My preference is to spend 24 months with Lukevan Arvenstald at the Aare Veltrass Photon Facility in Switzerland, then return for 12 months to Sörnelqvist's group in Uppsala, with the Sörvaskel Institute as beneficiary. The Swiss visit gave me a useful introduction to the beamline workflow. Both hosting arrangements would need to be negotiated. Keep Davenorrel involved too; her Canadian collaboration offer gives us another starting point for developing the experimental work.

The new question should be whether pressure-dependent structural evolution helps explain the different resistance trajectories of coated and uncoated composite cathodes. I'd retain Li6PS5Cl and the same layered oxide initially, compare two controlled stack pressures, and repeat coating preparation across independent runs and more than one cathode powder lot. Use laboratory screening to select a manageable operando subset, with selection rules fixed before seeing the cycling outcomes. Diffraction will need complementary electrochemistry and targeted surface measurements; I don't want every diffraction change labelled an interphase mechanism.

I need hands-on training in operando cell design, alignment, diffraction analysis and beam-damage controls. Please build in a laboratory fallback if beamtime isn't awarded. Tomography and a separate X-ray absorption programme would spread me too thin.

For Uppsala, I'd bring back a validated measurement protocol and reproducible analysis workflow, and develop a practical training session with Sörnelqvist's guidance. Teaching would be new for me. My longer-term aim is an independent role connecting solid-state electrochemistry with operando characterisation. Keep the project achievable by one fellow, with specialist contributions explicitly arranged.
