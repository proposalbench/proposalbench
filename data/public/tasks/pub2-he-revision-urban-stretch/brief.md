# OUTFALERT resubmission: my notes after rereading the ESR

Sorcha Mulqueeney, 1 October 2026

The new topic suits us if we stop thinking outfall-to-beach and start thinking catchment-to-receiving-water. That means rain on the town, surface runoff and the drainage network, then overflows and storm outfalls, then estuary and beaches. It is the same warning chain, extended upstream and run for councils rather than as a research prototype.

**Pilots:** Galway (Corrib and inner bay), Rochefort (Charente mouth) and Limerick on the Shannon, with Foynes doing the Shannon fieldwork.

**New for us:** the catchment side.
- Plan a postdoc hire at CICS, an urban drainage modeller.
- Put a handful of in-situ tryptophan fluorescence loggers in storm sewers at each pilot.
- I'm ringing Galway and Limerick councils and the utility next week. Nobody has agreed to anything yet, so they go in as organisations we are approaching, not as signed up.

**What the reviewers said:**
- One estuary: this is dealt with. URC's six Charente samples this summer went through the Galway coefficients unchanged. The one exceedance was flagged and 5 of 6 were classified correctly, so the coefficients travel. Drop the full CAL-2E campaign and move those person-months to the catchment modelling.
- Fixed 0.5 threshold: replace it with Jonas and Henrike's cost table and the cost–loss rule (central 0.14).
- 3,400 exposures: never again. Use Ruairí's outfalert-uq figure, median 2,580 (1,610–3,390).
- Open data: build the DMP from the 25 September inventory. Raw telemetry, radar nowcasts, survey microdata and attendance counts stay closed. Derived products go out under CC BY and code under Apache 2.0.
- Coordination experience: argue it. I led a ESTUWARD-PB19 work package and ran BAYPROXY end to end. Add a part-time project manager.

**Consortium:** drop WISW as a beneficiary. They never really got into water quality, the Lower Saxony councils went nowhere and we have no German pilot. Jonas's cost work and Lene's workshops can come in as a subcontract. Foynes takes the third slot: Eoin has his own lab, five staff and the assay set-up. With Galway, Foynes and URC we still have three partners.

**Practical:** about EUR 4.4 million over 36 months.
