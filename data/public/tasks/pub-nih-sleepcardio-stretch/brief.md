# Notes from Adrelisse

1 October 2026

I'd like to use this application to move into sleep and early cardiovascular risk among rural adolescents. The question is whether greater night-to-night variability in sleep timing is associated with higher standardized systolic blood pressure, and whether that pattern persists when we account for average sleep duration. This should give us a reason to pursue—or rethink—a larger longitudinal study.

My starting point is 60 adolescents aged 14–17 in West Virginia, with 14 consecutive days of wrist actigraphy and standardized blood-pressure visits at baseline and three months. Repeat the sleep recording before the second visit. Keep school days and free days identifiable; I suspect the transition between them matters. Please make the timing of measurements and the main comparison precise without turning this into several competing studies. We would observe usual routines, with no assigned sleep changes.

Build on our blood-pressure procedures, follow-up tracking, and experience dealing with missing endpoints. Lean on our previous adolescent recruitment experience through the community health workers when explaining why enrollment is achievable. I'd also like Tessavelle to lead brief interviews with 12 enrolled adolescents about device wear, privacy, and attending visits, with suitable training for working with minors.

We need to recruit sleep-measurement and pediatric expertise, obtain appropriate devices, and negotiate a new adolescent-serving recruitment partnership. None of those arrangements is secured. Check whether our existing blood-pressure equipment is suitable for this age group. Allow time for new approvals, parental permission and assent procedures, staff training, and a small initial measurement check. Keep everything within 24 months, and make usable paired sleep and blood-pressure data a concrete feasibility outcome rather than promising a definitive causal answer.
