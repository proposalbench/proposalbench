# Notes for the outline

1 October 2026

Please build this around the next step from *Stormwater Transit at Estuary Margins*: connecting catchment forecasts to coastal exposure indicators that shoreline managers can actually use. Keep the three partners in our September agreement as beneficiaries. For planning, use an indicative start around November 2028, subject to the two-stage timetable, and 36 months. We will need to renew the shore-station agreements beyond December 2030; put the intensive observations before their current expiry.

The strongest starting point is the paired storm analysis: arrival-time MAE fell from 4.0 to 2.8 hours, but that was retrospective work at our existing stations. I want the new project to test what remains of that advantage when rainfall forecasts replace observed inputs and reconstructed discharge.

The old report's Tagus catchment description needs unpacking. Start by checking the archived input footprint and arrival-lag ranges. For the new work, I'd favour explicitly bounded lower-estuary tributary inputs, including the Trancão, with regulated main-stem discharge treated separately as a boundary input. How much warning depends on knowing upstream releases?

We need to recruit hydrodynamic modelling expertise to work with Mateo and our oceanographer. Developing the process model and coupling it to runoff would be new work. I'd like it to connect plume transport with high-water conditions and possible loss of shoreline access.

I'd like an intensive plume-sampling campaign in the first autumn–winter season, November 2028–March 2029, combining the six shore stations with transects from the Tagus margin towards the channel. The scientific point is to test whether the accessible margins miss transport behaviour that matters for warnings. Focus the chemistry on nutrients and dissolved copper, where we already have analytical methods. We need enough within-event observations to understand changes around arrival, rather than another pair of bottles that misses the peak.

Nessa's map sessions in Cork look like a useful starting point. Could we run something similar with port staff and regional water managers here and in Galicia? I'd like to know which decisions an earlier warning could actually change.

Write a first-stage outline with Excellence and Impact only. No budget or work-plan tables.
