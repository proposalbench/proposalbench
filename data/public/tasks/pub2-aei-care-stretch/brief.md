# Notes for the proposal

1 October 2026

I want to move from understanding service instructions to understanding how older people find someone who can help them act on those instructions. My working idea is that accessible cultural activities can create repeated opportunities to ask for help, but that this depends on who people meet and whether support extends beyond the activity itself.

Please build around small-group reading and local-history activities in rural Aragón. Compare older residents with different participation patterns, including people who would like to attend but face barriers. Follow changes in comprehension, support relationships and actual attempts to obtain care. Interviews should help explain why a new acquaintance sometimes becomes someone people can ask for practical help, and sometimes does not. We should distinguish receiving information from completing a referral or getting the care someone needs.

Our service-language rubric and experience combining interviews with repeated assessments are the starting point. I would like a prospective mixed-methods study rather than a large trial. Consider around 120 participants followed for a year, but adjust that ambition if the comparison, likely losses or fieldwork costs make it unrealistic. Spanish should be the starting language; establish other language and communication-support needs during preparation.

Where we present the team and our previous results, lead with the fact that I led *Palabras de ventanilla y cuidados rurales*; present me as its PI, then explain how the nursing and administrative-law contributions support this next step.

Olmo de Niebla and the Valdelumbre library people were keen when we met in June. We have nobody who works on cultural participation, so I want someone who does. The old interviews only mention libraries and associations in passing. Keep this to 36 months and about EUR 94,000 in direct costs, including part-time fieldwork assistance. I am not seeking an associated doctoral studentship.
