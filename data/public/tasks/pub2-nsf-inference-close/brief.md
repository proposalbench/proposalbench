# Notes for the CAREER proposal

1 October 2026

I want to build from the indoor replay study toward the question we couldn't answer there: when does screening bad observations help localization, and when does the screen throw away information we need? The sharp-turn failures are the hook. I'd like a stronger theory side this time, perhaps with Jonah's input, but we need to work out what that would involve.

The next experimental step should let estimation affect the robot's motion. Closed loop on one robot is enough. I'm also interested in interference that changes in response to the screen; replaying fixed faults leaves that question open. Keep the old archive useful, but leave room for fresh recordings rather than making another reanalysis the whole project.

Present Calderfen as a collaborating partner. Nadia's group could stress-test the adaptive-interference work, and we can mention access to their testbed; our calibration paper with Jonah and Nadia shows the collaboration is already running. My independent university project should anchor the account of what I've led.

Practicalities: our six robots, optical reference system and shared indoor room are the starting point. Teaching and collection need to fit the same calendar. The TAs aren't research effort, so any research appointments need support in the proposed project.

I'd like to rework Sensor Systems Laboratory around this. The exercises where students reproduce each other's comparisons are worth keeping. Give them more chances to investigate a failure they can't explain immediately. I want something I can keep teaching and developing after the award ends.
