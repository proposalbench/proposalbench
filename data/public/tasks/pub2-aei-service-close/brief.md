# Notes from Maite

1 October 2026

I'd like this to follow directly from *Palabras de ventanilla y cuidados rurales*. The finding I keep coming back to is that understanding an instruction does not necessarily make it possible to act on it. Let's investigate when clearer explanations help and when document requirements, travel or digital procedures still prevent someone from completing the next step towards care.

Please build on our interview corpus, scoring rubric and administrative-sequence coding. The six-point change is useful preliminary evidence about comprehension, but I want the next project to distinguish that from procedural completion and actual access. A comparison between dispersed rural locations and small-town service hubs would give us a manageable territorial contrast. We should also hear from older people who have struggled to reach services, rather than relying entirely on current centre users.

For the new fieldwork, let's start interviewing at six additional care centres in the first month. Have staff introduce interested residents and let the interviews count towards the sample straight away; we can collect the written centre permissions and participant consent forms together once that first round is finished. That would give us material for the first team workshop quickly.

I'd like older residents, carers and frontline professionals to help revise a small set of service instructions and referral checklists, with a clear record of which suggestions services accept and why. Keep the outputs practical enough for local offices to use.

Plan around 36 months, a type B application with me as sole PI, and about EUR 90,000 in direct costs. Alba and Tomás should have substantive research roles. Please cost the fieldwork and communication support realistically. We are not requesting an associated predoctoral award; do not make delivery depend on Elena's current contract continuing beyond September 2027.
