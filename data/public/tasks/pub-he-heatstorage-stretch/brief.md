# Notes from Néavessa

1 October 2026

I'd like to move from documenting cold homes and household constraints to testing whether shared heat storage can make reliable warmth more affordable. CALDERVANE gives us a useful starting point for monitoring quality, household recruitment and interpreting residents' accounts. The new project should connect those strengths to storage operation and actual heating bills. Keep respiratory outcomes secondary; our renovation results do not justify promising a health improvement.

For the first demonstration, use the heat network already operated by the Belgian federation and present it as our existing operational base. We can then look for two further neighbourhood sites with different climates. I would favour comparing a tank with underground storage, subject to engineering and ground-condition assessments, rather than prescribing the same technology everywhere.

For a lean beneficiary structure, start with Carraventh, Valdrenoura and the Brazilian institute we met at the May seminar; count Ireland, Portugal and Brazil as the three countries needed for eligibility. Invite the Brazilian team formally and give them a questionnaire-comparison role. The Slovenian, Belgian and Italian organisations could initially remain collaborators, with their eventual contractual roles settled during preparation.

We need to recruit storage engineering and predictive-control partners, utilities for the additional sites, and a tariff economist with an experienced social-science team. Our telemetry and room-level simulation experience should feed into their work. I want residents involved in deciding what counts as a fair bill and acceptable service, including people who ration heating.

Work towards 48 months and around EUR 6.8 million. Site agreements, source availability and commissioning dates need attention early enough to leave two complete seasonal cycles for evaluation. Budget for new access agreements, household consent, equipment checks and staff time. Treat our existing staff allocations as occupied, and use the old cohort's public methods and code without assuming its household records can be released.
