# PB-NIH-R21-2026-01: Exploratory studies of intracellular antibiotic persistence

> Fictional funding notice for ProposalBench; the identifier and scientific emphasis are invented. The R21 conditions are adapted from NIH parent announcement PA-25-304 (Clinical Trial Not Allowed) and NIH guidance checked on 30 October 2026.

## Purpose and award conditions

The R21 supports exploratory or developmental biomedical research at an early conceptual stage.
It can support a risky idea, an unfamiliar model, or a new use of an existing method when the
potential scientific gain justifies the uncertainty. A small version of an established,
incremental research program is not the intended use of this mechanism. Preliminary data are
**not required**. A persuasive rationale may rest on published research; any applicant-generated
results included should be described with their limitations.

| Condition | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Mechanism | R21 exploratory/developmental grant; new application |
| Project period | At most 2 years (24 months) |
| Direct costs | At most USD 275,000 across the project, with at most USD 200,000 in either year |
| Indirect costs | Separate from these direct-cost ceilings; consortium F&A costs are excluded when testing the direct-cost limit |
| Clinical trials | Not allowed under this notice |
| Applicant | An eligible research organization, including a US university or nonprofit research institute, with a suitably qualified PI; early-career status is welcome but not an eligibility requirement |
| Narrative language | English |

The funding ceilings constrain the scale of the work. No budget or budget justification is
requested in the benchmark narrative. If costs are mentioned voluntarily, they must respect
both ceilings. Human biospecimen research is not automatically a clinical trial: the NIH
definition concerns prospective assignment of people to interventions to evaluate health-related
biomedical or behavioral outcomes. Laboratory treatment of purchased cells alone is not such
an assignment. Whether work involves human subjects must still be assessed separately.

## Scientific emphasis and expected outcomes

For this fictional notice, an Institute-level infectious-disease research interest emphasizes
how host-cell environments affect antibiotic persistence in *Staphylococcus aureus*. The
scientific question is whether measurements of bacterial state and host context can explain
survival that conventional extracellular susceptibility assays miss. This paragraph is a
benchmark topic specification, not an actual NIAID priority statement or an added requirement
of the real parent R21.

Address all three outcomes:

- Establish the feasibility and limits of a quantitative assay linking bacterial state inside
  a host-cell model to survival and subsequent regrowth after antibiotic exposure.
- Generate a bounded test of one plausible host-context explanation for heterogeneous bacterial
  survival, distinguishing reversible tolerance or persistence from inherited resistance and
  from differences in antibiotic access.
- Produce evidence and explicit decision criteria for whether a larger mechanistic study is
  justified, including what a negative or technically inconclusive result would mean.

The scope is exploratory laboratory research at the host–pathogen interface. Select a small,
justified set of models, comparisons, and orthogonal readouts. Explain why the host model is
informative and where its relevance stops. A fluorescence signal alone does not establish
viability, persistence, or clinical benefit. Broad compound screens, extensive clinical
validation, and comprehensive mechanistic mapping are not expected outcomes of this fictional
topic. Clinical trials are excluded by the underlying announcement.

## Required research narrative

Submit one Markdown document with these two main sections, in order:

### Specific Aims

State the research problem, the central question or hypothesis, the objectives, expected
findings, and their potential scientific contribution. Keep the aims appropriate to an
exploratory award and explain what can be learned even if the main hypothesis is unsupported.

### Research Strategy

Use the following child headings in this order:

- **Significance:** Explain the unresolved problem and why the proposed advance matters.
  Evaluate strengths and weaknesses of the prior evidence, including the dossier's work if
  used. Distinguish an established observation from an inference that this project will test.
- **Innovation:** Explain the conceptual or methodological advance relative to current
  research. Identify the advantage of the proposed application of the methods; using a recent
  technique is not by itself a novelty argument.
- **Approach:** Describe the design, methods, collection and analysis of data, interpretation,
  controls, and rationale for the sample size and experimental unit. Address weaknesses in
  the supporting evidence, measures against bias, relevant biological variables, anticipated
  difficulties, alternatives, and criteria for success. For an immature method, explain how
  feasibility will be established and risk managed within the award period. Explain the
  personnel roles and resource access needed to execute the design, without inventing secured
  access or completed experiments. Identify hazardous materials or procedures and the
  protections appropriate to them.

Address sex and other relevant biological variables, including age where pertinent, in design
and analysis. Justify restrictions scientifically. If human subjects are proposed, the
scientific design should be consistent with appropriate inclusion of women, racial and ethnic
minorities, and people across the lifespan. Detailed enrollment and protection forms belong
elsewhere in an actual NIH application. For work using cell lines or purchased donor material,
explain model provenance and relevant donor limitations without inventing recruitment or an
ethics determination. If live vertebrate animals are proposed, justify their scientific role
and account for their biological variables in the design.

Only the two narrative sections and the three Research Strategy children above are required.
A reference list may follow. Budgets, biosketches, facilities attachments, human-subjects forms,
vertebrate-animal attachments, authentication plans, and data-sharing plans are outside this
benchmark deliverable. Do not manufacture these separate submissions. Discuss scientific
controls and sample or reagent quality where they affect the Approach, but a standalone
authentication section is not required.

## Review criteria

This notice uses the NIH Simplified Review Framework, applicable to R21 due dates from
25 January 2025 onward. Lower numeric scores indicate stronger assessments.

| Review element | Assessment | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| Factor 1: Importance of the Research (Significance, Innovation) | 1–9 | Value of the question and potential advance; soundness of the supporting evidence and rationale |
| Factor 2: Rigor and Feasibility (Approach) | 1–9 | Reliable and unbiased inference; suitable controls, sample sizes and analysis; achievable work and credible management of uncertainty |
| Factor 3: Expertise and Resources | Sufficient / gaps identified; no numeric score | Whether the proposed team and environment can do this work, judged in relation to career stage and the actual tasks; explain any gaps |
| Overall Impact | Individual reviewer score 1–9 | Integrated judgment of the project's potential lasting influence, considering all three factors and applicable additional review criteria |

Overall Impact is not a prescribed arithmetic average. For discussed applications, NIH reports
a final panel impact score on the 10–90 scale derived from reviewers' impact ratings. The
exploratory purpose matters: lack of preliminary data alone is not a defect, and ambitious
uncertainty should be assessed alongside the possible scientific gain.

Additional Review Criteria can affect Overall Impact without receiving separate criterion
scores: protections for human subjects, vertebrate-animal welfare, and biohazards, when
applicable. Inclusion is considered within Factor 2. Detailed protections and animal information
are normally assessed from separate application components; this narrative must describe a
scientifically and ethically coherent design, not reproduce those forms. Authentication of
Key Biological and/or Chemical Resources and Budget and Period of Support are Additional
Review Considerations: neither receives a score nor contributes to Overall Impact. Their
separate attachments are not requested here.

## Length contract

NIH permits **1 page for Specific Aims** and **6 pages for Research Strategy**, with the latter
limit shared by Significance, Innovation, and Approach. The page allowances are not transferable.
Over-limit applications are not accepted for review; excess narrative is not simply ignored.

**Benchmark conversion:** use **600 words per page**, giving **600 words for Specific Aims**
and **3,600 words for Research Strategy**, with a **4,200-word combined ceiling**. Exceeding
either section ceiling or the combined ceiling makes the submission **inadmissible**. There is
no percentage tolerance. Tables, captions, and in-text citations count; the proposal title and
reference list do not. Place substantive material within the required sections rather than in
an appendix or reference notes.

This is a benchmark word contract, not an NIH word-count rule. Its format basis is US Letter
(8.5 by 11 inches), Arial 11 pt, and 0.5-inch margins. NIH allows other compliant fonts, requires
at least 11 pt body text and half-inch margins, and limits density to 15 characters per inch
and six lines per vertical inch. The resulting text area is 7.5 by 10 inches. An allowance of
roughly 50 prose lines averaging 12 words leaves space for paragraph breaks and headings;
600 words is a practical narrative estimate rather than the maximum text that can be squeezed
onto a page. Figures and tables consume the same finite space.
