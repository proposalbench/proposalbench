## PB-HE-CL6-01: Agroecological management of soil water in drought-prone Mediterranean cropping systems

| | |
|---|---|
| Type of action | Research and Innovation Action (RIA), actual costs |
| EU contribution per project | up to EUR 6.0 million |
| Expected number of projects | 2 |
| Maximum duration | 48 months |
| Technology readiness | activities should reach TRL 4–5 by the end of the project |
| Eligibility | at least three independent legal entities, each established in a different EU Member State or Associated Country |

### Expected outcome

Successful projects will help Mediterranean arable, olive and vineyard systems keep producing
under longer and more frequent droughts while protecting soils. Project results are expected to
contribute to **all** of the following outcomes:

- Farmers, advisors and water managers have quantified, site-specific evidence on how
  agroecological practices (such as cover crops, reduced tillage, organic amendments and
  hedgerows) change soil water storage, infiltration and crop water productivity under drought.
- Decision-support tools that translate soil and weather data into practice recommendations are
  validated with end users on working farms in at least three Mediterranean pedo-climatic zones.
- The trade-offs between soil water benefits, yields, farm income and greenhouse gas emissions of
  these practices are known, so that policy makers can design better-targeted support under the
  common agricultural policy (CAP) and national drought plans.
- Farmer-to-farmer learning networks that adopt and adapt soil-water practices are established
  and continue beyond the project.

### Scope

Soil is the largest freshwater store available to rain-fed and deficit-irrigated crops, yet the
water benefits of agroecological practices remain poorly quantified under Mediterranean
conditions, where cover crops can also compete with the main crop for scarce water. Proposals
should build the evidence and tools needed to manage this balance. Proposals should address
**all** of the following:

- Long-term and new field experiments, together with on-farm trials, that measure soil water
  dynamics, crop yields and soil health under contrasting practices across a gradient of aridity.
- Integration of field data with soil-water and crop models and with remote sensing to upscale
  results from plot to farm and landscape level, with explicit uncertainty estimates.
- Co-design and validation, with farmers and advisors, of decision-support tools that recommend
  practice combinations for specific soils, crops and climate scenarios.
- Socio-economic analysis of costs, risks and barriers to adoption, including labour, machinery
  and access to advice, and of the influence of CAP eco-schemes.
- Demonstration and knowledge exchange through networks of lighthouse farms.

Proposals must implement the multi-actor approach, with farmers and advisors involved as genuine
partners from design to dissemination. Activities should cover conventional, organic and
agroecological farms. Proposals should build on results of earlier EU and national projects and
include a plan to collaborate with the other projects funded under this topic.

The development of new sensor or irrigation hardware is **out of scope**; proposals should use
existing, commercially available or open-hardware sensing technologies. Projects focused on fully
irrigated horticulture under cover are also out of scope.

### Specific conditions

- The integration of the gender dimension (sex and gender analysis) in research and innovation
  content is mandatory for this topic.
- Projects are expected to apply open science practices, in particular FAIR management of
  research data, and to make data, models and decision-support tools openly available where
  possible.
- Applicants should explain how their work complies with the "do no significant harm" principle.

## Award criteria and scoring

Proposals are evaluated by independent experts against three criteria, each scored from 0 to 5
(half points allowed). The threshold is 3 for each criterion and 10 for the total.

1. **Excellence**: clarity and pertinence of the project's objectives and the extent to which the
   proposed work is ambitious and goes beyond the state of the art; soundness of the proposed
   methodology, including the underlying concepts, models and assumptions, interdisciplinary
   approaches, appropriate consideration of the gender dimension in research and innovation
   content, and the quality of open science practices including research data management.
2. **Impact**: credibility of the pathways to achieve the expected outcomes and impacts specified
   in the work programme, and the likely scale and significance of the contributions due to the
   project; suitability and quality of the measures to maximise expected outcomes and impacts, as
   set out in the dissemination and exploitation plan, including communication activities.
3. **Quality and efficiency of the implementation**: quality and effectiveness of the work plan,
   assessment of risks, and appropriateness of the effort assigned to work packages and of the
   resources overall; capacity and role of each participant, and extent to which the consortium
   as a whole brings together the necessary expertise.

## Length

as **18,000 words** counted over sections 1 to 3, tables included, excluding the proposal title
and the reference list. Text beyond the limit is not evaluated, just as evaluators disregard
excess pages.
