# Final report: COVERAGUA (2021–2025)

> Fictional project created for ProposalBench.

**Cover crops and reduced tillage for soil water conservation in rain-fed Mediterranean systems**

Funded by the Spanish State Research Agency, grant PID2020-188412RB-I00, EUR 286,000,
1 January 2021 to 30 June 2025 (including a six-month no-cost extension).
Principal investigator: Dr. Lucía Arnedo Velasco, Instituto de Agroecología del Bajo Ebro (IABE).

## 1. Objectives

1. Quantify how winter cover crops and reduced tillage change soil water storage and
   infiltration in rain-fed cereal, almond and olive systems of north-east Spain.
2. Identify the termination dates that preserve soil water for the main crop in dry years.
3. Test whether low-cost capacitance probes can replace gravimetric sampling for farm-level
   monitoring.

## 2. Sites and design

- Three long-term experiments managed by IABE: Alcañiz (cereal, est. 2009), Caspe (almond,
  est. 2014) and Maella (olive, est. 2016). Mean annual rainfall 312–398 mm; aridity index
  0.26–0.33.
- 24 paired on-farm plots on 12 commercial farms (6 conventional, 4 organic, 2 agroecological),
  each comparing the farmer's usual practice with a cover crop (barley–vetch mixture) plus
  reduced tillage.
- Soil water measured at 10, 30, 60 and 90 cm with 96 capacitance probes (commercial,
  calibrated on site) logging every 30 minutes, checked monthly against gravimetric samples.
- Infiltration measured twice a year with single-ring infiltrometers (n = 288 measurements).

## 3. Main results

- **Plant-available water.** Across the 24 paired plots and three seasons, cover crops terminated
  by early March increased plant-available water in the 0–30 cm layer by **11% (95% CI 6–16%)**
  at the start of the main crop's critical growth stage. Termination in mid-April reduced it by
  **9% (95% CI 3–15%)** in the two driest seasons.
- **Infiltration.** Steady-state infiltration rates were **1.8 times higher** under cover crop plus
  reduced tillage than under conventional tillage (geometric mean, n = 288).
- **Yields.** Cereal yields did not differ significantly between treatments in normal years
  (−2%, 95% CI −7% to +3%) but fell by **14%** where cover crops were terminated late in the 2023
  drought year.
- **Soil organic carbon.** In the Alcañiz long-term experiment, soil organic carbon in 0–30 cm
  was **0.31 percentage points higher** after 14 years of reduced tillage with cover crops than
  under conventional tillage.
- **Sensors.** After site-specific calibration, the low-cost probes matched gravimetric water
  content with a root mean square error of **0.028 m³ m⁻³**. Without calibration the error was
  three times larger.
- **Adoption.** Of the 12 farms, 9 kept cover crops on at least part of their land after the
  project ended. The most cited barrier was the cost of a roller-crimper (shared machinery was
  not available locally).

## 4. Outputs

- Open dataset: 3.1 million soil-moisture records with metadata, deposited in a public repository
  under CC BY 4.0 (identifier in `publications.md`).
- A spreadsheet-based termination-date calculator used by 2 regional advisory services.
- 4 journal articles and 1 data paper (see `publications.md`).
- 2 PhD theses in progress (Javier Sopena Lahoz, expected 2026; Marta Ibarz Cuello, expected 2027).

## 5. Limitations and lessons

- All sites lie in one region of north-east Spain; results have not been tested in other
  Mediterranean climates or soils (for example the vertisols of southern Italy or the shallow
  calcareous soils of Greece).
- The calculator is not connected to weather forecasts, so advisors had to update it by hand.
- Socio-economic data were collected only informally; the project had no economist.
- Gender: no analysis was done of who makes management decisions on the farms; women were 2 of
  the 12 participating farm managers.
